## C — Interstate driving and passing (part two)

FACT_ID: sec10-F17 | anchor: A02

FACT_ID: sec10-F24 | anchor: A03
A driver may pass on the right on a multilane road, and on other roads may pass on the right of a vehicle or bicycle that is turning or preparing to turn left when there is room to go by safely [A03].

FACT_ID: sec10-F25 | anchor: A03

FACT_ID: sec10-F26 | anchor: A03
Do not pass on hills, on curves, at railroad crossings, at intersections or where a changed path ahead sign appears [A03].
Why it matters: Five no passing locations.
Source: [A03 -> src:rdmv-vt-class-std:vermont-department-of-motor-vehicles-age:handbook:pdf-0053]

Misconceptions:
- If you miss your exit, use the crossover to turn around: false, crossovers are for emergency and maintenance vehicles; continue to the next exit.
- You may stop on the interstate shoulder to check a map: false, stopping is legal only for an emergency, in the breakdown lane as far right as possible.
- Slowing to twenty under the limit is always enough for a stopped emergency vehicle: false, the first duty is to change lanes away when safe; the slowdown applies only when a lane change is impossible.
- You may pass on the right whenever there is room: false, only on a multilane road or beside a vehicle turning left, and never off the pavement.
- When being passed, hold your speed so the pass ends sooner: false, slow a little and keep right.

Mnemonics:
- Right, signal, speed, shoulder, change: the five entry steps from the acceleration lane.
- Hood up, white cloth, stay inside: the interstate emergency stop signal.
- Yellow on the right, you are wrong: the divided highway wrong way check.
- Hills, curves, rails, crossings, changed path: the five places never to pass.

Glossary:
- Acceleration lane: the lane beside the interstate where an entering vehicle speeds up to traffic speed before merging
- Deceleration lane: the lane nearest an exit where a leaving vehicle slows to the posted exit speed
- Breakdown lane: the right side lane reserved for emergency stops on an interstate
- Emergency turnaround: a median crossover on the interstate reserved for emergency and maintenance vehicles
- Controlled access road: a highway that can be entered and left only at ramps, allowing uninterrupted travel
