## A — Exam authority for the graduated licensing for drivers under eighteen section (Tennessee Class D knowledge test)
Issuer: the Tennessee Department of Safety and Homeland Security, Driver Services Division, publishes the Tennessee Comprehensive Driver License Manual and takes written comments on it [A01].
Edition statement: the manual says its information was current as of July 1, 2022, and later law, rule or policy changes override anything outdated in it [A01].
Freshness: that content date is old and no issuer page confirms it as the current edition, so every fact here is held under freshness debt until rechecked (verify TN-GDL-A-FRESH).
Knowledge test: a multiple-choice exam drawn from Sections B and C of the manual, split roughly equally across traffic signs and signals, safe driving principles, rules of the road, and drugs and alcohol [A02].
Format: given on computerized testing machines at Driver Service Centers, with Spanish, Korean and Japanese versions where computerized testing is offered, and oral tests by appointment for applicants who cannot read [A02].
Vision: a screening of at least 20/40 in each eye and in both eyes together, with or without corrective lenses, comes before any license or permit [A02].
Retake rule: after failing any knowledge test you must wait at least one day before trying again [A03].
Question count, passing score and time limit: the manual states none of them, so all three stay unknown and are never shown to learners (verify TN-GDL-A-EXAM).
This section feeds the knowledge test: the four Graduated Driver License steps with their ages, holding periods, practice hours, curfews and passenger limits, the points and violation penalties that delay or reverse progress, out-of-state teen transfers, the adult affidavit and school proof, and the teen cell phone and texting bans.
Practice quizzes follow the product mastery policy from the learning loop, which is not an official exam threshold.
## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: graduated licensing for drivers under eighteen
Audience: Tennessee applicants aged fifteen to seventeen starting on a learner permit, and the parents who sign for them and supervise practice.
Why it matters to a first-timer: the permit and intermediate rules [A01] and the curfew and passenger limits [A06] govern every drive a teen makes until graduation to a regular license.
Level: rule recall with many look-alike ages, hours and day counts; moderate load because the same points total triggers different penalties at different levels.
Prerequisites: the license-class and application chapters, which define the Class PD permit and the documents a minor must bring.
