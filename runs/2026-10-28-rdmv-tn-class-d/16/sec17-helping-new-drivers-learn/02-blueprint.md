Format: given on computerized testing machines at Driver Service Centers, with Spanish, Korean and Japanese versions where computerized testing is offered, and oral tests by appointment for applicants who cannot read [A02].

## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: helping teens and new drivers learn
Audience: first-time Tennessee Class D or learner permit applicants and the parents or mentors who supervise their practice driving.
Why it matters to a first-timer: the permit holding period [A01], the supervised hours requirement and practice phases [A02] and the English requirement for the road test [A03] shape how and when a new driver reaches the license.
Level: light recall with a few exact values such as the permit period, the required supervised hours and the teen crash rates; low to moderate load.
Prerequisites: the graduated license chapter, which sets out the permit, intermediate and full license stages this chapter refers to.

Misconceptions: the road test is not proof that safe habits will last; random practice drives are not harmless; correcting every mistake loudly does not speed learning; early success is a reason for continued practice, not for relaxing.
Mnemonics: lot, neighborhood, traffic, highway for the four practice phases; same words, said early; stop and discuss while it is fresh.
Glossary: graduated driver license program means Tennessee's staged licensing system for drivers under eighteen; teachable moment means a real driving situation used to explain a skill; driving log means a record of supervised practice; driving contract means a family agreement on when, where and how a teen may drive.
Dynamic facts: the permit holding period, the supervised hours requirement and the teen crash rates are set by Tennessee law or come from dated research; confirm them on official Tennessee sources before showing them to learners.
