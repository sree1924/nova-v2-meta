## A — Exam authority for the distracted driving and electronic communication devices section (Missouri Class F operator license written test)
Issuer: the Missouri Department of Revenue publishes the Missouri Driver Guide [A01].
Source document: Missouri Driver Guide, with the front cover reading Revised August 2026 [A02].
Written test: 25 multiple-choice questions, all drawn from information in the guide [A03].
Passing standard: 20 of the 25 questions must be answered correctly, which is 80 percent [A03].
The driver examination has four parts (written, vision, road sign and driving skills) and is given by the Missouri State Highway Patrol; the Class F written test is not open book and leaves out the commercial vehicle chapter [A04].
Time limit: the guide does not state one, so it stays unknown and is never shown to learners (verify MO-DIST-A-TIME).
This section feeds the written test: what counts as a distraction, why texting is the riskiest, managing children, friends and pets, setting in-vehicle controls before driving, roadside gawking, and what the electronic communication device law bans and allows while the vehicle is moving.

Misconceptions: hands-free calling is allowed but holding or supporting the phone while moving is not; voice-to-text is allowed but typing or reading texts by hand is not; a video call is banned even though a voice call is allowed; looking at a crash scene is itself a distraction, not harmless curiosity.
Mnemonics: eyes, hands and mind are the three kinds of attention texting steals; set it before you go, not as you drive.
Glossary: distraction means anything that takes attention away from driving safely; electronic communication device means a phone or similar device used to communicate; hands-free means operating a phone without holding it; voice-to-text means dictating a message instead of typing it; infraction means the violation level the guide says may apply once the warning period ended.
Dynamic facts: the device law is described as new in this guide, its warning period ended on a fixed date, and its fines and penalties are set by statute and can change.
