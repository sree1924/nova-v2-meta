## A — Exam authority for the traffic signs and signals section (Indiana learner's permit and driver's license knowledge exam)
Issuer: the Indiana Bureau of Motor Vehicles (BMV) publishes the Indiana Driver's Manual; both names appear on the front cover [A02].
Source document: the front cover prints no edition; the back cover reads Published July 2026 and points readers to IN.gov/BMV for updates [A04].
Knowledge exam: a multiple-choice, computer-based exam drawn from Indiana law, defensive driving practices and the manual, which also tests reading of regulatory, warning and traffic signs and signals [A01].
Exam structure: the exam has two components, one on common signs (Chapter 6) and one on traffic rules and laws (Chapters 6, 7 and 8) [A03].
Passing standard: 80 percent or higher correct on each component [A03].
Branch logistics: arrive at least 30 minutes before the branch closes to test that day; after a failure, wait until the next business day to retake [A01].
Question count: the manual gives no number of questions for either component or in total, so it stays unknown and is never shown to learners (verify IN-SIGNS-A-COUNT).
Time limit: the manual does not state one, so it stays unknown (verify IN-SIGNS-A-TIME).

## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: traffic signs and signals
Audience: every first-time Indiana permit applicant, since the manual says understanding traffic signs is necessary to obtain a license [A01].
Why it matters to a first-timer: the signs component of the knowledge exam draws on this chapter, and sign shape and color let a driver read a sign before its words are legible [A02]; signal rules decide who goes first at every intersection [A08].
Level: recognition and rule recall (color meanings, shape meanings, steady versus flashing signals) plus right-of-way reasoning at turns and dark signals.
Prerequisites: none beyond the permit basics; the safe-operation chapter builds on these rules for lanes, turns and passing.

Misconceptions: a flashing red is not a slow-down signal, it requires a full stop like a stop sign; a green light without an arrow does not give left-turners priority over oncoming traffic; a left on red is never allowed from a two-way street; a steady yellow is not a signal to speed up, it only lets you finish when you are too close to stop safely; white regulation signs are rules, not suggestions.
Mnemonics: eight sides means stop, three sides means yield, five sides means school, round means railroad, pennant means no passing; orange is for work zones, brown is for parks, blue is for services, green is for guidance.
Glossary: right of way means the legal priority to proceed first; pennant means a sideways triangular sign on the left side of a two-way road; advisory speed means a recommended speed for a curve, ramp or exit; pedestrian hybrid beacon means a mid-block crossing signal that stays dark until a pedestrian activates it; stop line means the solid white line where a vehicle stops.
