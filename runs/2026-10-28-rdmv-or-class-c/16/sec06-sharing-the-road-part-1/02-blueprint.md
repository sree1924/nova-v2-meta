## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: pedestrians, school zones, bicycles, motorcycles, large vehicles, buses and special vehicles

Misconceptions: an unmarked crosswalk is still a crosswalk at every intersection; a pedestrian on the far side of a safety island does not require a stop, but one in the lane beside yours does; a children-present school sign applies at any hour and on any day, not only during school hours; a green light does not end the duty to wait for a white-cane or guide-dog pedestrian; the bicycle fall-distance passing rule is tied to speeds above a set limit, not to every pass; sharing a lane with a motorcycle is illegal, not a courtesy.
Mnemonics: any part on the road means crossing; signal means wait for a clear six-foot gap, no signal means two lanes clear; if you cannot see the mirror, the trucker cannot see you; full lane for every motorcycle.
Glossary: vulnerable road user means a pedestrian, bicyclist, motorcyclist, highway worker or rolling-device user; unmarked crosswalk means the crossing formed by extending the corner sidewalk or shoulder; bike box means a green painted stop area for bicyclists ahead of cars at a signal; sharrow means a shared-lane marking of chevrons over a bicycle; blind spot means an area the driver cannot see even with mirrors.
Dynamic facts: school speed zones depend on time and presence, since the school-hours sign applies only during set hours on school days and the children-present sign applies only while children are present.
