FACT_ID: sec04-F01 | anchor: A01
Hawaii law requires the vehicle you drive on public highways to be registered in the county where you operate it, and to be licensed every year by that same county [A01].
Why it matters: registration is county-based, not state-based, which surprises many newcomers.

Misconceptions: the title is held by the lien holder while a loan is outstanding, not by the driver; the registration certificate, not the title, must stay in the vehicle; registration is renewed yearly with the county, not the state; a registration address change is a written notice while a registration name change requires a personal visit; the temporary permit for an out-of-state vehicle can never exceed twelve months even if the home registration runs longer.

Mnemonics: Register with the county, renew every twelfth month, report a change within thirty days; two plates on a car, one on a motorcycle, trailer or moped; a permit for an out-of-state car lasts at most a year.

Glossary: certificate of ownership (the Hawaii vehicle title); lien holder (the lender who holds the title until the loan is paid); staggered registration (expirations spread across the year, each at the end of a twelfth month); manufacturer's certificate of origin (the ownership document for a new vehicle before its first title); non-title state (a state that issues registrations but no titles); temporary registration permit (the Hawaii permit for a vehicle still registered elsewhere).

Dynamic facts: the online renewal web address can change and is not stated as stable knowledge; fee amounts are not printed on these pages; registration periods, document lists and display rules are stable statutory facts of this printing.
