## A — Exam authority for the rules of the road, part two section (Tennessee Class D knowledge test)
Issuer: the Tennessee Department of Safety and Homeland Security, Driver Services Division, publishes the Tennessee Comprehensive Driver License Manual and takes written comments on it [A01].
Edition statement: the manual says its information was current as of July 1, 2022, and later law, rule or policy changes override anything outdated in it [A01].
Freshness: that content date is old and no issuer page confirms it as the current edition, so every fact here is held under freshness debt until rechecked (verify TN-ROADTWO-A-FRESH).
Knowledge test: a multiple-choice exam drawn from Sections B and C of the manual, split roughly equally across traffic signs and signals, safe driving principles, rules of the road, and drugs and alcohol [A02].
Format: given on computerized testing machines at Driver Service Centers, with Spanish, Korean and Japanese versions where computerized testing is offered, and oral tests by appointment for applicants who cannot read [A02].
Vision: a screening of at least 20/40 in each eye and in both eyes together, with or without corrective lenses, comes before any license or permit [A02].
Retake rule: after failing any knowledge test you must wait at least one day before trying again [A03].
Question count, passing score and time limit: the manual states none of them, so all three stay unknown and are never shown to learners (verify TN-ROADTWO-A-EXAM).
This section feeds the knowledge test: rules of the road is one of the four roughly equal topic areas the manual names for the knowledge test, and this second half covers turn signals, left and right turns on two-way and one-way streets, turns on red, roundabouts, U-turns, lane use, crossovers, reserved and reversible lanes, passing and being passed, bicycles and trucks, backing, parking rules and no-parking zones; its sample questions target hydrant and railroad parking distances, the left-turn lane on a one-way street and the bicycle passing distance.
Practice quizzes follow the product mastery policy from the learning loop, which is not an official exam threshold.
## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: rules of the road, part two
Audience: every first-time Tennessee Class D or learner permit applicant; this completes the rules of the road topic on the knowledge test.
Why it matters to a first-timer: turn lane positions [A02], passing limits [A07] and parking distances [A10] are daily legal duties and appear directly in the chapter's sample questions.
Level: rule recall with many lane positions, distances and look-alike cases (one-way versus two-way turns, curb versus no-curb hill parking); moderate to high load.
Prerequisites: the first rules of the road chapter, which covers signals, right-of-way and stopping that these turning and passing rules build on.
