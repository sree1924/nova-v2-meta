## A — Exam authority for the Maryland MVA knowledge test

Issuer: the Maryland Department of Transportation Motor Vehicle Administration (MVA) sets the licensing requirements and gives the knowledge test [A07].
Publication: the manual's copyright page names the MDOT/MVA as the owner of the Maryland Driver's Manual [A02].
Edition: the copyright page prints the form line "DL-002 (10-26)" and the words "October 2026 Edition" [A02]; the front cover prints no edition, so this run's source liveness is unconfirmed and the current edition and effective date stay unconfirmed by the issuer (verify — sec06-A-EDITION).
License class: the manual's licensing chapter calls the noncommercial license for cars, pickup trucks and vans a Class C driver's license [A09].
Exam path: a first license requires passing a vision screening, a knowledge test and a driving skills test [A07].
Knowledge test: it covers motor vehicle laws, safe driving practices and traffic signs, is taken at an MVA location, and the manual says it holds all the information that is tested [A07].
Scoring: the manual's knowledge-test paragraph prints a question count and a time allowance, but while the source edition is unconfirmed neither figure is asserted here; the manual states no passing score or percent anywhere (verify — sec06-A-SCORING).
Question format: the manual does not describe the item format of the knowledge test (verify — sec06-A-FORMAT).
Practice: the MVA website hosts an Online Driver Test Tutorial with practice questions [A07]; practice mastery targets in this product are product policy from the accepted learning loop, never an official passing line.
Section focus: this section covers the meaning of white and yellow, solid, broken, double and dotted line markings, word and symbol markings, stop and yield lines, bicycle markings and bike lanes, and crosswalk lines. [A24]
Dynamic facts (updatable): MVA phone numbers, appointment booking, website addresses, office locations, accepted-document lists and fees can change between editions and are kept out of stable knowledge; why_dynamic: administrative details set by the MVA outside the printed rules of the road.
## B — Audience and scope

Audience: first-time Maryland applicants who have never held a driver's license and are preparing in English for the MVA knowledge test for a noncommercial Class C license; this includes teens entering the Rookie Driver graduated licensing path, adults licensing for the first time, and holders of out-of-country licenses who must still test.
Level: plain-English reading level; no prior driving vocabulary is assumed, so every term from the manual is defined where it first appears.
Prerequisites: the traffic sign and signal meanings from manual Section Four parts A and B, and the passing and lane rules from Section Three.
Section scope: manual Section Four, part C, on pavement markings: line marking types and their passing and lane-change rules, word and symbol markings, stop lines, yield lines, shared-use lane markings, bike lanes and crosswalk lines, plus the speed and seat belt safety campaign page that closes the chapter.
Out of scope: commercial and motorcycle licensing content, document-by-document identity paperwork beyond what the manual prints, and anything not printed on the selected manual pages.

Glossary: broken line — a dashed line you may cross when it is safe [A24]. Reversible lane — a lane whose direction of travel changes at different times [A24]. Stop line — the solid white line marking where to stop [A25]. Yield line — a row of small painted triangles marking where to yield [A25]. Shared-use lane marking — a pavement marking showing that bicycles and vehicles share the lane [A25].
Memory aid: yellow lines separate opposite directions, white lines separate the same direction; broken means you may cross, solid means stay put [A24].
Misconception: double solid yellow lines do not ban every crossing; you may still turn left across them into a driveway [A24].
