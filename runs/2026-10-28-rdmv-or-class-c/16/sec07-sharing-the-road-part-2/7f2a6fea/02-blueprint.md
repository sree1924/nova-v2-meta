## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: emergency vehicles, police stops, work zones, railroad crossings, streetcars and light rail
Audience: every first-time Oregon Class C applicant; emergency vehicle, work zone and railroad rules are everyday driving law and recurring knowledge-test material.
Why it matters to a first-timer: the emergency vehicle duty includes a following distance after it passes [A01], work zone speeds apply even when no workers are visible [A02], and the railroad stop rules set an exact stopping distance and list the triggers to stop [A05].
Level: condition-and-action recall with a few exact values (following distance behind an emergency vehicle, railroad stop distance, escape angle); moderate load.
Prerequisites: the sharing-the-road part one section, especially buses that stop at tracks, and the signs section, especially work zone and railroad signs.

Misconceptions: you do not pull right in the middle of an intersection for a siren, you clear it first; a work zone speed applies even when no workers are visible; a crossbuck is not a sign to just slow down, it carries a yield or stop sign you obey; a raised or rising gate is still not permission to go around; when stuck, you run toward the train at an angle, not straight away down the tracks.
Mnemonics: siren means right, stop, wait, then stay back; orange means slow and doubled fines; look, listen, slow at every crossbuck; get out, get away, report it at the ENS sign; streetcars go first.
Glossary: work zone means a road area marked by orange traffic control devices; automated flagger means a signal device with red and yellow lights and a stop arm; crossbuck means the X-shaped railroad crossing sign assembly; ENS sign means the Emergency Notification System sign at a crossing; light rail means an electric train running on tracks in the street.
Dynamic facts: work zones are temporary, so their posted speeds and layouts change with the project, and automated flagger and crossing signals change state as traffic and trains move.
