## A — Exam authority for the signals, pavement markings and lane controls section (Wisconsin Class D regular driver license knowledge test)
Issuer: the Wisconsin Department of Transportation and its Division of Motor Vehicles (DMV), which offers the Motorists Handbook as a resource that helps train and qualify new drivers [A03].
Source document: Wisconsin Motorists Handbook, with 2026 printed on the front cover [A01]; the back cover carries form number BDS126 with 2026 [A04].
Knowledge test: 50 questions on traffic laws, and 40 correct answers (80 percent) are needed to pass [A02].
Highway signs test: a separate test of 15 questions, with 12 correct answers (80 percent) needed to pass [A02].
Vision screening: regular car and motorcycle applicants need visual acuity of at least 20/40 in one eye, corrected or not, and a 70-degree field of vision from the center of each eye [A02].
Duration: the handbook says the knowledge test takes about 45 minutes to finish, but it states no formal time limit [A02].
Time limit: unknown, so no limit is ever shown to learners (verify WI-SIGNALS-A-TIME).
Delivery: the signs and knowledge tests need no appointment and are free at a DMV customer service center; applicants aged 15 to 17 can also sit the knowledge test online for a $10 fee [A02].
This section feeds the knowledge test: the meanings of steady and flashing lights and arrows, turning on red, line colors and passing lines, and special lane rules are core traffic-law content for the knowledge test.

Misconceptions: a dark or broken signal is not a free-for-all, it is treated as a stop sign; a green light does not mean go immediately if cross traffic is still clearing; you do not slam on the brakes when the light turns yellow after you are already in the intersection; left on red is not legal from a two-way street; the slow-vehicle passing exception never covers farm equipment.
Mnemonics: flashing red means stop then go, steady red means stop and wait; white lines split lanes going your way, yellow lines split you from oncoming traffic; dashed means you may, solid means you may not; red X closed, yellow X leave, green arrow drive.
Glossary: steady signal means a light that stays on; flashing signal means a light that blinks on and off; edge line means the line marking the side of the travel lane; shared center lane means a middle lane for left turns from both directions; reversible lane means a lane whose direction changes by time of day; reserved lane means a lane for one vehicle type such as buses or bikes; flex lane means a shoulder opened as a travel lane.
Dynamic facts: reversible, reserved and flex lanes open and close by posted hours, traffic volume and special events, so read the lane lights and signs each time you drive them.
