Misconceptions: the two-way left turn lane is not a passing lane or an acceleration lane, it is only for left turns; a quiet light rail train can be close before you hear it, so watch for headlights instead of waiting for noise; a red arrow at a light rail crossing means no turn even if the way looks clear; the left turn from a one-way road onto a two-way road is not one of the turns allowed on red.
Mnemonics: White lights coming, red lights going; in the center lane, turn left only.
Glossary: two-way left turn lane means a shared center lane used for left turns by traffic from both directions; trackway means the strip of street reserved for light rail trains; coupler means the connector that joins one light rail car to the next; frontage road means a local road running alongside a main route or the tracks.
Dynamic facts: the METRO system's length, its signal-controlled turn locations and its station layouts can change as the line is extended or rebuilt.
