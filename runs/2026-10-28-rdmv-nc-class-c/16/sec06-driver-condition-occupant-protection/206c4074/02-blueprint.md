Misconceptions: air bags do not replace seat belts, they only supplement them; coffee or a cold shower does not sober a drinker; the under-eighteen phone rule bans all phone use while moving apart from emergency calls, not just texting; a loose DOT sticker does not make a novelty helmet legal; a short nap is a temporary fix, not a cure for fatigue.
Mnemonics: belts first, bags second; kids in back; only time sobers; tired means stop.
Glossary: drowsy driving means driving while sleepy enough to lose focus; distracted driving means any activity taking attention from driving; child passenger restraint means a crash-tested child safety seat; booster seat means a seat raising a child so the adult belt fits; open bed means a cargo area without permanent overhead structure.
Dynamic facts: phone, seat belt, child restraint and helmet rules are set by statute and can change, and rental-seat programs vary by community; confirm with current North Carolina law.
