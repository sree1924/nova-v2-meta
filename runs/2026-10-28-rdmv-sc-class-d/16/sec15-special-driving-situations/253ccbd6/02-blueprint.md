Misconceptions: high beams in fog, rain or snow make seeing harder, not easier; tinted lenses reduce vision at night; a work zone still calls for slowing when no workers are visible; ice can form above freezing on wet bridges and in shade; spinning your wheels digs a stuck car in deeper; braking during trailer sway makes things worse, so you steer straight and ease off.
Mnemonics: dim at five hundred feet for oncoming cars, low beams within two hundred feet behind another; add one second at night and two on strange roads at night; sixty in front, forty in back; to back a trailer, hand at the bottom moves the way the trailer should go.
Glossary: high beams means the bright headlight setting that lets you see twice as far as low beams; work zone means an area of roadwork with lane closures, detours or equipment; blind corner means a corner you cannot see around; uncontrolled intersection means one with no stop or yield signs; trailer sway means side-to-side whipping of a trailer; coupler and hitch ball mean the trailer and vehicle parts that lock together.
Dynamic facts: the yearly count of people injured in work zone collisions and the manual's national trailer towing mileage are statistics that change over time; refresh them before they are shown.
