## A — Exam authority for the traffic signs, signals and pavement markings section (Tennessee Class D knowledge test)
Issuer: the Tennessee Department of Safety and Homeland Security, Driver Services Division, publishes the Tennessee Comprehensive Driver License Manual and takes written comments on it [A01].
Edition statement: the manual says its information was current as of July 1, 2022, and later law, rule or policy changes override anything outdated in it [A01].
Freshness: that content date is old and no issuer page confirms it as the current edition, so every fact here is held under freshness debt until rechecked (verify TN-SIGN-A-FRESH).
Knowledge test: a multiple-choice exam drawn from Sections B and C of the manual, split roughly equally across traffic signs and signals, safe driving principles, rules of the road, and drugs and alcohol [A02].
Format: given on computerized testing machines at Driver Service Centers, with Spanish, Korean and Japanese versions where computerized testing is offered, and oral tests by appointment for applicants who cannot read [A02].
Vision: a screening of at least 20/40 in each eye and in both eyes together, with or without corrective lenses, comes before any license or permit [A02].
Retake rule: after failing any knowledge test you must wait at least one day before trying again [A03].
Question count, passing score and time limit: the manual states none of them, so all three stay unknown and are never shown to learners (verify TN-SIGN-A-EXAM).
This section feeds the knowledge test: traffic signs and signals is one of the four roughly equal topic areas the manual names for the knowledge test, and this chapter covers sign colors and shapes, stop, yield and railroad signs, regulatory, warning, work zone and guide signs, signal lights and arrows, dark signals, pedestrian and lane signals, and pavement markings; its sample questions target the yield sign, the yellow left arrow and the solid-and-broken yellow center line.
Practice quizzes follow the product mastery policy from the learning loop, which is not an official exam threshold.
## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: traffic signs, signals and pavement markings
Audience: every first-time Tennessee Class D or learner permit applicant; this is one of the four topic areas the knowledge test samples.
Why it matters to a first-timer: the color and shape code [A01], the signal and arrow rules [A07] and the pavement line rules [A09] are recognized at a glance on every drive and make up a large share of test questions.
Level: recognition and recall with many look-alike pairs (turn versus curve, stop ahead versus yield ahead, solid versus broken lines); moderate load because meaning depends on color, shape and position together.
Prerequisites: none beyond the earlier Section B chapters; right-of-way and lane-use details continue in the rules of the road chapter.
