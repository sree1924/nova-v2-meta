## A — Exam authority for the night and inclement weather driving section (Tennessee Class D knowledge test)
Issuer: the Tennessee Department of Safety and Homeland Security, Driver Services Division, publishes the Tennessee Comprehensive Driver License Manual and takes written comments on it [A01].
Edition statement: the manual says its information was current as of July 1, 2022, and later law, rule or policy changes override anything outdated in it [A01].
Freshness: that content date is old and no issuer page confirms it as the current edition, so every fact here is held under freshness debt until rechecked (verify TN-NIGHT-A-FRESH).
Knowledge test: a multiple-choice exam drawn from Sections B and C of the manual, split roughly equally across traffic signs and signals, safe driving principles, rules of the road, and drugs and alcohol [A02].
Format: given on computerized testing machines at Driver Service Centers, with Spanish, Korean and Japanese versions where computerized testing is offered, and oral tests by appointment for applicants who cannot read [A02].
Vision: a screening of at least 20/40 in each eye and in both eyes together, with or without corrective lenses, comes before any license or permit [A02].
Retake rule: after failing any knowledge test you must wait at least one day before trying again [A03].
Question count, passing score and time limit: the manual states none of them, so all three stay unknown and are never shown to learners (verify TN-NIGHT-A-EXAM).
This section feeds the knowledge test: this chapter sits in Section B of the manual, from which the knowledge test is drawn, and most closely matches the safe driving principles area; it covers night visibility, outdriving headlights, glare and glare recovery, headlight aim and dimming, parking at night, rain, fog and smoke, slippery surfaces, hydroplaning, skids, running off the pavement, rumble stripes, flooding, storms, wind, sun glare, getting unstuck and winter braking; its sample questions target hard braking on ice, high beams in fog and the hazard of night driving.

## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: night and inclement weather driving
Audience: every first-time Tennessee Class D or learner permit applicant, including drivers who have only practiced in daylight and dry weather.
Why it matters to a first-timer: outdriving headlights and dimming rules [A01], fog and rain conduct [A02] and hydroplaning and skid recovery [A03] are where new drivers lose control, and they appear in the sample questions.
Level: procedural recall plus scenario judgment (which beam, when to brake, which way to steer); moderate load with a few exact values such as the headlight seeing distance, the dimming distance and flood depths.
Prerequisites: the vehicle familiarity chapter on headlights and brakes and the rules of the road chapters on following distance and parking.

Mnemonics: never outrun your headlights; low beams in fog, rain and snow; Turn Around, Don't Drown; steer where you want to go; overpower, over-brake, over-steer are the snow and ice errors.
Glossary: glare recovery time means the interval for your eyes to readjust after bright headlights pass; hydroplaning means tires riding on water instead of the road; skid means tires losing grip on the road; rumble stripe means an edge line painted over shallow indentations; antilock brakes means a system that keeps wheels from locking so you can steer.
