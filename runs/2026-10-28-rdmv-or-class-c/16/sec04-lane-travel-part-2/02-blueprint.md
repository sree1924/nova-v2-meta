## A — Exam authority for the curves, lane changes, passing, freeways and towing section (Oregon Class C non-commercial knowledge test)
Issuer: Oregon Driver and Motor Vehicle Services (DMV), part of the Oregon Department of Transportation, publishes the Oregon Driver Manual to help drivers get ready for Oregon licensing tests [A04].
Source document: Oregon Driver Manual, with the edition span 2026 - 2027 printed on the front cover [A01].
Scope: the manual covers only the Class C non-commercial driving privilege [A03].
Knowledge test: the Class C knowledge test has 35 multiple-choice questions [A02].
Passing standard: you must answer 28 of the 35 questions correctly, which is exactly 80 percent [A02].
Delivery: the knowledge test can be taken online through the DMV online service or at a DMV office, and the office version is offered in several languages with or without audio [A02].
Time limit: the manual does not state one, so it stays unknown and is never shown to learners (verify OR-PASS-A-TIME).
This section feeds the knowledge test: traffic laws are a named test topic, and this section holds scanning and curve technique, lane change checks, passing on the left and right, the no-passing situations and their exceptions, being passed, freeway entry, ramp meters, exits and breakdowns, large vehicles and the trailer passenger and towing limits.

## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: curves, lane changes, passing, freeways and towing
Audience: every first-time Oregon Class C applicant; passing and freeway rules are everyday driving law and common knowledge-test material.
Why it matters to a first-timer: passing errors cause sideswipes and head-on crashes [A02], the no-passing list is a set of exact conditions [A03], and freeway merging reverses the usual habit of slowing before a busy road [A05].
Level: condition-and-action recall (when passing is allowed, what to do on a ramp) with little arithmetic; moderate load.
Prerequisites: the first lane travel part, especially pavement markings, the basic rule and following distance.

Misconceptions: matching the flow of traffic does not excuse going over the posted speed; a dark ramp meter is not a stop signal; passing on the right is not banned outright but allowed only in the listed cases; waving a driver past is not a courtesy, flashing signals for it is unlawful.
Mnemonics: signal, mirror, shoulder before every lane change; slow into a curve, speed out of it; on-ramp means speed up, off-ramp means slow down after you leave; one green, one car at a ramp meter.
Glossary: blind spot means the area around a vehicle the driver cannot see even with mirrors; passing zone means a stretch where markings allow passing; ramp meter means a signal that releases on-ramp traffic one vehicle at a time; turnout means a roadside pull-off where slow vehicles let traffic pass.
Dynamic facts: ramp meters run only at certain times of day, and the fine amounts printed in the towing box are held as debt in part D until their scope is confirmed.
