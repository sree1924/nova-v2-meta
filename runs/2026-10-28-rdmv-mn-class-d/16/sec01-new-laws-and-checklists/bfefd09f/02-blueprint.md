## A — Exam authority for the new laws, office conduct and test-day checklists section (Minnesota Class D written test)
Issuer: the Minnesota Department of Public Safety, whose Driver and Vehicle Services division licenses drivers, permits the printing of the Minnesota Driver’s Manual [A01].
Source document: Minnesota Driver’s Manual, with the edition line June 2026 printed on the front cover [A02].
Written test format: multiple choice and true-or-false questions on Minnesota traffic laws and road signs, taken on paper or on a computer, with English, several other languages and American Sign Language at computer test stations [A03].
Passing standard: a score of 80 percent, and only one written test may be taken per day [A03].
Retest fee: after two consecutive failed written tests, a fee of $10 is charged for the third and every later attempt [A03].
Vision screening: normal or corrected vision of at least 20/40 and peripheral vision of at least 105 degrees [A03].
Question count: the manual does not state how many questions the written test has, so the count stays unknown and is never shown to learners (verify MN-NEWLAWS-A-COUNT).
Time limit: the manual does not state one, so it stays unknown and is never shown to learners (verify MN-NEWLAWS-A-TIME).
This section feeds the written test: the motorcycle lane-sharing rule, the school bus stopping rule and its divided-highway exception, work-zone hazards, and the documents, ages and permit holding times on the written and road test checklists.
Practice quizzes follow the product mastery policy from the learning loop, which is not an official exam threshold.
## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: new laws, office conduct and test-day checklists
Audience: first-time Minnesota Class D applicants of every age, especially teens under eighteen and their parents or guardians who sign the application, plus adults preparing for the written and road tests.
Why it matters to a first-timer: the chapter opens with law reminders that are easy test items, such as the school bus stopping rule [A01], and closes with the checklists that decide whether an applicant can sit the written test [A04] or the road test [A05].
Level: rule recall plus logistics; light cognitive load, but the ages, cards and permit holding times look alike and are easy to swap.
Prerequisites: none; this is the opening chapter of the manual.
