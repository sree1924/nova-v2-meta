Misconceptions: the space directly behind a large truck is invisible to its driver, not just hard to see; a motorcycle looks farther away and slower than it is, so a second and third look are needed; a motorcycle's blinking signal may simply be one the rider forgot to cancel; a motorcyclist is entitled to the whole lane, so a car never shares it; the trailer brake rule triggers at half the tow vehicle's weight or at three thousand pounds, whichever applies; too little hitch weight, not too much, causes fishtailing.

Mnemonics: Second look, third look for a motorcycle; two seconds behind it and a full lane beside it; ten to fifteen percent on the hitch, chains to the frame, brakes at three thousand pounds or half the tow weight.

Glossary: tractor-semitrailer (a truck tractor pulling a semitrailer); slipstream (the wind gust a large fast vehicle creates); breakaway protection (a device that brakes a trailer if it separates from the tow vehicle); fishtailing (side-to-side swaying of a trailer with too little hitch weight); lane position (the part of the lane a motorcyclist chooses in order to see and be seen); placard (the marking that shows a truck's hazardous cargo).
