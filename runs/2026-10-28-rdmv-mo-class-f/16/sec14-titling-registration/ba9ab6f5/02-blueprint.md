## A — Exam authority for the vehicle titling and registration section (Missouri Class F operator license written test)
Issuer: the Missouri Department of Revenue publishes the Missouri Driver Guide [A01].
Source document: Missouri Driver Guide, with the front cover reading Revised August 2026 [A02].
Written test: 25 multiple-choice questions, all drawn from information in the guide [A03].
Passing standard: 20 of the 25 questions must be answered correctly, which is 80 percent [A03].
The driver examination has four parts (written, vision, road sign and driving skills) and is given by the Missouri State Highway Patrol; the Class F written test is not open book and leaves out the commercial vehicle chapter [A04].
Time limit: the guide does not state one, so it stays unknown and is never shown to learners (verify MO-TITLE-A-TIME).
This section feeds the written test: the title deadline and fees, the MSO and properly assigned titles, out-of-state purchases and the ID/OD form, odometer disclosure, registering as a resident or new resident, two-year registration, renewal requirements, the notice of sale, and replacing stolen or lost plates, tabs and titles.
Practice quizzes follow the product mastery policy from the learning loop, which is not an official exam threshold.
## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: vehicle titling and registration
Audience: first-time Missouri Class F applicants, including adult newcomers who bring a vehicle from another state and teens whose family will title and register a first vehicle.
Why it matters to a first-timer: the title deadline and fees are exact values [A01], renewal timing and the two-year registration rule are easy to confuse [A03], and the notice of sale and stolen plate rules carry exact limits [A04].
Level: deadline, fee and document recall with several look-alike values; low to moderate load.
Prerequisites: the mandatory insurance chapter for proof of financial responsibility and the inspections chapter for the inspection certificate.

Misconceptions: a title is not something to keep in the glove box; a missing renewal notice does not excuse late renewal; an MSO is for a new vehicle and an assigned title is for a used one; stolen plates are replaced free only with a notarized affidavit and still carry the processing fee; a two-year registration is not open to every vehicle.
Mnemonics: even with even and odd with odd for two-year registration; title in the drawer, not in the car.
Glossary: title means the document proving vehicle ownership; MSO means Manufacturer's Statement of Origin, the new-vehicle ownership document; properly assigned title means a title with the seller-to-buyer assignment area completed; ID/OD verification means the identification number and odometer check done by a licensed inspection station; lienholder means a lender with a claim on the vehicle; year tab means the sticker on the plate showing the registration year.
Dynamic facts: title, plate, tab and processing fees, sales tax amounts, and the online renewal and specialty plate pages can change and must be refreshed from the Department of Revenue.
