## A — Exam authority for the young drivers, permits, license classes, renewals, ID cards and fees section (Nevada Basic Class C driver license knowledge test)
Issuer: the Nevada Department of Motor Vehicles publishes the Nevada Driver's Handbook and says the knowledge test for a Nevada license draws on what the handbook contains [A01].
Source document: the Nevada Driver's Handbook whose front cover carries the form and edition line DMV 700 March 2024 [A02].
Edition freshness: this edition was already about two and a half years old when it was staged, so whether it is still the current one stays open until the DMV confirms it (verify NV-LICB-A-FRESH).
Knowledge test format: it checks your grasp of highway signs and markings, traffic laws and safe driving practices, on automated testing equipment or on paper; an oral exam can be requested, and Basic Class C testing is also offered in Spanish [A03].
Vision: every applicant has vision checked against minimum standards, and needing glasses or contact lenses to drive puts a restriction on the license [A03].
Question count, passing standard and time limit: the handbook states none of them, so all three stay unknown and are never shown to learners (verify NV-LICB-A-COUNT, NV-LICB-A-PASS, NV-LICB-A-TIME).
This section feeds the knowledge test: it holds the teen-specific rules most likely to be tested: the young-driver penalty affidavit, driver's education and practice hours, the instruction permit and its supervising driver, the passenger limit and curfew, license classes and restriction codes, renewals and change reporting, ID cards and fees.
Practice quizzes follow the product mastery policy from the learning loop, which is not an official exam threshold.
## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: young drivers, permits, license classes, renewals, ID cards and fees
Audience: first-time Nevada Basic Class C applicants, above all teens under eighteen and their parents or guardians, plus adults checking renewal, ID card and fee rules.
Why it matters to a first-timer: the young-driver affidavit and its penalties come before any permit [A01], and the permit, supervising-driver, passenger and curfew rules govern the first months of driving [A03].
Level: procedural recall dense with exact thresholds (ages, hours, months, blood alcohol range, towing weights, fees); low conceptual load but many confusable numbers.
Prerequisites: sec01 (eligibility, documents and the Testing page).

Misconceptions: the teen curfew does not end when the passenger limit ends, it lasts until eighteen; a first-time DUI by a young driver is not treated more leniently than an adult's; getting an ID card is not neutral, it surrenders your driving privilege; Class C towing over ten thousand pounds needs the J endorsement, not a commercial license, until the combination passes twenty-six thousand pounds.
Mnemonics: fifteen and a half for the permit, six months holding it, six months with no young passengers; ten to five is curfew; fifty hours with ten at night, or one hundred with ten in the dark when far from a school.
Glossary: DLD-130 means the Beginning Driver Experience Log; DMV-301 means the school Certification of Attendance; endorsement means added permission for a vehicle needing more skill; restriction code means a letter or number on the license limiting how you may drive; reinstatement means restoring a suspended or revoked privilege.
Dynamic facts: fees are expressly subject to change, and forms, websites and phone numbers may be revised; confirm them on the Nevada DMV website.
