## A — Exam authority for the safe vehicle operation part two (work zones, railroad crossings, sharing the road, parking and reversing, pedestrians, seat belts, child restraints and equipment) section (Indiana learner's permit and driver's license knowledge exam)
Issuer: the Indiana Bureau of Motor Vehicles (BMV) publishes the Indiana Driver's Manual; both names appear on the front cover [A02].
Source document: the front cover prints no edition; the back cover reads Published July 2026 and points readers to IN.gov/BMV for updates [A04].
Knowledge exam: a multiple-choice, computer-based exam drawn from Indiana law, defensive driving practices and the manual, which also tests reading of regulatory, warning and traffic signs and signals [A01].
Exam structure: the exam has two components, one on common signs (Chapter 6) and one on traffic rules and laws (Chapters 6, 7 and 8) [A03].
Passing standard: 80 percent or higher correct on each component [A03].
Branch logistics: arrive at least 30 minutes before the branch closes to test that day; after a failure, wait until the next business day to retake [A01].
Question count: the manual gives no number of questions for either component or in total, so it stays unknown and is never shown to learners (verify IN-SAFETWO-A-COUNT).
Time limit: the manual does not state one, so it stays unknown (verify IN-SAFETWO-A-TIME).

## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: safe vehicle operation part two (work zones, railroad crossings, sharing the road, parking and reversing, pedestrians, seat belts, child restraints and equipment)
Audience: first-time Indiana permit and license applicants, including teens, who will soon share the road with trains, school buses, trucks, motorcycles, bicycles and pedestrians.
Why it matters to a first-timer: railroad crossing stops [A02], the Move Over law [A05] and the school bus stop rule [A06] are high-consequence rules that carry infractions or criminal penalties.
Level: rule recall (distances, infraction classes, age thresholds) plus scenario judgment about right of way, blind spots and disabled-vehicle warnings.
Prerequisites: the first half of the safe-operation chapter for passing, turning and speed; the signs chapter for railroad and school sign shapes.

Misconceptions: a crossbuck alone does not always require a stop, but an approaching train does; a stop sign at a crossing never means a train is not coming; a tractor-trailer driver sitting higher does not see you everywhere; the move-over rule covers amber-light work and tow vehicles and disabled cars, not only police and ambulances; air bags do not replace seat belts.
Mnemonics: fifteen to fifty feet from the rail for buses and hazmat; off the tracks at an angle, toward the train; downhill to the curb, uphill away; three feet to pass a bike; if you cannot see the mirrors, the trucker cannot see you.
Glossary: crossbuck means the X-shaped sign marking a railroad crossing; flagger means a worker directing traffic in a work zone; Move Over law means Indiana's rule to change lanes or slow down near flashing emergency, amber or hazard lights; stop arm means the swing-out stop sign on a school bus; child restraint system means a car seat or booster seat; ENS means the railroad's Emergency Notification System.
Dynamic facts: the Emergency Notification System number differs by crossing and is read from the blue sign on site, not memorized.
