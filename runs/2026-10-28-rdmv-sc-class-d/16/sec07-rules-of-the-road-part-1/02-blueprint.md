## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: rules of the road: right-of-way, traffic signals, warning, railroad and work zone signs
Audience: every first-time South Carolina Class D applicant; the yielding and signal rules apply from the first permit drive.
Why it matters to a first-timer: the four-way stop tie rule and the left turn yielding rule are set out here [A02], along with what steady and flashing lights require and when a right turn on red is allowed [A03].

Misconceptions: no driver is ever automatically owed the right-of-way; a steady yellow asks you to stop, not to speed up, unless you are already in the intersection; a flashing red works like a stop sign, while a steady red means waiting for green; right on red still needs a full stop first; not every railroad crossing has lights or a track-count sign.
Mnemonics: first to arrive goes first, and on a tie the car on the right goes; red over yellow over green from top to bottom, or red, yellow, green from left to right; yellow diamond warns, orange works.
Glossary: right-of-way means the privilege of going first that a driver must sometimes yield; traffic control device means any signal, sign, marking or official direction controlling traffic; grade crossing means a place where a highway crosses railroad tracks at the same level; flagger means a person in a work zone who directs traffic with a sign or flag; fluorescent yellow means the brighter yellow used on some school and pedestrian warning signs.
