Misconceptions: the belt law covers passengers too, not only the driver; age six alone does not end the child-restraint rule, a child under fifty-seven inches still needs a restraint; a rear-facing seat in the front passenger seat is not safe when that seat has an airbag; leaving a pet in a hot or freezing car is a crime, not just poor judgment.
Mnemonics: under two, rear-facing in back; under six or under fifty-seven inches, approved restraint; seven or younger never left alone without a watcher of twelve or more.
Glossary: child restraint system means an approved car seat, booster or belt-positioning seat; rear-facing seat means a seat installed so the baby faces the back of the car; shoulder harness means the upper-body belt paired with the lap belt; belt-positioning seat means a booster that routes the adult belt correctly over a child.
Dynamic facts: the crash-fatality statistics cite a fixed reporting period and will be replaced in later editions; they are context, not rules to memorize.
