Issuer: the Nevada Department of Motor Vehicles publishes the Nevada Driver's Handbook and says the knowledge test for a Nevada license draws on what the handbook contains [A01].
Source document: the Nevada Driver's Handbook whose front cover carries the form and edition line DMV 700 March 2024 [A02].

## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: special driving conditions (night, weather, emergencies, floods, work zones and stopped emergency vehicles)
Audience: every first-time Nevada Basic Class C applicant; the night, weather and emergency rules apply statewide, including desert flash floods and winter mountain driving.
Why it matters to a first-timer: headlight requirements and dimming distances are exact legal values [A01], and the duty to slow and move over for stopped emergency and service vehicles is a Nevada statute [A06].

Misconceptions: low beams are not just for city streets, they are required before meeting or following another vehicle; sunglasses never help at night; pumping is not the method for an ABS car on ice, firm steady pressure is; a few inches of moving water is not safe to drive through; orange speed signs are advisory but black and white ones are the law; when a car runs onto the shoulder, swerving straight back is the mistake.
Mnemonics: five hundred to meet, three hundred to follow; half an hour after sunset, half an hour before sunrise; turn around, don't drown; never park when brakes or pedal fail; slow down and move over.
Glossary: low beams means the dipped headlight setting used near other vehicles; skid means the tires losing traction; blowout means a tire suddenly losing air; flash flood means sudden fast-rising water after storms or thaw; advisory speed means a suggested safer speed on an orange sign; move over means changing out of the lane next to a stopped emergency vehicle.
Dynamic facts: the studded-tire season dates, work-zone penalties and the shoulder-use list are set by law and can change by statute, so confirm them against the current Nevada handbook.
