Misconceptions: alcohol is a depressant even though it can feel stimulating; coffee, food and cold showers wake you but never sober you, only time does; the kind of drink does not matter, only the amount of alcohol in the blood; refusing the breath or blood test still brings revocation and can still lead to a driving under the influence charge; the under-twenty-one limit is point zero two, far below the adult point zero eight; impaired begins above point zero five while under the influence begins at point zero eight.

Mnemonics: Point zero two for under twenty-one, above point zero five is impaired, point zero eight is under the influence; refusal means thirty days of temporary permit then one, two or four years of revocation; only time sobers you.

Glossary: depressant (a substance that slows the nervous system, as alcohol does); implied consent (the agreement to be tested that every driver gives by driving on public roads); administrative license revocation (loss of the license through the licensing agency after a failed or refused test, with a right to a hearing); zero tolerance (the near-zero alcohol limit for drivers under twenty-one); open container (an opened alcoholic beverage kept in the passenger area).

Dynamic facts: the national fatal-crash statistic is a figure that changes year to year and is stated only as this edition prints it; the thresholds, revocation periods and permit length are statutory values stable within this printing.
