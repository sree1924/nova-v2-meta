FACT_ID: sec07-F01 | anchor: A01
A driver sits upright with back supported, feet resting on the floor, and pedals comfortably reachable [A01].
Why it matters: establishes the base posture before belts, mirrors, and controls are adjusted.
Source: [A01 -> src:rdmv-ut-class-d:utah-dld:handbook:pdf-0037]

FACT_ID: sec07-F02 | anchor: A01
A driver keeps about 10 inches of space between chest and steering wheel, keeps the seatback upright, and keeps the wheel top at or below shoulder and chin height [A01].
Why it matters: preserves steering control while keeping distance from a deploying air bag.
Source: [A01 -> src:rdmv-ut-class-d:utah-dld:handbook:pdf-0037]

FACT_ID: sec07-F03 | anchor: A01
A driver positions the headrest to touch the back of the head above ear level and moves the foot between pedals with the heel staying on the floor [A01].
Why it matters: reduces whiplash risk and prevents pedal error.
Source: [A01 -> src:rdmv-ut-class-d:utah-dld:handbook:pdf-0037]

FACT_ID: sec07-F04 | anchor: A01
A driver centers the inside mirror on the rear window and selects the day or night setting to match driving conditions [A01].
Why it matters: gives a stable rear view and reduces nighttime glare.
Source: [A01 -> src:rdmv-ut-class-d:utah-dld:handbook:pdf-0037]

FACT_ID: sec07-F05 | anchor: A01
A driver sets the outside mirrors so only a sliver of the vehicle side shows, widening coverage of neighboring lanes to shrink blind spots from a normal seated position [A01].
Why it matters: the most commonly misadjusted control; correct setting changes lane-change safety.
Source: [A01 -> src:rdmv-ut-class-d:utah-dld:handbook:pdf-0037]

FACT_ID: sec07-F06 | anchor: A01
Every person in the vehicle uses a fastened belt or child seat, because belts hold occupants inside, reduce crash injury, and help the driver keep control [A01].
Why it matters: universal-restraint rule underlies every belt and child-seat question.
Source: [A01 -> src:rdmv-ut-class-d:utah-dld:handbook:pdf-0037]

FACT_ID: sec07-F07 | anchor: A01
A correctly worn belt has a snug shoulder strap across shoulder and chest plus a lap portion low across the hips, fastened securely and never tucked under the arm or behind the back [A01].
Why it matters: distinguishes correct fit from common misuse.
Source: [A01 -> src:rdmv-ut-class-d:utah-dld:handbook:pdf-0037]

FACT_ID: sec07-F08 | anchor: A02
Utah law requires every passenger to buckle up, and every child age eight or younger must ride properly secured in a car or booster seat [A02].
Why it matters: pairs the universal belt mandate with the stricter young-child rule.
Source: [A02 -> src:rdmv-ut-class-d:utah-dld:handbook:pdf-0037]

FACT_ID: sec07-F09 | anchor: A03
Crash forces equal body weight multiplied by vehicle speed, so a 150-pound occupant in a 30 mph crash experiences 4,500 pounds of force that cannot be resisted by bracing [A03].
Why it matters: explains why belts matter even at low speed, where most crashes happen.
Source: [A03 -> src:rdmv-ut-class-d:utah-dld:handbook:pdf-0038]

FACT_ID: sec07-F10 | anchor: A04
A driver who carries an unrestrained passenger can receive one citation for each unrestrained person, with special stop authority when anyone under 16 is unrestrained [A04].
Why it matters: converts belt knowledge into enforcement consequences.
Source: [A04 -> src:rdmv-ut-class-d:utah-dld:handbook:pdf-0038]

FACT_ID: sec07-F11 | anchor: A04
Every child age eight or younger must use an approved safety seat unless the child has reached 57 inches in height [A04].
Why it matters: the gateway threshold for all child-restraint staging.
Source: [A04 -> src:rdmv-ut-class-d:utah-dld:handbook:pdf-0038]

FACT_ID: sec07-F12 | anchor: A04
Children ride rear-facing in the back seat to at least age two and 30 pounds, then forward-facing with a harness in the back seat to at least age four and 40 pounds, then in a booster until the belt fits and they reach 57 inches [A04].
Why it matters: the full progression is the highest-yield testable sequence in this section.
Source: [A04 -> src:rdmv-ut-class-d:utah-dld:handbook:pdf-0038]

FACT_ID: sec07-F13 | anchor: A04
Children ages eight through twelve must use a suitable restraint or belt, and children age twelve or younger should keep riding in the back seat [A04].
Why it matters: extends protection beyond the booster stage.
Source: [A04 -> src:rdmv-ut-class-d:utah-dld:handbook:pdf-0038]

FACT_ID: sec07-F14 | anchor: A05
Air bags supplement belts rather than replacing them, deploy from the dashboard at up to 200 mph, and protect best when occupants are buckled and children ride restrained in the back [A05].
Why it matters: corrects the pillow misconception and links belts to air-bag effectiveness.
Source: [A05 -> src:rdmv-ut-class-d:utah-dld:handbook:pdf-0039]

FACT_ID: sec07-F15 | anchor: A05
Infants never ride in front of an active passenger air bag, children twelve and under ride buckled in the rear, and any child who must use an air-bag seat is moved fully rearward and properly secured [A05].
Why it matters: placement rules prevent air-bag injury to small occupants.
Source: [A05 -> src:rdmv-ut-class-d:utah-dld:handbook:pdf-0039]

FACT_ID: sec07-F16 | anchor: A05
Disabling or failing to repair a vehicle air-bag system is a Class B misdemeanor, and smoking in a vehicle carrying children age 15 or younger is an infraction when stopped [A05].
Why it matters: two distinct Utah penalties candidates often confuse.
Source: [A05 -> src:rdmv-ut-class-d:utah-dld:handbook:pdf-0039]

High-yield ranked: 1) belt fit and every-occupant restraint; 2) child-seat progression; 3) air-bag cooperation with belts; 4) seat distance and posture; 5) mirror setup.
Misconceptions: belts are unnecessary on short or slow trips; air bags replace belts; outside mirrors should show much of the car side; a shoulder strap behind the back is acceptable; older children are safe in front.
Mnemonics: HIPS not LIPS for lap-belt placement; BACK for kids in back, belts, and bags as helpers.
Glossary: lap belt; shoulder harness; blind spot; day/night mirror; booster seat; harness; restraint system.
