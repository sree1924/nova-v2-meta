Misconceptions: braking hard during a blowout or after running onto the shoulder makes losing control more likely; in a skid you steer the way the rear is sliding, not the opposite way; anti-lock brakes should not be pumped, but brakes without ABS should be pumped gently; repairs on the edge of a travel lane are never safe; stopping to help at a crash is protected from civil liability unless the helper does intentional wrong or acts unruly; when no one is hurt, drivable cars should leave the travel lane rather than wait there.
Mnemonics: low gear and emergency brake when the pedal fails; grip, straight, off the gas for a blowout; steer where the back goes in a skid; stop, help, report, exchange after a crash.
Glossary: emergency brake means the separate parking brake used when the foot brake fails; low range means the lowest gear position on an automatic shifter; blowout means a sudden loss of air from a tire; over-steer means turning the wheel too far while recovering from a skid; reportable crash means one with death, injury or damage at or above the reporting threshold.
Dynamic facts: the NC DOT animal-collision phone number and the dollar threshold for a reportable crash can change and are flagged as dynamic.

FACT_ID: sec13-R01 | anchor: A01
With failed brakes, a manual car downshifts and releases the clutch before the emergency brake, while an automatic applies the emergency brake and selects low range. [A01]
Why it matters: group brake failure; difficulty medium; confusable manual versus automatic.

FACT_ID: sec13-R02 | anchor: A01

Why it matters: group brakes; difficulty medium; confusable using versus sparing the brakes.
Source: [A01 -> pdf-0077]

FACT_ID: sec13-R03 | anchor: A01

Why it matters: group pedal emergencies; difficulty high; confusable stuck throttle versus failed brakes.

FACT_ID: sec13-R04 | anchor: A02
