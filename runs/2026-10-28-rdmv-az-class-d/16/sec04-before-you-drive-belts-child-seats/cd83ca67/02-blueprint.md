## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: before you drive: equipment, tires, safety belts and child restraints
Audience: first-time Arizona drivers and permit holders, including parents who will carry young children, preparing for the written test in English.
Why it matters to a first-timer: the driver answers for working equipment and for buckling up every passenger under 16 [A01], and child restraint, height and front-seat rules apply on every trip with young children [A02].
Level: practical recall of checklists and of age and height thresholds; a few numbers are easy to confuse (five, eight, twelve and sixteen).
Prerequisites: none; this is the first driving chapter of the manual.

Misconceptions: a lap belt alone is not a reason to skip buckling where that is all the seat has — it must still be fastened; reaching age five does not end child-seat use, the height rule continues until eight; a child in the front seat is not made safe by the air bag, it should be switched off; checking signals and brakes comes before the vehicle moves, not after the first stop.
Mnemonics: Seat, Wheel, Mirrors, then Signals and Brakes — adjust first, test before rolling; for tread, Lincoln's head should be partly hidden.
Glossary: psi means pounds per square inch, the unit for tire pressure; child restraint system means a car seat or booster that meets federal safety standards; emission inspection means the exhaust test some Arizona vehicles must pass; az511.com means the free ADOT travel-information website.
Dynamic facts: the child-safety hotline, the highway safety office phone numbers and the travel-information phone menu features are contact and service details that can change; the manual says only that a vehicle may need an emission inspection without saying which vehicles do, so check with ADOT for your own vehicle.
