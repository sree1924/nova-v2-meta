## A — Exam authority for the rules of the road section (Missouri Class F operator license written test)
Issuer: the Missouri Department of Revenue publishes the Missouri Driver Guide [A01].
Source document: Missouri Driver Guide, with the front cover reading Revised August 2026 [A02].
Written test: 25 multiple-choice questions, all drawn from information in the guide [A03].
Passing standard: 20 of the 25 questions must be answered correctly, which is 80 percent [A03].
The driver examination has four parts (written, vision, road sign and driving skills) and is given by the Missouri State Highway Patrol; the Class F written test is not open book and leaves out the commercial vehicle chapter [A04].
Time limit: the guide does not state one, so it stays unknown and is never shown to learners (verify MO-ROAD-A-TIME).
This section feeds the written test: the general duty of care, passing on the right and the no-left-of-center zones, intersection and roundabout right-of-way, moving and stopped emergency vehicles, school bus stops and penalties, signaling distance, turn-lane choice, U-turns and roundabout conduct.
Practice quizzes follow the product mastery policy from the learning loop, which is not an official exam threshold.
## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: rules of the road
Audience: first-time Missouri Class F applicants learning the core traffic rules that make up much of the written test, from teens on an instruction permit to adult newcomers.
Why it matters to a first-timer: right-of-way, emergency vehicle and school bus rules are stated as firm duties [A02], and the school bus offense carries points and suspensions [A03].
Level: rule application with several look-alike distances and lane counts; moderate load because right-of-way depends on the type of intersection.
Prerequisites: the examination overview and the license and GDL rules of the first two chapters.

Misconceptions: the law never gives anyone the right-of-way, it only says who must yield; you may not pass on the right by using the shoulder; you do not pull over in the middle of an intersection for a siren; drivers going the opposite way on an undivided two-lane road must stop for a school bus.
Mnemonics: right-hand rule at an uncontrolled intersection, the car on your right goes first; roundabouts run counterclockwise, so enter right, look left.
Glossary: right-of-way means the legal order of who must yield; roundabout means a circular intersection controlled by yield signs; two-way left-turn lane means a shared center lane used only to set up a left turn; stop arm means the sign that swings out from a stopped school bus.
Dynamic facts: work zone fine amounts are statutory and can change, and local ordinances can add speed limits, turn rules and parking controls that differ by city.
