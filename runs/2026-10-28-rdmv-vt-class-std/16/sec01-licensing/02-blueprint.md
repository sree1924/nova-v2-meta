## A — Exam authority, Chapter Four, general license information and the driving tests

This dossier serves first-time, never-licensed applicants preparing for the Vermont knowledge test under the standard noncommercial driver license / learner permit (no class letter asserted) path. The issuing authority is the Vermont Department of Motor Vehicles (Agency of Transportation), and the sole source is the pinned current edition of the Vermont Driver's Manual, whose edition year and form number are recorded in the run configuration and whose currency was confirmed on the issuer's site at registration. The licensing chapter of the handbook itself describes the knowledge test as an online test with a stated number of questions, four answer choices each and a stated minimum number of correct answers; those statements are carried as cited knowledge facts in part C. The safe exam-fact view bound to this run carries the official question count, passing standard, format and time limit as unknown, so this part asserts no value for any of them; where the handbook's licensing chapter states a count or a required number of correct answers, that statement lives only as a cited knowledge fact in the licensing section's part C, never as an official exam field here. Practice scoring follows only the accepted LearningLoop product policy, labelled as product policy. The corpus is one annual handbook edition, so no BlockedProbe is raised, and no time-variable or location-variable fact is carried as stable knowledge in this section.
## B — Audience, Chapter Four, general license information and the driving tests

The audience is first-time, never-licensed Vermont applicants, typically teenagers on the learner's permit and junior license path and adults applying for a first driver's license, reading at a plain-English level with no assumed driving vocabulary. This section teaches who must hold a Vermont license, how a learner's permit, a junior driver's license and a full driver's license are earned, what each stage restricts, the endorsements that sit outside the standard license, and what the vision, knowledge and road tests evaluate and require. The content assumes no prior rule knowledge and no prerequisites beyond literacy; every term the section introduces is defined in the glossary lines of part C.

Misconceptions:
- A learner's permit holder may drive with any licensed adult: false, the front seat supervisor must be a parent or guardian, a driver education instructor, or a licensed unimpaired person who is at least twenty-five years old.
- Practice hours logged for the junior license can include the behind the wheel hours of the driver education course: false, the forty practice hours are required in addition to the course hours.
- A junior license holder may carry friends once the first three months are over: false, the second three months allow only family members, and other passengers come only after six months.
- Failing the road test and failing the knowledge test carry the same wait: false, the knowledge test retake wait is one day while the road test wait is one week, rising to a month after three failures.
- Any licensed driver's license counts for a new resident: false, a license expired more than three years means taking the vision, written and road tests again.

Mnemonics:
- Sixty to settle, thirty for the truck: sixty days to get a regular license after moving, thirty days to transfer a commercial one.
- Thirty, six and six, then forty with ten dark: classroom, behind the wheel and observation hours, then practice hours with ten at night.
- Alone, family, anyone: the three passenger stages of the junior license, three months each until the limit lifts.
- Day, week, month: the waits after failing the knowledge test, the road test, and the road test three times.

Glossary:
- Learner's permit: the first stage document, earned at fifteen or older by passing the online permit test, that allows driving only with a qualified supervisor in the front seat
- Junior driver's license: the provisional license for sixteen and seventeen year olds who held a permit for a year, finished driver education and passed the road test
- Driver's license: the full license for people eighteen or older, valid for any noncommercial vehicle except a school bus or motorcycle
- Nighttime driving: driving between thirty minutes after sunset and thirty minutes before sunrise, for the purpose of the ten required night practice hours
- Type Two school bus endorsement: the regular license endorsement for carrying school children in buses rated under sixteen passengers
- Provisional license: a license that can be withdrawn by the Commissioner for unsafe driving or by a parent withdrawing permission

Dynamic facts isolated:
- None: every rule in this section is stable handbook law for the pined edition; no officeholder, date or fee amount is carried as knowledge.
