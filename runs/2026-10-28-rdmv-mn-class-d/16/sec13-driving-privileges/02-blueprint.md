## A — Exam authority for the suspension, revocation, limited license and cancellation section (Minnesota Class D written test)
Issuer: the Minnesota Department of Public Safety, whose Driver and Vehicle Services division licenses drivers, permits the printing of the Minnesota Driver’s Manual [A01].
Source document: Minnesota Driver’s Manual, with the edition line June 2026 printed on the front cover [A02].
Written test format: multiple choice and true-or-false questions on Minnesota traffic laws and road signs, taken on paper or on a computer, with English, several other languages and American Sign Language at computer test stations [A03].
Passing standard: a score of 80 percent, and only one written test may be taken per day [A03].
Retest fee: after two consecutive failed written tests, a fee of $10 is charged for the third and every later attempt [A03].
Vision screening: normal or corrected vision of at least 20/40 and peripheral vision of at least 105 degrees [A03].
Question count: the manual does not state how many questions the written test has, so the count stays unknown and is never shown to learners (verify MN-PRIVILEGE-A-COUNT).
Time limit: the manual does not state one, so it stays unknown and is never shown to learners (verify MN-PRIVILEGE-A-TIME).
This section feeds the written test: the driving record, the three forms of license withdrawal, the grounds for suspension and for revocation, reinstatement requirements, the limited license and its permitted trips, cancellation grounds and commercial license disqualification.

Misconceptions: refusing an alcohol test is not a way to avoid penalties, because refusal itself can bring revocation; a limited license does not restore general driving; suspension, revocation and cancellation are different actions with different grounds; a bad-check suspension ends only when everything is paid.
Mnemonics: suspend for repeats, money and paperwork, revoke for alcohol, speed over one hundred, fleeing and serious crimes, cancel when you never had the right; limited means work, treatment, school and dependents.
Glossary: suspension means a temporary withdrawal of driving privileges; revocation means a withdrawal that ends only after a period and full reinstatement requirements including a new license; cancellation means withdrawal because you have no legal right to the license; limited license means a restricted license for specific trips; reinstatement means getting driving privileges back after meeting the listed requirements.
