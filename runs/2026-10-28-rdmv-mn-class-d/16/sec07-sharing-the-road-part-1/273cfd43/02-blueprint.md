## A — Exam authority for the stopping, school buses, right-of-way, emergency vehicles, traffic stops, pedestrians and bicyclists section (Minnesota Class D written test)
Issuer: the Minnesota Department of Public Safety, whose Driver and Vehicle Services division licenses drivers, permits the printing of the Minnesota Driver’s Manual [A01].
Source document: Minnesota Driver’s Manual, with the edition line June 2026 printed on the front cover [A02].
Written test format: multiple choice and true-or-false questions on Minnesota traffic laws and road signs, taken on paper or on a computer, with English, several other languages and American Sign Language at computer test stations [A03].
Passing standard: a score of 80 percent, and only one written test may be taken per day [A03].
Retest fee: after two consecutive failed written tests, a fee of $10 is charged for the third and every later attempt [A03].
Vision screening: normal or corrected vision of at least 20/40 and peripheral vision of at least 105 degrees [A03].
Question count: the manual does not state how many questions the written test has, so the count stays unknown and is never shown to learners (verify MN-SHAREA-A-COUNT).
Time limit: the manual does not state one, so it stays unknown and is never shown to learners (verify MN-SHAREA-A-TIME).
This section feeds the written test: the required full stops, the school bus warning and stopping distances and penalties, school safety patrols and flaggers, the right-of-way order, yielding to buses and funeral processions, emergency vehicles and the Move Over Law, following fire vehicles, careless and reckless driving, conduct during a traffic stop, fleeing penalties, pedestrians and crosswalks, and bicycle lanes and bicycle laws.
Practice quizzes follow the product mastery policy from the learning loop, which is not an official exam threshold.
## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: stopping, school buses, right-of-way, emergency vehicles, traffic stops, pedestrians and bicyclists
Audience: first-time Minnesota Class D applicants, especially teens who will meet school buses, safety patrols, pedestrians and bicyclists on local roads.
Why it matters to a first-timer: the required stops [A01], the school bus stopping distance and penalty [A02], the right-of-way order [A03] and the bicycle passing clearance [A08] are core written test items.
