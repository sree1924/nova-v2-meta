## A — Exam authority for the required vehicle equipment, lights, tires and glass section (Minnesota Class D written test)
Issuer: the Minnesota Department of Public Safety, whose Driver and Vehicle Services division licenses drivers, permits the printing of the Minnesota Driver’s Manual [A01].
Source document: Minnesota Driver’s Manual, with the edition line June 2026 printed on the front cover [A02].
Written test format: multiple choice and true-or-false questions on Minnesota traffic laws and road signs, taken on paper or on a computer, with English, several other languages and American Sign Language at computer test stations [A03].
Passing standard: a score of 80 percent, and only one written test may be taken per day [A03].
Retest fee: after two consecutive failed written tests, a fee of $10 is charged for the third and every later attempt [A03].
Vision screening: normal or corrected vision of at least 20/40 and peripheral vision of at least 105 degrees [A03].
Question count: the manual does not state how many questions the written test has, so the count stays unknown and is never shown to learners (verify MN-VEHICLE-A-COUNT).
Time limit: the manual does not state one, so it stays unknown and is never shown to learners (verify MN-VEHICLE-A-TIME).
This section feeds the written test: the brake, bumper, horn, headlight, taillight, plate light, muffler, mirror, seat belt, tire, windshield, tint, wiper and fender requirements, the headlight-use rule, and which vehicles may use blue, flashing or strobe lights.
Practice quizzes follow the product mastery policy from the learning loop, which is not an official exam threshold.
## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: required vehicle equipment, lights, tires and glass
Audience: first-time Minnesota Class D applicants and the parents or owners who supply the vehicle for practice and for the road test.
Why it matters to a first-timer: equipment rules such as the two brake systems [A01], the headlight distances [A02] and the tread and tint limits [A03] are short numeric items on the written test, and the road test vehicle must meet them.
Level: straightforward recall with a few look-alike numbers (two headlight distances, two tint percentages); low to moderate load.
Prerequisites: the road test vehicle requirements in chapter one.

Misconceptions: the correct tire pressure comes from the door sticker, not the tire sidewall; daytime running lights do not turn on taillights; airbags do not replace seat belts and cannot prevent ejection; blue lights are not allowed on ordinary tow trucks, which may use flashing lights only.
Mnemonics: high beams reach three hundred fifty feet, headlights on when you cannot see five hundred feet; tint below fifty or above twenty is for the back of trucks and vans; Lincoln's head hidden means the tread is good.
Glossary: PSI means pounds per square inch, the unit of tire pressure; tread means the grooved rubber that grips the road; daytime running lights mean lights that switch on automatically in daylight; wheel guards mean fenders that stop tires from throwing debris.
