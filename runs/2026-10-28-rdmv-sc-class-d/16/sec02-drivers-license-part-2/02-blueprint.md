## A — Exam authority for the skills retests, residency, renewal, the point system and restricted licenses section (South Carolina Class D knowledge test)
Issuer: the South Carolina Department of Motor Vehicles (SCDMV) publishes the South Carolina Driver's Manual; the front cover names the agency and the manual [A01].
Source document: the front cover carries the edition line Revised 06/2024 [A01].
Required tests: an original license or an upgrade to a different class needs vision, knowledge and skills tests, and vision and knowledge tests can be taken at any SCDMV branch [A02].
Every first license test includes vision, knowledge and skills tests except the moped license, which has no skills test [A03].
Knowledge test content: traffic laws, road signs and driving safety rules [A04].
Test conduct: manuals, testing aids, written material and electronic devices are banned, and leaving the test area early or talking to anyone fails the test [A02].
Question count, passing score and time limit: the manual states none of them, so all three stay unknown and are never shown to learners (verify SC-LICB-A-COUNT, SC-LICB-A-PASS, SC-LICB-A-TIME).
Edition freshness: the issuer page links this Revised 06/2024 file, but a possibly newer unlinked upload exists on the issuer site and was not compared (verify SC-LICB-A-FRESH).
This section feeds the knowledge test: this part of the licensing chapter covers the skills retest waits, new-resident and visitor rules, renewal channels and vision proof, how points accumulate and trigger letters or suspension, and the provisional, route-restricted and temporary alcohol licenses.
Practice quizzes follow the product mastery policy from the learning loop, which is not an official exam threshold.
## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: skills retests, residency, renewal, the point system and restricted licenses
Audience: first-time South Carolina Class D applicants who may need a skills retest, new residents switching from another state, and every licensed driver who must understand renewal and the point system.
Why it matters to a first-timer: this part sets the waits between failed skills tests and the deadline for new residents [A01], and the point totals that bring a warning letter or a suspension, which are stricter for teen license holders [A07].
Level: procedural recall with many exact thresholds (day counts, months, point totals, fees, blood alcohol limits); moderate confusion risk between teen and adult point rules.
Prerequisites: the license classes, beginner's permit and graduated license stages from the first part of this section.

Misconceptions: the skills retest wait grows with each failure instead of staying fixed; an International Driving Permit only translates a foreign license and never replaces it; points halve after a year but do not vanish; a defensive driving course cannot lift a suspension that has already started; driving under the influence is handled by mandatory suspension, not points.
Mnemonics: retest waits climb two, seven, thirty, meaning two business days, then seven calendar days, then thirty calendar days; teen letters at two and four points, adult letter at six, suspension at twelve; points halve at one year.
Glossary: DUAC means driving with an unlawful alcohol concentration; ADSAP means the Alcohol and Drug Safety Action Program; route-restricted license means a license limited to an approved route during a suspension; Temporary Alcohol License means a short-term license while an alcohol suspension hearing is pending; Certificate of Vision Examination means an eye care professional's vision report accepted in place of a branch screening.
Dynamic facts: fees, mailing addresses, mailing times, renewal eligibility and the payment plan minimum can change; confirm them on the SCDMV website.
