Misconceptions: a yellow light is a warning to avoid entering, not a signal to speed up, and entering after red is always prohibited; a left turn on red is allowed only from a one-way street onto a one-way street; a flashing red signal is a stop sign, while a flashing yellow only asks for caution; a steady yellow X means get out of the lane, but a flashing yellow X allows the lane for a left turn; a flashing DON'T WALK lets pedestrians already in the street finish crossing.

Mnemonics: Stop line, then crosswalk, then intersection for the red stop point; flashing red is a stop sign, flashing yellow is caution; red X no, green arrow yes, yellow X leave, flashing yellow X left turn only.

Glossary: stop line (the white line marking the red-light stopping point); lighted arrow (a signal that governs one lane's turning movement); combination signal (arrows and standard lights working together); lane control signal (an overhead X or arrow that sets a reversible lane's direction); upraised palm (the pedestrian symbol for do not walk).

Dynamic facts: none; signal meanings are national conventions printed as stable knowledge in this edition.
