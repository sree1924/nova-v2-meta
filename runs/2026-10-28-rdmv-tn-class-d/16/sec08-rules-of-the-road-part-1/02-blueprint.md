## A — Exam authority for the rules of the road, part one section (Tennessee Class D knowledge test)
Issuer: the Tennessee Department of Safety and Homeland Security, Driver Services Division, publishes the Tennessee Comprehensive Driver License Manual and takes written comments on it [A01].
Edition statement: the manual says its information was current as of July 1, 2022, and later law, rule or policy changes override anything outdated in it [A01].
Freshness: that content date is old and no issuer page confirms it as the current edition, so every fact here is held under freshness debt until rechecked (verify TN-ROAD1-A-FRESH).
Knowledge test: a multiple-choice exam drawn from Sections B and C of the manual, split roughly equally across traffic signs and signals, safe driving principles, rules of the road, and drugs and alcohol [A02].
Format: given on computerized testing machines at Driver Service Centers, with Spanish, Korean and Japanese versions where computerized testing is offered, and oral tests by appointment for applicants who cannot read [A02].
Vision: a screening of at least 20/40 in each eye and in both eyes together, with or without corrective lenses, comes before any license or permit [A02].
Retake rule: after failing any knowledge test you must wait at least one day before trying again [A03].
Question count, passing score and time limit: the manual states none of them, so all three stay unknown and are never shown to learners (verify TN-ROAD1-A-EXAM).
This section feeds the knowledge test: rules of the road is one of the four roughly equal topic areas the manual names for the knowledge test, and this first half covers obeying officers, headlight rules, speed limits and the basic speed rule, following and stopping distances, stops required by law, railroad crossings, school buses, police stops, intersections, right-of-way, emergency vehicles, the Move Over Law and turn signals.
Practice quizzes follow the product mastery policy from the learning loop, which is not an official exam threshold.
## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: rules of the road, part one
Audience: every first-time Tennessee Class D or learner permit applicant; this is the core of the rules of the road topic on the knowledge test.
Why it matters to a first-timer: speed limits [A03], school bus and railroad stops [A07] and right-of-way at intersections [A10] are everyday legal duties and frequent test material.
Level: heavy rule recall with many exact distances, times, speeds and fines plus scenario judgment on who yields; the densest chapter so far.
Prerequisites: the vehicle setup and signs and signals chapters, which introduce braking, stop lines and signal meanings used here.

## D — Accuracy notes and source flags: rules of the road, part one
