## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: revocation, suspension, violation points, impound and court enforcement
Audience: first-time Arizona drivers and permit holders preparing for the written test in English, who need to know how violations turn into points, suspensions and revocations.
Why it matters to a first-timer: serious offenses bring revocation and points add up to a required school [A01], each violation carries a set point value and a chemical test refusal brings its own suspension [A02], and missing court or lacking insurance proof suspends a license [A03].
Level: practical recall of a ladder of point values and several look-alike suspension lengths and time windows.
Prerequisites: the impairment, aggressive driving and revocation pages that precede this section.

Misconceptions: revocation and suspension are different, since suspension is temporary while revocation removes the privilege until an investigation clears you; points stay on your permanent record, and it is eight points inside any twelve-month window that triggers Traffic Survival School; a red-light conviction brings mandatory school in addition to the court fine, not instead of it; refusing a chemical test is penalized on its own.
Mnemonics: Eight for the big six offenses, six for leaving a crash, three for speeding, two for the rest; insurance violations climb from three months to six months to a year.
Glossary: impound means towing and holding a vehicle, while immobilize means locking it in place; gore area means the space between a through roadway and an entrance or exit ramp, as the manual's freeway pages define it; chemical test means a test for alcohol or drug content; financial responsibility means proof of required vehicle insurance; restricted license means a license limited to certain driving.
Dynamic facts: point values, suspension lengths, impound periods and the grounds for MVD action are set by statute and MVD rules and can be amended.
