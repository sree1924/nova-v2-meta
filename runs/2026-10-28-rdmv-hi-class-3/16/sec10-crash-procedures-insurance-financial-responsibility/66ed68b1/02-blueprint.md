FACT_ID: sec10-F15 | anchor: A03

FACT_ID: sec10-F16 | anchor: A03
Each violation of the motor vehicle insurance law is a separate offense punishable by a fine of up to five thousand dollars or thirty days in jail, suspension of the driver's license, forfeiture of the vehicle registration certificate, or any combination of these [A03].
Source: [A03 -> src:rdmv-hi-x:hawaii-department-of-transportation-hdot:handbook:pdf-0054]

Misconceptions: basic insurance does not cover damage to your own car; the crash report threshold is three thousand dollars of property damage, while the bodily-injury lawsuit threshold is five thousand dollars of medical bills; SR twenty-one is the crash filing and SR twenty-two is the conviction filing kept for three years; you must leave a written notice after hitting an unattended vehicle even if no one saw it; injured people should not be moved unnecessarily.

Mnemonics: Three thousand to report, five thousand in medical bills to sue; twenty, forty and ten thousand are the liability minimums and ten thousand is the medical benefit; twenty-one after a crash, twenty-two after a conviction, for three years.

Glossary: safety responsibility law (the law requiring proof of financial responsibility after crashes and convictions); SR twenty-one (the evidence of insurance form filed after a crash); SR twenty-two (the evidence of insurance form filed after a conviction); aggregate limit (the total payable per accident regardless of how many people are injured); forfeiture of registration (loss of the vehicle's registration certificate as a penalty).

Dynamic facts: the dollar thresholds, coverage minimums and penalties are statutory amounts that the legislature can change between printings, so they are stated exactly as this edition prints them and should be rechecked at each new edition.
