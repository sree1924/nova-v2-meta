## C — Parking rules and roundabouts

FACT_ID: sec04-F01 | anchor: A01
Parking options on the road include parallel parking, angle parking to the left or right, and perpendicular parking to the left or right either forward or backward, and a driver must never cross the center line to park [A01].
Why it matters: Crossing the center line to park is prohibited.
Source: [A01 -> src:rdmv-vt-class-std:vermont-department-of-motor-vehicles-age:handbook:pdf-0030]

FACT_ID: sec04-F02 | anchor: A01
On a two lane road a parked vehicle's right wheels must sit no more than twelve inches from the curb or road edge, on a one way road the vehicle must be within twelve inches of the roadside, and in both cases it must point the same way as traffic [A01].
Why it matters: Twelve inches and facing traffic direction are both tested.
Source: [A01 -> src:rdmv-vt-class-std:vermont-department-of-motor-vehicles-age:handbook:pdf-0030]

FACT_ID: sec04-F03 | anchor: A01
When parking on a rural highway all wheels must be off the traveled portion of the road, and you must not stop on a hill, on a curve, anywhere the vehicle cannot be seen for at least one hundred fifty feet in either direction, or in an area posted for no parking [A01].
Why it matters: One hundred fifty feet of visibility is the rural parking rule.
Source: [A01 -> src:rdmv-vt-class-std:vermont-department-of-motor-vehicles-age:handbook:pdf-0030]

FACT_ID: sec04-F04 | anchor: A01
When parking on a hill you must make sure the vehicle cannot roll into traffic, so always turn the front wheels and set the parking brake [A01].
Why it matters: Wheels turned plus parking brake, every time on a hill.
Source: [A01 -> src:rdmv-vt-class-std:vermont-department-of-motor-vehicles-age:handbook:pdf-0030]

FACT_ID: sec04-F05 | anchor: A01
You may not park on the part of the highway that traffic uses, next to a car already stopped or parked at the street side, on a sidewalk, inside an intersection, on a crosswalk, beside road work where you would block traffic, or on a bridge or in a tunnel [A01].
Why it matters: Double parking and bridge or tunnel parking are both barred.
Source: [A01 -> src:rdmv-vt-class-std:vermont-department-of-motor-vehicles-age:handbook:pdf-0030]

FACT_ID: sec04-F06 | anchor: A01
You may not park on railroad tracks, closer than fifty feet to the nearest rail at a crossing, across the mouth of any driveway, or closer than six feet to a fire hydrant [A01].
Why it matters: Fifty feet from a rail, six feet from a hydrant.
Source: [A01 -> src:rdmv-vt-class-std:vermont-department-of-motor-vehicles-age:handbook:pdf-0030]

FACT_ID: sec04-F07 | anchor: A01
Parking is prohibited within twenty feet of a crosswalk at an intersection and within thirty feet of a flashing red or yellow light, a stop sign or a traffic light [A01].
Why it matters: Twenty feet for crosswalks, thirty feet for signs and signals.
Source: [A01 -> src:rdmv-vt-class-std:vermont-department-of-motor-vehicles-age:handbook:pdf-0030]

FACT_ID: sec04-F08 | anchor: A01
Where signs are posted, parking is prohibited within twenty feet of a fire station driveway and, across the street, within seventy-five feet of that driveway [A01].
Why it matters: The fire station rule reaches across the street.
Source: [A01 -> src:rdmv-vt-class-std:vermont-department-of-motor-vehicles-age:handbook:pdf-0030]

FACT_ID: sec04-F09 | anchor: A01
Parking is also prohibited anywhere an official sign says no parking, on a marked bicycle lane and in a marked disabled parking area [A01].
Why it matters: Bike lanes and disabled spaces round out the list.
Source: [A01 -> src:rdmv-vt-class-std:vermont-department-of-motor-vehicles-age:handbook:pdf-0030]

FACT_ID: sec04-F10 | anchor: A01
A roundabout is an intersection control where traffic enters and exits only by right turns and circulates counterclockwise, and roundabouts reduce vehicle delay, crashes, fuel use and air pollution [A01].
Why it matters: Right turns in and out, counterclockwise flow.
Source: [A01 -> src:rdmv-vt-class-std:vermont-department-of-motor-vehicles-age:handbook:pdf-0030]

FACT_ID: sec04-F11 | anchor: A02
In most situations a roundabout moves cars, walkers and cyclists through an intersection more safely than a stop sign or a traffic signal does, and a driver should keep speed low when approaching, circulating and exiting [A02].
Why it matters: Slow is the rule at every stage of a roundabout.
Source: [A02 -> src:rdmv-vt-class-std:vermont-department-of-motor-vehicles-age:handbook:pdf-0031]

FACT_ID: sec04-F12 | anchor: A02
Drivers must yield to traffic already in the roundabout, enter only when there is a safe gap, yield to pedestrians and bicyclists when entering or exiting, and always signal the intention to exit [A02].
Why it matters: Yield on entry, signal on exit.
Source: [A02 -> src:rdmv-vt-class-std:vermont-department-of-motor-vehicles-age:handbook:pdf-0031]

FACT_ID: sec04-F13 | anchor: A02
A multilane roundabout requires reading the lane assignment signs and pavement markings before entering to pick the proper lane, drivers may not change lanes once inside, and mirror and blind spot checks are required before exiting from the inside lane [A02].
Why it matters: No lane changes inside a roundabout.
Source: [A02 -> src:rdmv-vt-class-std:vermont-department-of-motor-vehicles-age:handbook:pdf-0031]

FACT_ID: sec04-F14 | anchor: A02
Entering a two lane roundabout that has no signs or arrows, the right lane may turn right or go through, while the left lane may turn left or go through [A02].
Why it matters: Both lanes may go through; only one may turn each way.
Source: [A02 -> src:rdmv-vt-class-std:vermont-department-of-motor-vehicles-age:handbook:pdf-0031]

FACT_ID: sec04-F15 | anchor: A02
Always yield to emergency vehicles at a roundabout: if you have not yet entered, pull over and let them pass, and if you are already inside, pull over only after you have safely exited [A02].
Why it matters: Never stop inside the circle for an emergency vehicle.
Source: [A02 -> src:rdmv-vt-class-std:vermont-department-of-motor-vehicles-age:handbook:pdf-0031]

Misconceptions:
- You may change lanes inside a multilane roundabout if the way is clear: false, lane changes are not allowed once inside; the lane is chosen from the signs and markings before entering.
- If an emergency vehicle approaches while you are circulating, stop inside the roundabout: false, keep going and pull over only after you have safely exited.
- Parking is fine beside a parked car for a quick stop: false, stopping beside a vehicle stopped or parked at the side of the street is prohibited.
- A parked vehicle may face either way on a two lane road: false, it must face the direction of traffic flow with its right wheels within twelve inches of the curb.

Mnemonics:
- Six hydrant, twenty crosswalk, thirty signal, fifty rail: the four no-parking clearances in rising order.
- Right in, right out, round to the left: a roundabout is entered and exited by right turns and travels counterclockwise.
- Wheels turned, brake set: the two hill parking steps.

Glossary:
- Roundabout: an intersection where all traffic enters and exits by right turns and circulates counterclockwise around a central island
- Traveled portion of the highway: the part of the road used by moving vehicles, where parking is prohibited
- Perpendicular parking: parking at a right angle to the curb, either forward or backward, to the left or right
- Lane assignment signs: signs and pavement markings before a multilane roundabout that show which lane may turn or go through
