## A — Exam authority for the special driving conditions section (Missouri Class F operator license written test)
Issuer: the Missouri Department of Revenue publishes the Missouri Driver Guide [A01].
Source document: Missouri Driver Guide, with the front cover reading Revised August 2026 [A02].
Written test: 25 multiple-choice questions, all drawn from information in the guide [A03].
Passing standard: 20 of the 25 questions must be answered correctly, which is 80 percent [A03].
The driver examination has four parts (written, vision, road sign and driving skills) and is given by the Missouri State Highway Patrol; the Class F written test is not open book and leaves out the commercial vehicle chapter [A04].
Time limit: the guide does not state one, so it stays unknown and is never shown to learners (verify MO-COND-A-TIME).
This section feeds the written test: night driving and beam distances, the headlight hours and wiper rule, snow and ice, wet pavement and hydroplaning, anti-lock braking, skid recovery, fog, and responses to brake failure, blowouts, running off the pavement, steering and headlight failure, a stuck accelerator, blocked vision, head-on vehicles and stalling on railroad tracks.
Practice quizzes follow the product mastery policy from the learning loop, which is not an official exam threshold.
## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: special driving conditions
Audience: first-time Missouri Class F applicants, from teens on an instruction permit to adult newcomers, who have little or no experience in darkness, winter weather or emergencies.
Why it matters to a first-timer: beam distances and headlight rules are exact test items [A01], snow, ice and hydroplaning guidance has exact gaps and speeds [A02], and emergency responses are ordered steps that are easy to get backwards [A04].
Level: rule recall plus ordered emergency procedures with several look-alike responses; moderate to high load.
Prerequisites: the everyday safe driving chapter on following distance and stopping distance.

Misconceptions: snow tires or chains do not make normal speeds safe on snow or ice; cruise control is not safe on wet or icy roads; you do not pump anti-lock brakes; four-wheel drive does not make ice safe; high beams make fog worse, not better; in a stall with a train coming you run toward the train's direction, not away along its path.
Mnemonics: ice and snow, take it slow; in a skid, steer the way the back end is sliding; off the gas, not on the brakes, for blowouts, hydroplaning and skids.
Glossary: overdriving your headlights means driving too fast to stop within the lit distance; hydroplaning means tires riding on a film of water and losing traction; anti-lock braking system means brakes that keep the wheels from locking in hard stops; edgeline means the painted line at the right edge of the road; blowout means a sudden tire failure.
Dynamic facts: headlight hours and the wiper headlight rule are statutory and can change, and vehicle-specific braking advice depends on the owner's manual.
