## A — Exam authority for the pavement markings, signals and warning signs section (Missouri Class F operator license written test)
Issuer: the Missouri Department of Revenue publishes the Missouri Driver Guide [A01].
Source document: Missouri Driver Guide, with the front cover reading Revised August 2026 [A02].
Written test: 25 multiple-choice questions, all drawn from information in the guide [A03].
Passing standard: 20 of the 25 questions must be answered correctly, which is 80 percent [A03].
The driver examination has four parts (written, vision, road sign and driving skills) and is given by the Missouri State Highway Patrol; the Class F written test is not open book and leaves out the commercial vehicle chapter [A04].
Time limit: the guide does not state one, so it stays unknown and is never shown to learners (verify MO-MARK-A-TIME).
This section feeds the written test: line types and colors, no passing zones, stop lines and crosswalks, dual-use and shared center lanes, lane rules without markings, traffic officers, steady, flashing and arrow signals, left-turn phases, sign shapes and colors, and common warning and intersection signs.
Practice quizzes follow the product mastery policy from the learning loop, which is not an official exam threshold.
## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: pavement markings, signals and warning signs
Audience: first-time Missouri Class F applicants, from teens on an instruction permit to adult newcomers; the separate road sign test makes this chapter doubly important.
Why it matters to a first-timer: signal meanings such as right on red and flashing lights [A03] and the shape and color code for signs [A04] are tested directly and are needed for the road sign part of the examination.
Level: high-volume recognition and recall with many look-alike pairs; moderate to high load.
Prerequisites: the rules-of-the-road chapter on right-of-way and turning, and the highway chapter on passing.

Misconceptions: a flashing yellow arrow does not give you the right-of-way over oncoming cars; a dark signal is not a free pass but a four-way stop; a green light still requires yielding to traffic and pedestrians in the intersection; a traffic officer overrides a green light.
Mnemonics: yellow means yonder, so traffic beyond a yellow line comes toward you, and white means with you; broken line, may pass, solid line, stay put.
Glossary: dual-use lane means a lane marked with both turn and straight arrows; shared center lane means a center lane both directions use for left turns; permissive turn means a left turn made in a gap in oncoming traffic; protected turn means a left turn made while oncoming traffic is stopped; regulatory sign means a sign stating a traffic rule; object marker means a sign flagging an obstacle near the lane.
Dynamic facts: the School Crossing sign is being changed over to yellow green during a transition period, so sign color in the field may vary, and local signal timing and No Right Turn On Red postings differ by location.
