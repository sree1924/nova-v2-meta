Misconceptions: a single solid white line may be crossed in unusual circumstances with great care, but a double solid white line may never be crossed; double yellow lines may be crossed to turn left into a driveway or alley even though passing across them is prohibited; the shared center left-turn lane is never a passing lane; yellow obstruction markings must be passed on the right only, while white ones may be passed on either side; a broken yellow centerline allows passing only when it is safe, not whenever it is present.

Mnemonics: Yellow divides opposite traffic, white divides same-direction traffic; broken means cross when safe, single solid means hazard, double solid means never; left turns into driveways are the one exception to solid yellow.

Glossary: regulatory device (a sign, signal or marking that requires an action, enforced by penalty); warning device (a device that advises of a hazard); centerline (the line separating opposing traffic, always yellow); edge line (a solid line along the pavement edge used as a guide); channelization markings (lines that assign a lane to a particular movement, such as the two-way left turn lane); safety island (a traffic island that shelters pedestrians).

Dynamic facts: none; marking colors, patterns and their meanings are national conventions printed as stable knowledge in this edition.
