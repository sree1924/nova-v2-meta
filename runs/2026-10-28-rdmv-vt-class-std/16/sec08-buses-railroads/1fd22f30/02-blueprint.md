## C — Sharing the road with motorcycles, trucks, slow vehicles, animals and wildlife (part two)

FACT_ID: sec08-F17 | anchor: A02
Large trucks have blind spots called no-zones around the front, back and sides, a truck could turn into a vehicle hidden there, and if you cannot see the truck's side mirrors the truck driver cannot see you [A02].

FACT_ID: sec08-F18 | anchor: A02

FACT_ID: sec08-F32 | anchor: A04
A collision with a moose or deer can kill the driver and passengers as well as the animal, so drive cautiously where deer and moose crossing signs are posted, especially in late evening, at night and in early morning when the animals roam and feed [A04].
Why it matters: Signed areas plus the dark hours.

FACT_ID: sec08-F33 | anchor: A04
Headlights may freeze a deer in place or send it bounding at up to forty miles per hour toward its habitat, so a deer on the far side of the road from its habitat may bolt in front of you, and animals travel in pairs so expect a second one after the first [A04].
Why it matters: Expect a second deer.
Source: [A04 -> src:rdmv-vt-class-std:vermont-department-of-motor-vehicles-age:handbook:pdf-0046]

FACT_ID: sec08-F34 | anchor: A04
Moose collisions are deadlier because a moose weighs more than a thousand pounds and stands much taller than a deer, so it can land on top of the vehicle with crushing force [A04].
Why it matters: Weight and height make moose crashes worse.
Source: [A04 -> src:rdmv-vt-class-std:vermont-department-of-motor-vehicles-age:handbook:pdf-0046]

FACT_ID: sec08-F35 | anchor: A04
Deer eyes reflect headlights, but a moose stands so tall that its eyes may not reflect and it is more darkly colored than a deer, which makes moose especially hard to see in the evening [A04].
Why it matters: No eye shine from a moose.
Source: [A04 -> src:rdmv-vt-class-std:vermont-department-of-motor-vehicles-age:handbook:pdf-0046]

Misconceptions:
- A motorcycle with its signal flashing is about to turn: not necessarily, motorcycle signals usually do not self cancel and may be left on by mistake.
- A truck driver can see you whenever you can see the truck: false, the test is whether you can see the truck's side mirrors; if not, the driver cannot see you.
- Honking warns a horse rider that you are coming: false, never sound the horn or rev the engine near animals; slow down, pass fully and leave four seconds.
- Four wheel drive stops better on ice: not claimed by the handbook here; the relevant point is that trucks, motorcycles and farm equipment each need extra following distance.
- Moose are easy to spot because their eyes shine: false, moose stand too tall for headlights to catch their eyes and are darker than deer.

Mnemonics:
- Closer and slower than it looks: the two motorcycle illusions corrected.
- No mirror, no driver: if you cannot see the truck's mirrors, the trucker cannot see you.
- Cab in the mirror before you move over: the truck passing rule.
- Quiet, wide and four behind: horn off, full lane to pass, four seconds behind animals.

Glossary:
- No-zones: the blind spots around the front, back and sides of a large truck where its driver cannot see other vehicles
- Slow moving vehicle emblem: the orange and red triangle on the rear of farm machinery and other vehicles that cannot travel at highway speed
- Convex mirror: a curved mirror, standard on motorcycles, that shows a smaller image and makes following vehicles look farther back than they are
- Animal drawn vehicle: a wagon or carriage pulled by animals, whose driver holds the rights of a motor vehicle driver on a public highway
