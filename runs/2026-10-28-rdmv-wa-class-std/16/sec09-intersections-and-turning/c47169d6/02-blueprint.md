## A — Exam authority for the Washington Driving Knowledge Exam

Issuer: the Washington State Department of Licensing (DOL) publishes the official Washington State Driver Guide [A15].
Edition: the guide prints the form line "520-400 (R/4/25)" on its inside front pages [A04]; the cover carries no edition date, and the current edition and effective date remain unconfirmed by the issuer (verify — sec09-A-EDITION).
Currency: the guide warns that it may not reflect the latest changes by the Washington Legislature and points readers to dol.wa.gov for the current version [A15].
Exam path: a new driver must pass two exams, the Driving Knowledge Exam on Washington's rules of the road and then the Driving Skills Exam [A32].
Order and timing: the knowledge exam must be passed before the skills exam, and after passing it you have two years to complete the skills exam [A32].
Where to test: exams can be taken at an approved driver training school exam location or by appointment at a DOL driver licensing office scheduled through License Express [A33].
Scoring: the guide states no question count, no passing score or percent, and no time limit for the knowledge exam; all three stay unknown and no number is asserted here (verify — sec09-A-SCORING).
Question format: the guide does not describe the item format of the knowledge exam (verify — sec09-A-FORMAT).
Practice mastery targets in this product are product policy from the accepted learning loop, never an official passing line.
Dynamic facts (updatable): DOL phone numbers, website services, office processes and fees can change between editions and are kept out of stable knowledge; why_dynamic: administrative details set by DOL outside the printed rules of the road.
## B — Audience and scope

Audience: first-time Washington applicants who have never held a license, studying for the Driving Knowledge Exam in English; this includes teens on the permit and intermediate-license path, adults licensing for the first time, and new residents checking transfer rules.
Level: plain-English reading level; no prior driving vocabulary is assumed, so every term from the guide is defined where it first appears.
Prerequisites: none beyond basic reading; earlier guide chapters are referenced by name, not assumed.
Section scope: guide sections on common intersections and what to do before entering one, right-of-way at four-way and two-way stops, turning and turn lanes, U-turns, cross, T and Y intersections, mid-block crossings, traffic calming circles, roundabouts, diverging diamond interchanges and uncontrolled intersections.
Out of scope: commercial, motorcycle and endorsement testing content, vehicle-title paperwork beyond what a new driver must know, and anything not printed on the selected guide pages.

FACT_ID: sec09-F22 | anchor: A126

FACT_ID: sec09-F24 | anchor: A127

FACT_ID: sec09-F27 | anchor: A127
Stop for pedestrians and bicyclists in the crosswalks on both entry and exit, look left and yield to all traffic already circulating, then enter to the right going counterclockwise when there is a gap. [A127]
Why it matters: traffic already inside the roundabout has the right-of-way over entering traffic.
Source: [A127 -> src:rdmv-wa-s:washington-state-department-of-licensing:handbook:pdf-0127]

FACT_ID: sec09-F28 | anchor: A127
If an emergency vehicle approaches while you are in a roundabout, drive on through it and then pull over, as at any other intersection. [A127]
Why it matters: stopping inside the circle is not the move; you clear it first.
Source: [A127 -> src:rdmv-wa-s:washington-state-department-of-licensing:handbook:pdf-0127]

FACT_ID: sec09-F29 | anchor: A128
A diverging diamond is a newer Washington interchange that shifts traffic to the opposite side of the road so left turns onto and off freeway ramps flow better, with fewer crossing points and less crash risk. [A128]
Source: [A128 -> src:rdmv-wa-s:washington-state-department-of-licensing:handbook:pdf-0128]

FACT_ID: sec09-F30 | anchor: A129
In a diverging diamond, markings and signals guide you to the left side of the road, you turn left onto the freeway without stopping or yielding to oncoming traffic, and you cross back to the right after the overpass or underpass. [A129]
Source: [A129 -> src:rdmv-wa-s:washington-state-department-of-licensing:handbook:pdf-0129]

FACT_ID: sec09-F31 | anchor: A129
An uncontrolled intersection has no signs, the normal right-of-way rules still apply, and such intersections are usually on local roads and streets. [A129]
Source: [A129 -> src:rdmv-wa-s:washington-state-department-of-licensing:handbook:pdf-0129]

FACT_ID: sec09-F32 | anchor: A129
At an uncontrolled intersection you must yield if a vehicle is already in it, if you are entering or crossing a state highway from a secondary road, if you are moving from an unpaved to a paved road, or if you are turning left with a vehicle coming from the opposite direction. [A129]
Source: [A129 -> src:rdmv-wa-s:washington-state-department-of-licensing:handbook:pdf-0129]

Glossary: right-of-way — the order the law gives for moving through an intersection [A122]. Yield — give up the right-of-way [A122]. Truck apron — the strip around a roundabout's center island built for large vehicles [A127]. Diverging diamond — an interchange that briefly moves traffic to the left side for easier left turns [A128]. Uncontrolled intersection — one with no signs [A129].
Memory aid: at a four-way stop think first come, first go, and ties go to the right [A122].
