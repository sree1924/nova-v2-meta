## A — Exam authority for the defensive driving, signals and turns, U-turns, passing and parking section (Nevada Basic Class C driver license knowledge test)
Issuer: the Nevada Department of Motor Vehicles publishes the Nevada Driver's Handbook and says the knowledge test for a Nevada license draws on what the handbook contains [A01].
Source document: the Nevada Driver's Handbook whose front cover carries the form and edition line DMV 700 March 2024 [A02].
Edition freshness: this edition was already about two and a half years old when it was staged, so whether it is still the current one stays open until the DMV confirms it (verify NV-DEFP-A-FRESH).
Knowledge test format: it checks your grasp of highway signs and markings, traffic laws and safe driving practices, on automated testing equipment or on paper; an oral exam can be requested, and Basic Class C testing is also offered in Spanish [A03].
Vision: every applicant has vision checked against minimum standards, and needing glasses or contact lenses to drive puts a restriction on the license [A03].
Question count, passing standard and time limit: the handbook states none of them, so all three stay unknown and are never shown to learners (verify NV-DEFP-A-COUNT, NV-DEFP-A-PASS, NV-DEFP-A-TIME).
This section feeds the knowledge test: the knowledge test checks traffic laws and safe driving practices, and this section carries many tested rules: look-ahead and following distance, the handheld phone ban, roundabouts, hand signals and signalling distances, turn lanes, legal and illegal U-turns, passing limits, passing cyclists, parking position, curb colors and no-parking distances.
Practice quizzes follow the product mastery policy from the learning loop, which is not an official exam threshold.
## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: defensive driving, signals and turns, U-turns, passing and parking
Audience: every first-time Nevada Basic Class C applicant; this is the longest block of everyday driving rules in the handbook.
Why it matters to a first-timer: space-management habits such as the look-ahead and following-time rules prevent the most common crashes [A01], and the no-parking distances are exact values that are easy to confuse [A09].
Level: rule recall with many exact distances and several lists of allowed versus forbidden situations; moderate load from the number of confusable values.
Prerequisites: sec04 for lane markings and sec05 for right-of-way, which the turning, passing and U-turn rules build on.

Misconceptions: the two-second gap is a minimum, not a target; a right-turn-only lane cannot be used to go straight; the left lane of a roundabout can also go straight; U-turns are not banned everywhere in Nevada, only in listed places; passing on the shoulder is never legal; on a hill with a curb, uphill tires point away from the curb, not toward it.
Mnemonics: twelve to look, two to follow, four to be safe; city one hundred, highway three hundred for signals; hydrant fifteen parallel, twenty angle; crosswalk twenty, signal thirty; curb colors white wait, green go-limited, yellow load, red no, blue disabled.
Glossary: following distance means the time gap between you and the vehicle ahead; roundabout means a circular intersection where traffic flows counterclockwise without signals; truck apron means the raised ring around a roundabout's center island for large trucks; blind spot means the area your mirrors do not show; business district means an area of businesses, churches, apartments or public buildings where mid-block U-turns are banned.
Dynamic facts: local authorities and the state transportation department can ban U-turns at any location, and limited-time parking periods are shown on local signs, so posted signs govern.
