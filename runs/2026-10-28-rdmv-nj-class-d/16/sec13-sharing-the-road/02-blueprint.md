## A — Exam authority for the sharing the road with pedestrians, cyclists, motorcycles, trucks, buses, trains and animals section (New Jersey Class D basic license knowledge test)
Issuer: the New Jersey Motor Vehicle Commission (MVC) publishes the New Jersey Driver Manual, which prepares applicants for the knowledge portion of the driver test [A01].
Source document: New Jersey Driver Manual, with 2026 printed on the front cover [A02].
Knowledge test: 50 questions, plus a survey question about organ donation [A03].
Passing standard: 80 percent, which means 40 of the 50 questions must be answered correctly [A04].
Vision screening is required for every motorist, and basic license applicants need 20/50 vision with or without corrective lenses [A04].
Time limit: the manual does not state one, so it stays unknown and is never shown to learners (verify NJ-SHARE-A-TIME).
This section feeds the knowledge test: pedestrian right-of-way in marked and unmarked crosswalks, speed and pedestrian survival, turning near pedestrians, the Safe Passing Law and its penalties, motorcycles, truck and bus no-zones and wide turns, yielding to buses, mopeds, bicycles, skateboards and inline skates, motorized scooters and low-speed vehicles, railroad crossing rules, off-road vehicles, animals and horses.

Misconceptions: an unpainted corner is still a crosswalk; a pedestrian's mistake does not cancel the driver's duty of care; motorcycles can stop faster than cars, so tailgating one is dangerous; seeing a truck does not mean its driver sees you; a train looks farther away and slower than it really is.
Mnemonics: four feet or slow to twenty-five for the Safe Passing Law; five percent at twenty but eighty-five percent at forty for pedestrian deaths; a car the truck driver cannot see in the mirrors is in a no-zone; a mile or more for a freight train to stop.
Glossary: unmarked crosswalk means the legal crossing at an intersection corner with no painted lines; vulnerable road user means a pedestrian, cyclist, skater or similar user who is easily hurt; no-zone means a blind spot around a truck or bus; moped means a low-speed pedal-equipped motorized bicycle; low-speed vehicle means a small four-wheeled vehicle with a capped top speed.
Dynamic facts: fines, minimum ages set by local governments and the moped and motorcycle safety web pages can change; confirm them on official New Jersey websites.
