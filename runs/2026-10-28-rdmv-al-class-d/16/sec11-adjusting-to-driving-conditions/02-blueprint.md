## A — Exam authority for the Alabama driver license knowledge test

Issuer: the Alabama Law Enforcement Agency, Driver License Division, issues Alabama driver licenses [A07] and publishes the Alabama Driver Manual.
Edition: the front cover of the manual prints the edition line "November 2024" under the Driver License Division name [A01]; no effective date is printed, so the effective date stays unconfirmed (verify — sec11-A-EFFECTIVE-DATE).
Exam path: the manual describes a vision screening, a knowledge test with questions on Alabama traffic laws, road signs and safe-driving rules taken from this manual, and a road test given by a driver license examiner [A13].
Learner path: a fifteen-year-old who passes the required exam receives a Class D learner license with the Y restriction [A11].
Delivery: automated testing is available to every applicant taking the knowledge test [A13], and oral and foreign-language versions exist [A11].
Scoring: the manual states no question count, no passing score or percent, and no time limit for the knowledge test; all three stay unknown and no number is asserted here (verify — sec11-A-SCORING).
Question format: beyond automated delivery, the manual does not describe the item format of the knowledge test (verify — sec11-A-FORMAT).
Section link: this dossier section covers night driving with headlight and glare rules, winter driving, the wiper-headlight law, slippery roads, skids, hydroplaning, fog and snow, hilly country, carbon monoxide and what to do in driving emergencies, all drawn from the manual the test is built on [A13].
Practice mastery targets in this product are product policy from the accepted learning loop, never an official passing line.
Dynamic facts (updatable): the knowledge test fee, office locations, appointments and online services are set by ALEA and can change between editions, so they are kept out of stable knowledge [A13]; why_dynamic: administrative details outside the printed rules of the road.
## B — Audience and scope

Audience: first-time Alabama applicants who have never held a license and are preparing in English for the driver license knowledge test; this section matters most to new drivers who learned in good conditions and now face night, rain, ice and fog, and to anyone preparing for situational emergency questions.
Level: plain-English reading level; no prior driving vocabulary is assumed, so each term from the manual is defined where it first appears.
Prerequisites: the following and stopping distance rules of the earlier chapter on the driver, which these conditions lengthen.
Section scope: manual chapter seven: why conditions must be studied, night driving with vision, overdriving headlights, dimming distances, headlight hours, glare, night emergencies and night speed, winter driving preparation, snow treads and chains, the windshield wiper and headlight law, slippery roads with starting and stopping technique, skidding and recovery for rear- and front-wheel drive, hydroplaning, fog and snow, hilly country and coasting, carbon monoxide, and driving emergencies from blowouts and brake failure to immersion, fire, overheating, light and wiper failure and a stuck accelerator.
Out of scope: commercial, motorcycle-manual and vessel testing content, office logistics and fees, and anything not printed on the selected manual pages.
