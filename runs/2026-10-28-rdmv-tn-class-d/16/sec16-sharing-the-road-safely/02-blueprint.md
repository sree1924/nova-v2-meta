## A — Exam authority for the sharing the road safely section (Tennessee Class D knowledge test)
Issuer: the Tennessee Department of Safety and Homeland Security, Driver Services Division, publishes the Tennessee Comprehensive Driver License Manual and takes written comments on it [A01].
Edition statement: the manual says its information was current as of July 1, 2022, and later law, rule or policy changes override anything outdated in it [A01].
Freshness: that content date is old and no issuer page confirms it as the current edition, so every fact here is held under freshness debt until rechecked (verify TN-SHARE-A-FRESH).
Knowledge test: a multiple-choice exam drawn from Sections B and C of the manual, split roughly equally across traffic signs and signals, safe driving principles, rules of the road, and drugs and alcohol [A02].
Format: given on computerized testing machines at Driver Service Centers, with Spanish, Korean and Japanese versions where computerized testing is offered, and oral tests by appointment for applicants who cannot read [A02].
Vision: a screening of at least 20/40 in each eye and in both eyes together, with or without corrective lenses, comes before any license or permit [A02].
Retake rule: after failing any knowledge test you must wait at least one day before trying again [A03].
Question count, passing score and time limit: the manual states none of them, so all three stay unknown and are never shown to learners (verify TN-SHARE-A-EXAM).
This section feeds the knowledge test: this chapter sits in Section C of the manual, which the exam page names alongside Section B as the source of the knowledge test, and spans the rules of the road and safe driving principles areas; it covers pedestrians, bicycles and hand signals, motorcycles, large trucks and buses and their No-Zones, school buses, slow-moving farm and animal-drawn vehicles, horseback riders, work zones, trains and railroad crossings, passenger vans and recreational vehicles; no chapter sample questions are printed in these pages.
Practice quizzes follow the product mastery policy from the learning loop, which is not an official exam threshold.
## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: sharing the road safely
Audience: every first-time Tennessee Class D or learner permit applicant, including applicants who until now have mostly been pedestrians, cyclists or passengers.
Why it matters to a first-timer: yielding to pedestrians [A01], passing bicycles [A03], staying out of truck blind spots [A07] and railroad crossing rules [A11] protect the road users who lose most in a crash.
Level: rule recall plus many scenario judgments (who yields, where to wait, when to pass), with exact values such as the bicycle passing distance, stopping distances and railroad stopping limits; high load because the chapter is long.
Prerequisites: the rules of the road chapters on right-of-way, passing and railroad crossings, and the defensive driving chapter on space cushions.

Mnemonics: left, right, left before crossing; three feet to pass a bike; see the truck's mirror or it cannot see you; orange means slow down; ten feet and five giant steps at the school bus.
Glossary: No-Zone means a blind-spot area around a truck or bus where crashes are more likely; right turn squeeze means getting trapped between a right-turning truck and the curb; slow-moving vehicle emblem means the orange triangle on slow farm and animal-drawn vehicles; Emergency Notification System sign means the blue railroad sign with a number to report stalled vehicles.
