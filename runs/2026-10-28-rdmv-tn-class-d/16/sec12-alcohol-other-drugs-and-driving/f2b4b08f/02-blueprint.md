## A — Exam authority for the alcohol, other drugs and driving section (Tennessee Class D knowledge test)
Issuer: the Tennessee Department of Safety and Homeland Security, Driver Services Division, publishes the Tennessee Comprehensive Driver License Manual and takes written comments on it [A01].
Edition statement: the manual says its information was current as of July 1, 2022, and later law, rule or policy changes override anything outdated in it [A01].
Freshness: that content date is old and no issuer page confirms it as the current edition, so every fact here is held under freshness debt until rechecked (verify TN-ALC-A-FRESH).
Knowledge test: a multiple-choice exam drawn from Sections B and C of the manual, split roughly equally across traffic signs and signals, safe driving principles, rules of the road, and drugs and alcohol [A02].
Format: given on computerized testing machines at Driver Service Centers, with Spanish, Korean and Japanese versions where computerized testing is offered, and oral tests by appointment for applicants who cannot read [A02].
Vision: a screening of at least 20/40 in each eye and in both eyes together, with or without corrective lenses, comes before any license or permit [A02].
Retake rule: after failing any knowledge test you must wait at least one day before trying again [A03].
Question count, passing score and time limit: the manual states none of them, so all three stay unknown and are never shown to learners (verify TN-ALC-A-EXAM).
This section feeds the knowledge test: this chapter is the drugs and alcohol area of the knowledge test, which the exam page lists as one of four roughly equal areas and which the chapter itself says must make up at least a quarter of the questions by law; it covers how alcohol is absorbed and cleared, BAC and what raises it, crash risk, effects on judgment, vision, reaction and alertness, everyday drugs, the DUI law, implied consent and No Refusal, DUI penalties, the under-twenty-one laws and prevention; its sample questions target drug versus alcohol penalties, how long three drinks take to clear, rising BAC, implied consent and the skills alcohol affects.
Practice quizzes follow the product mastery policy from the learning loop, which is not an official exam threshold.
## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: alcohol, other drugs and driving
Audience: every first-time Tennessee Class D or learner permit applicant, and especially applicants under twenty-one, whom special alcohol laws target.
Why it matters to a first-timer: the chapter states that drugs and alcohol questions make up a fixed share of the knowledge test [A01], and the DUI and implied consent rules [A03] and under-twenty-one laws [A05] carry license loss for new drivers.
Level: fact recall heavy with exact values (BAC levels, crash multipliers, suspension lengths, fines) plus a few cause-and-effect ideas such as body fat and drinking rate; high load.
Prerequisites: none beyond the earlier license chapters; the graduated license chapter's restrictions on young drivers give useful context for the under-twenty-one rules.
