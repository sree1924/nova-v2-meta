## A — Exam authority for the driving conditions: reduced visibility, severe weather, winter and rural farm driving section (Wisconsin Class D regular driver license knowledge test)
Issuer: the Wisconsin Department of Transportation and its Division of Motor Vehicles (DMV), which offers the Motorists Handbook as a resource that helps train and qualify new drivers [A03].
Source document: Wisconsin Motorists Handbook, with 2026 printed on the front cover [A01]; the back cover carries form number BDS126 with 2026 [A04].
Knowledge test: 50 questions on traffic laws, and 40 correct answers (80 percent) are needed to pass [A02].
Highway signs test: a separate test of 15 questions, with 12 correct answers (80 percent) needed to pass [A02].
Vision screening: regular car and motorcycle applicants need visual acuity of at least 20/40 in one eye, corrected or not, and a 70-degree field of vision from the center of each eye [A02].
Duration: the handbook says the knowledge test takes about 45 minutes to finish, but it states no formal time limit [A02].
Time limit: unknown, so no limit is ever shown to learners (verify WI-CONDITIONS-A-TIME).
Delivery: the signs and knowledge tests need no appointment and are free at a DMV customer service center; applicants aged 15 to 17 can also sit the knowledge test online for a $10 fee [A02].
This section feeds the knowledge test: bald tires, visibility effects, icy bridges, hydroplaning and skid recovery, snowplow distance and livestock and farm machinery rules are safety and traffic-law content for the knowledge test.

Misconceptions: slower-looking traffic in fog may actually be moving faster than it seems; a bridge can be icy even when the road leading to it is not; hydroplaning is not only a high-speed problem when tires are bald; braking hard in a skid makes things worse, so stay off the brake; winter driving means headlights on but brights off; honking at livestock may scare them.
Mnemonics: bridges freeze first; in a slide, ease off, keep straight, wait for grip; in a skid, look and steer where you want to go, then steer back as it straightens; clear every window, light, mirror and plate before you roll.
Glossary: bald tires means heavily worn tires with little tread; traction means the grip between tires and road; hydroplaning means tires sliding on a layer of water; skid means a loss of control as the tires lose grip; ABS means an anti-lock braking system; cruise control means a setting that holds a steady speed automatically; livestock means farm animals such as cattle.
