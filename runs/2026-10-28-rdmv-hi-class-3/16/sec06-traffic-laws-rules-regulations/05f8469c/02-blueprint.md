Misconceptions: signaling is required even when no one is around and does not by itself give you the right to move; the end of a no passing zone means better visibility, not permission to pass; a left turn yields to oncoming traffic even on a green light; speeding up while being passed is unlawful; the railroad stop is at least fifteen feet from the rail, not from the crossing sign; the slow-moving vehicle emblem marks vehicles built for twenty-five miles per hour or less, not any slow driver.

Mnemonics: One hundred feet of signal, one hundred feet of no-left-half and no-passing at intersections, crossings, bridges and tunnels; fifteen feet back from the rail; twelve inches to the curb, ten feet of street left free, twenty-four hours is the parking limit; three in the front seat is the most.

Glossary: basic speed law (never faster than reasonable and prudent for conditions); protected route (the road whose traffic has the right of way at an intersection); double parking (stopping on the roadway side of a car parked at the curb); tow-away zone (a signed area where even a momentary stop brings a tow); slow-moving vehicle emblem (the reflective orange triangle on vehicles built for twenty-five miles per hour or less); access aisle (the striped space beside an accessible parking stall).
