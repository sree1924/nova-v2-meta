## A — Exam authority for the highway driving section (Missouri Class F operator license written test)
Issuer: the Missouri Department of Revenue publishes the Missouri Driver Guide [A01].
Source document: Missouri Driver Guide, with the front cover reading Revised August 2026 [A02].
Written test: 25 multiple-choice questions, all drawn from information in the guide [A03].
Passing standard: 20 of the 25 questions must be answered correctly, which is 80 percent [A03].
The driver examination has four parts (written, vision, road sign and driving skills) and is given by the Missouri State Highway Patrol; the Class F written test is not open book and leaves out the commercial vehicle chapter [A04].
Time limit: the guide does not state one, so it stays unknown and is never shown to learners (verify MO-HWY-A-TIME).
This section feeds the written test: lane choice on two-lane and multi-lane highways, speed control, passing, blind spots and being passed, keeping pace with traffic, where to stop, entrance and exit ramps, interchanges, highway hypnosis, drowsy driving and breakdown emergencies.
Practice quizzes follow the product mastery policy from the learning loop, which is not an official exam threshold.
## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: highway driving
Audience: first-time Missouri Class F applicants, from teens on an instruction permit to adult newcomers, preparing for multi-lane and controlled access highway driving.
Why it matters to a first-timer: keep-right and passing rules [A01] and the duty to yield when merging from an acceleration lane [A03] are common written-test items and real merge-crash causes.
Level: rule application with spatial sequences for passing and merging; moderate load.
Prerequisites: the rules-of-the-road chapter on passing and signaling, and the sharing-the-road chapter on trucks.

Misconceptions: the left lane of a multi-lane highway is not a cruising lane but a passing lane; you do not speed up when someone passes you; a missed exit is never fixed by backing up; driving far below the flow of traffic is not automatically safer.
Mnemonics: mirror, shoulder, signal before every lane change; see the passed car in the mirror before you move back.
Glossary: blind spot means an area around a vehicle that its mirrors do not show; controlled access highway means a road entered and left only by ramps; acceleration lane means the lane at the end of an entrance ramp for building up speed; deceleration lane means the lane before an exit ramp for slowing down; highway hypnosis means drowsy inattention caused by monotonous highway driving.
Dynamic facts: the emergency cellphone number and the CB channel or phone numbers posted along roads are set by authorities and can change, so the posted signs and current Highway Patrol guidance control.
