## A — Exam authority for the graduated licensing and restrictions section (North Carolina regular Class C)
Issuer: the North Carolina Department of Transportation, Division of Motor Vehicles (NCDMV) publishes the handbook and administers the license tests [A01].
Source document: North Carolina Driver Handbook, back cover revision note Revised May 2025 [A03].
Test structure for an original license: vision check, traffic signs test, knowledge test on traffic laws and safe driving, and an on-road test, each passed separately [A02].
This section feeds the knowledge test: graduated licensing levels, provisional licensee rules, license restrictions and first-time document requirements.
Not stated in the handbook: the knowledge-test question count, passing score and time limit; they stay unknown and are never shown to learners (verify NC-GDL-A-EXAM).

## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: graduated licensing and restrictions
Audience: first-time North Carolina applicants, including teens entering graduated licensing and the parents who supervise them, studying for the regular Class C knowledge test in English.
Teen entry route: the Level One permit starts at age 15 with a completed driver education course [A01].
Level: plain-language rule recall with many numeric limits (ages, hours, months, passenger counts), so learners need repetition and scenario practice.
Prerequisite: the licensing and testing section, which explains the vision, signs, knowledge and road tests these levels require.
Adult applicants still need the provisional rules for the knowledge test, because every driver under 18 is a provisional licensee [A02].

Misconceptions: Level One night limits end after six months but supervision does not; Level Two still requires supervision outside the unsupervised hours; any amount of alcohol counts for a provisional licensee, not just a legal limit; the Full Provisional License removes hour and passenger limits but not the under-age cell phone ban.
Mnemonics: the three levels run Permit, Provisional, Full — learn with an adult, drive alone by day, then drive freely; the phone rule is No Phone except an Emergency call.
Glossary: provisional licensee means any driver younger than eighteen; supervising driver means a parent, grandparent, guardian or approved adult with a long-held license; driving log means the signed record of supervised practice hours; restriction means a limit printed on a license.
Dynamic facts: the federal-identification document date and online upgrade availability are policy details that can change and are isolated as dynamic; confirm on the DMV website.
