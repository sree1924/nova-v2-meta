## A — Exam authority for the progression of licenses section (Wisconsin Class D regular driver license knowledge test)
Issuer: the Wisconsin Department of Transportation and its Division of Motor Vehicles (DMV), which offers the Motorists Handbook as a resource that helps train and qualify new drivers [A03].
Source document: Wisconsin Motorists Handbook, with 2026 printed on the front cover [A01]; the back cover carries form number BDS126 with 2026 [A04].
Knowledge test: 50 questions on traffic laws, and 40 correct answers (80 percent) are needed to pass [A02].
Highway signs test: a separate test of 15 questions, with 12 correct answers (80 percent) needed to pass [A02].
Vision screening: regular car and motorcycle applicants need visual acuity of at least 20/40 in one eye, corrected or not, and a 70-degree field of vision from the center of each eye [A02].
Duration: the handbook says the knowledge test takes about 45 minutes to finish, but it states no formal time limit [A02].
Time limit: unknown, so no limit is ever shown to learners (verify WI-PROG-A-TIME).
Delivery: the signs and knowledge tests need no appointment and are free at a DMV customer service center; applicants aged 15 to 17 can also sit the knowledge test online for a $10 fee [A02].
This section feeds the knowledge test: the instruction permit requires passing the knowledge test, highway signs test and vision screening, so this chapter explains when the knowledge test fits in the license ladder, along with permit companion rules, supervised hours, GDL limits, renewal and transfers that are themselves common test topics.
Practice quizzes follow the product mastery policy from the learning loop, which is not an official exam threshold.
## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: progression of licenses
Audience: first-time Wisconsin Class D applicants, especially teens under eighteen and their sponsors, plus adults applying for a first permit and new residents transferring a license.
Why it matters to a first-timer: the chapter lists what to bring and who may sponsor a minor [A01], what the permit requires and who must ride along [A03], and the practice hours and GDL limits that follow [A04].
Level: rule recall with many age, hour and day-count thresholds that are easy to confuse; moderate load.
Prerequisites: the driving test requirements section, which describes the knowledge test, signs test and vision screening that the permit depends on.

Misconceptions: a sponsor is not just a signature, because the sponsor takes legal and financial responsibility and can withdraw at any time; the nighttime permit companion must be an instructor or an older licensed adult, not any adult; the GDL limits end at eighteen even if nine months have not passed, and they can be extended, not just completed; a REAL ID from another state does not transfer automatically.
Mnemonics: forty plus ten makes fifty supervised hours; the GDL night window runs from midnight to five in the morning; transfers are sixty days for a regular license and thirty for a CDL; renew every eight years.
Glossary: sponsor means the adult who signs a minor's application and accepts liability; habitually truant means repeatedly absent from school without excuse; instruction permit means the learner permit for supervised practice; probationary license means the first full license, carrying GDL limits for drivers under eighteen; GDL means graduated driver license; out-of-state transfer means a new resident licensed in another state; REAL ID means a federally compliant card marked with a star.
Dynamic facts: the document list, fees, the REAL ID enforcement date, renewal channels, the RoadReady app and the DMV web pages for sponsors and new residents can change; confirm them on the Wisconsin DMV website.
