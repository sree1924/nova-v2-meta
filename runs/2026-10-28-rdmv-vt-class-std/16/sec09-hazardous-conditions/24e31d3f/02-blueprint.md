Misconceptions:
- Pumping the brakes helps in every emergency stop: false, pumping disengages anti-lock brakes; hold steady pressure and let the pedal pulse.
- Braking is the first step in a skid: false, braking makes a skid worse; steer where you want to go.
- Four wheel drive stops and corners well on ice: false, it helps in snow but not on ice, and every vehicle struggles to stop and turn on winter roads.
- Daytime running lights satisfy the night headlight law: false, you must switch on the headlights yourself from a half hour after sunset.
- Shallow moving water is safe to walk or drive through: false, four inches can knock a walker down and a motor stalls in water; stay out and abandon a stalled car.

Mnemonics:
- Ten minutes an hour, window cracked, exhaust clear: the stranded engine routine.
- Steady, not pumped: anti-lock braking in two words.
- Half hour after, half hour before: the headlight clock around sunset and sunrise.
- Five hundred feet or lights on: the visibility headlight rule.

Glossary:
- Black ice: a thin, nearly invisible ice layer that forms quickly when sleet, freezing rain or snow begin, first on bridges, ramps and overpasses
- Anti-lock brakes: a braking system that lets you brake and steer at once, used with steady pressure and signaled by a pulsing pedal
- Overdriving your headlights: driving so fast at night that you cannot stop within the distance your headlights show
- Daytime running lights: automatic low intensity lights for daytime visibility that are not a substitute for headlights
- Traveler's advisory: a weather warning during which driving should be avoided unless absolutely necessary
