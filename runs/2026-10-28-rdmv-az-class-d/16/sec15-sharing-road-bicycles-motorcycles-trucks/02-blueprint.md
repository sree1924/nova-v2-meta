## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: sharing the road with bicyclists, motorcycles, trucks and slow-moving vehicles
Audience: first-time Arizona drivers and permit holders preparing for the written test in English, who will share the road with bicyclists, motorcycles, trucks and farm vehicles.
Why it matters to a first-timer: drivers must give bicyclists a minimum passing distance [A01], motorcycles are easy to misjudge and may legally lane filter under set limits [A02], and trucks and slow-moving vehicles need extra room and patience [A03].
Level: practical recall of several look-alike numbers (passing gap, headlamp range, filtering speeds, slow-vehicle speed) and of where truck blind spots lie.
Prerequisites: the light rail and turning pages that precede this section.

Misconceptions: a bicyclist moving left is not necessarily breaking the law, since riders may move left for several legal reasons; a motorcycle that looks far away and slow may be closer and faster than it seems; a motorcycle's blinking signal does not prove the rider is turning; a farm vehicle pulling left may actually be about to turn right; lane filtering is for motorcyclists only and only under its three conditions.
Mnemonics: Bikes get a three-foot cushion; trucks need double the stopping room; motorcycles filter only slowly, on slow multi-lane streets.
Glossary: lane filtering means a motorcycle passing stopped vehicles in its own lane under strict limits; No-Zone means the large blind spots around trucks and buses; slow-moving vehicle symbol means the rear emblem marking a vehicle that cannot exceed a low top speed; practicable means as far as conditions safely allow.
Dynamic facts: the lane filtering conditions, the bicycle passing distance and the bicycle equipment rules are statutory and can be amended.
