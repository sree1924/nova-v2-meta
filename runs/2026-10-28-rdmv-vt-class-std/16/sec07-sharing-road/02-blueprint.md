Misconceptions:
- If a car ahead stops in a two lane road, you may pass it in the other lane: false, never pass a vehicle stopped for a pedestrian, even with two lanes.
- A school bus with yellow flashing lights means stop immediately: false, yellow means prepare to stop; the stop is required once the red lights flash.
- You may pass a stopped school bus from the opposite direction on any road: false, the exception applies only on a divided highway or where a concrete barrier separates traffic from the bus.
- Bicyclists must stay at the far right edge at all times: false, they may ride as far left as safety needs and may take the lane.
- A right turn on red is allowed on a red arrow when clear: false, never turn right on a red arrow.

Mnemonics:
- Fifteen feet, three reasons: stop at least fifteen feet from the rail for signals, a seen or heard train, or a stop sign.
- Yellow gets ready, red gets stopped: the two school bus light phases.
- Four feet for a bike: the minimum passing clearance.
- Walk left, ride right: pedestrians face traffic, bicycles go with it.

Glossary:
- Grade crossing: a place where a road crosses railroad tracks at the same level
- Divided highway: a road whose opposing directions are separated by a median or barrier, where a school bus on the far side does not require a stop
- Taking the lane: a bicyclist riding toward the center of the lane for safety or lane positioning
- White cane rule: the duty to stop and yield to a pedestrian with a white cane or guide dog who is crossing or waiting to cross
- Low shoulder: a road edge lower than the pavement, recovered from by slowing without braking and steering back gently
