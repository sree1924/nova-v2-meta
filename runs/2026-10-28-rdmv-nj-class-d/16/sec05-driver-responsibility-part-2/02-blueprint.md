Misconceptions: a missed turn is never fixed by backing up; front-wheel or rear-wheel drive does not change how to brake; a backup camera may be used on the road test but the basic reversing principles still come first; the right-turn hand signal is the arm up, not out.
Mnemonics: hands at nine and three; downhill means wheels toward the curb, uphill means wheels away from it; signal a hundred feet ahead; back up at walking speed.
Glossary: hand-over-hand steering means turning by crossing one hand over the other while both stay on the wheel; controlled slipping means letting the wheel slide through the fingers to straighten after a turn; reaction distance and braking distance mean the two parts of the manual stopping table that add up to the total stopping distance; K-turn means the three-point turn used to reverse direction on a street.
Dynamic facts: road test equipment rules, such as the allowance for backup cameras and parking sensors, can change; confirm them on the MVC website before a road test.
