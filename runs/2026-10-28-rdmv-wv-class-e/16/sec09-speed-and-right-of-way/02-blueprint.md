## C — Speed, slow driving and signaling

FACT_ID: sec09-F02 | anchor: A01
You should slow down when approaching and crossing an intersection, approaching and going around a curve, approaching the top of a hill, traveling on a narrow road, and whenever traffic, weather, roadway hazards or other conditions limit visibility or stopping distance [A01].
Source: [A01 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0052]

FACT_ID: sec09-F03 | anchor: A01
Your speed determines how much time you have to react, and the higher the speed the less time there is to spot hazards, judge the speed of other vehicles and act to avoid your own mistakes and those of other drivers [A01].
Source: [A01 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0052]

FACT_ID: sec09-F04 | anchor: A01
Most speed limits are posted, but West Virginia law also prohibits driving at any speed greater than is reasonable and prudent for existing conditions, and the law sets designated speeds for highways and for areas such as school zones and business and residential districts [A01].
Source: [A01 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0052]

FACT_ID: sec09-F05 | anchor: A01
Unless otherwise posted or unreasonable for conditions, the designated limits are seventy miles per hour on an interstate, fifty-five miles per hour on an open county highway, twenty-five miles per hour in business or residential areas, and fifteen miles per hour in a school zone while children are present along the roadway [A01].
Source: [A01 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0052]

FACT_ID: sec09-F06 | anchor: A01
A school zone is all school property including the school grounds and any street or highway abutting the grounds for one hundred twenty-five feet along the street or highway from the grounds [A01].
Source: [A01 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0052]

FACT_ID: sec09-F07 | anchor: A01
Authorized emergency vehicles may exceed posted speed limits while on emergency duty with their emergency signal equipment operating, as long as other road users are not endangered [A01].
Source: [A01 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0052]

FACT_ID: sec09-F08 | anchor: A01
The simple safety rule for speed is to observe and follow the limits and always drive at a speed that is reasonable and prudent for the existing conditions [A01].
Source: [A01 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0052]

FACT_ID: sec09-F09 | anchor: A02
When you drive at less than the normal speed of traffic you should drive as close as possible to the right edge of the road [A02].
Source: [A02 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0053]

FACT_ID: sec09-F10 | anchor: A02
If you must drive slower than the designated speed on a road with one lane in each direction where overtaking traffic has no clear lane for passing, pull off at the first safe turnout to let faster traffic pass [A02].

FACT_ID: sec09-F11 | anchor: A02
Other drivers expect you to keep traveling in the same direction, so your turn signals tell them you intend to turn or change lanes, and you should give them time to react to your moves [A02].
Source: [A02 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0053]

FACT_ID: sec09-F12 | anchor: A02
Use your turn signal before you change lanes, turn at an intersection, enter or leave an interstate, pull away from the curb, or pull over to the side of the road [A02].
Source: [A02 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0053]

FACT_ID: sec09-F13 | anchor: A02
Signal every time you make a change and at least one hundred feet before you plan to turn, then switch the signal off after completing the turn, because driving with a signal on without turning creates confusion [A02].
Source: [A02 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0053]

FACT_ID: sec09-F14 | anchor: A02
When you plan to turn just beyond an intersection, signaling too soon can make other drivers think you are turning at the intersection and pull into your path, while signaling too late leaves drivers behind too little time to react, so judge the traffic conditions [A02].
Source: [A02 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0053]

FACT_ID: sec09-F15 | anchor: A02
The hand signals are an arm pointing down for slow or stop, an arm pointing up for a right turn, and an arm held straight out for a left turn [A02].
Source: [A02 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0053]

FACT_ID: sec09-F16 | anchor: A02
Keep both hands on the steering wheel, with the left hand at about the eight o'clock position and the right hand at about four o'clock, which gives maximum control and reduces the chance that a deploying air bag knocks your hands off the wheel [A02].
