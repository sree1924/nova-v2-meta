## A — Exam authority for the state laws of interest to drivers and golf cart rules section (South Carolina Class D knowledge test)
Issuer: the South Carolina Department of Motor Vehicles (SCDMV) publishes the South Carolina Driver's Manual; the front cover names the agency and the manual [A01].
Source document: the front cover carries the edition line Revised 06/2024 [A01].
Required tests: an original license or an upgrade to a different class needs vision, knowledge and skills tests, and vision and knowledge tests can be taken at any SCDMV branch [A02].
Every first license test includes vision, knowledge and skills tests except the moped license, which has no skills test [A03].
Knowledge test content: traffic laws, road signs and driving safety rules [A04].
Test conduct: manuals, testing aids, written material and electronic devices are banned, and leaving the test area early or talking to anyone fails the test [A02].
Question count, passing score and time limit: the manual states none of them, so all three stay unknown and are never shown to learners (verify SC-LAWS-A-COUNT, SC-LAWS-A-PASS, SC-LAWS-A-TIME).
Edition freshness: the issuer page links this Revised 06/2024 file, but a possibly newer unlinked upload exists on the issuer site and was not compared (verify SC-LAWS-A-FRESH).
This section feeds the knowledge test: this short section supplies law items that can appear as knowledge questions: Selective Service registration for young male applicants, organ donor registration and the red heart, littering penalties, and the golf cart age, license, distance and road-speed limits.

Male US citizens and immigrants younger than 26 must complete form 447-SEL, the Supplement for SC Credential Selective Service Requirements, when applying for a permit, license or ID card. [A01]
Why it matters: the form is part of the application packet for most young male applicants.

FACT_ID: sec03-F03 | anchor: A01
When such an applicant turns 18, the SCDMV must send his name, address, date of birth, sex, Social Security number and application date to the US Selective Service, under SC Code §56-1-125. [A01]
Why it matters: this is an automatic data transfer tied to the credential.
Source: [A01 -> pdf-0029]

FACT_ID: sec03-F04 | anchor: A01
That Selective Service transfer does not happen if the applicant physically surrenders the permit, license or ID card before turning 18. [A01]
Why it matters: surrender before the birthday is the only exception the manual names.
Source: [A01 -> pdf-0029]

FACT_ID: sec03-F05 | anchor: A01
You can register as an organ and tissue donor when you apply for or renew a license, beginner's permit or ID card. [A01]
Why it matters: the choice is offered at every issuance and renewal.
Source: [A01 -> pdf-0029]

FACT_ID: sec03-F06 | anchor: A01
Registering adds your identity information to the SC Donor Registry and puts a red heart on your license, permit or ID card. [A01]
Why it matters: the red heart is the visible donor marker an officer or medic would see.
Source: [A01 -> pdf-0029]

FACT_ID: sec03-F07 | anchor: A01
Choosing to be an organ or tissue donor is a legally binding agreement under the SC Uniform Anatomical Gift Act. [A01]
Why it matters: the decision carries legal weight, not just a preference.
Source: [A01 -> pdf-0029]

FACT_ID: sec03-F08 | anchor: A01
If you register as a donor before turning 18, your parent or legal guardian makes the final decision about donation. [A01]
Why it matters: minors' donor choices are not final on their own.
Source: [A01 -> pdf-0029]

FACT_ID: sec03-F09 | anchor: A02
To change or update your donor decision, you use the Donate Life SC website, under SC Code §44-43-320. [A02]
Why it matters: the change goes through the registry website rather than a branch.
Source: [A02 -> pdf-0030]

FACT_ID: sec03-F10 | anchor: A02
Throwing trash along streets and highways is illegal, and offenders can be arrested and fined. [A02]
Why it matters: littering is a criminal matter under SC Code §16-11-700, not just a courtesy issue.
Source: [A02 -> pdf-0030]

FACT_ID: sec03-F11 | anchor: A02
A court may also order a litter offender to pick up roadside litter under court supervision, on top of a fine. [A02]
Why it matters: the penalty can include supervised cleanup work.
Source: [A02 -> pdf-0030]

FACT_ID: sec03-F12 | anchor: A02
Cigarettes and cigarette butts count as litter and should stay inside the vehicle until they can be thrown away properly. [A02]
Why it matters: flicking a butt out the window is littering.
Source: [A02 -> pdf-0030]

FACT_ID: sec03-F13 | anchor: A02
The manual suggests keeping a litter container in the vehicle and emptying it into a trash can once it is full. [A02]
Why it matters: a simple habit that prevents roadside litter.
Source: [A02 -> pdf-0030]

FACT_ID: sec03-F14 | anchor: A02
Driving a golf cart requires being at least 16 with a valid driver's license, and the cart must carry an SCDMV-issued permit or registration decal. [A02]
Why it matters: golf carts are licensed vehicles on public roads in SC.
Source: [A02 -> pdf-0030]

FACT_ID: sec03-F15 | anchor: A02
Daytime golf cart driving is allowed within four miles of the address on the cart's registration certificate. [A02]
Why it matters: the distance is measured from the registered address.
Source: [A02 -> pdf-0030]

FACT_ID: sec03-F16 | anchor: A03
A golf cart may also be driven by day within four miles of a gated community's gate, but only on secondary streets and highways posted at 35 mph or less, under SC Code §56-2-105. [A03]
Why it matters: faster roads are off limits to golf carts.
Source: [A03 -> pdf-0031]

FACT_ID: sec03-F17 | anchor: A03

Why it matters: CDL rules are outside this manual.
Source: [A03 -> pdf-0031]

FACT_ID: sec03-F18 | anchor: A03
Mopeds and motorcycles are covered in the SCDMV Motorcycle & Moped Operator's Manual, and large non-commercial and recreational vehicles in the Large Non-Commercial & Recreational Vehicles Driver's Manual. [A03]
Why it matters: these vehicle types have their own SCDMV manuals.
Source: [A03 -> pdf-0031]

Misconceptions: the Selective Service step applies to young male immigrants as well as citizens; a donor registration made as a minor does not have the final say; golf carts are not free to use on any road, only on slower secondary roads near home or a gated community and only by day; cigarette butts are litter like any other trash.
Mnemonics: golf cart rule of four and thirty-five, meaning within four miles and on roads posted at thirty-five or less; the red heart marks a donor.
Glossary: Selective Service means the federal registration system the SCDMV reports young men to; SC Donor Registry means the state list of registered organ and tissue donors; Uniform Anatomical Gift Act means the law that makes a donor choice binding; secondary street means a lower-speed road as opposed to a primary highway.
Dynamic facts: the donor registry website, form numbers and the referenced manual titles can change; confirm them on the SCDMV and Donate Life SC websites.
