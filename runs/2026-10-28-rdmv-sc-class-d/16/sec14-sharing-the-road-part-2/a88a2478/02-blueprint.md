Misconceptions: never cut in front of a large vehicle, because it needs far longer to stop than a car; being close behind a truck does not make you visible to its driver; a truck swinging left may be about to turn right; refusing to sign a ticket is lawful in South Carolina but may lead to arrest elsewhere; a stopped emergency vehicle calls for moving over or slowing, not just passing at normal speed.
Mnemonics: about three hundred thirty-five feet for a loaded truck to stop from fifty-five, a rear no zone up to two hundred feet; if you cannot see the driver in the mirror, the driver cannot see you; at a stop, engine off, flashers on, hands on the wheel; orange and red triangle, under twenty-five.
Glossary: front no zone means the area just ahead of a large vehicle, which it needs to stop; rear no zone means the large blind spot directly behind a large vehicle; weigh station means a roadside truck site from which large vehicles re-enter faster traffic; probable cause means the grounds that let an officer who believes a crime was committed search a vehicle without a warrant; move-over law means the rule to change lanes away from, or slow for, a stopped emergency vehicle; slow-moving vehicle triangle means the orange and red triangle on vehicles traveling under twenty-five miles per hour.
