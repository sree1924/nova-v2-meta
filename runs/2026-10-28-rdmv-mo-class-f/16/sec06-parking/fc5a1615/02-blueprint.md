## A — Exam authority for the parking section (Missouri Class F operator license written test)
Issuer: the Missouri Department of Revenue publishes the Missouri Driver Guide [A01].
Source document: Missouri Driver Guide, with the front cover reading Revised August 2026 [A02].
Written test: 25 multiple-choice questions, all drawn from information in the guide [A03].
Passing standard: 20 of the 25 questions must be answered correctly, which is 80 percent [A03].
The driver examination has four parts (written, vision, road sign and driving skills) and is given by the Missouri State Highway Patrol; the Class F written test is not open book and leaves out the commercial vehicle chapter [A04].
Time limit: the guide does not state one, so it stays unknown and is never shown to learners (verify MO-PARK-A-TIME).
This section feeds the written test: safe placement and securing of a parked vehicle, exiting on the curb side, the list of no-parking zones, who may use a handicapped space and the fine for misuse, the hill parking checklist and the parallel parking steps.
Practice quizzes follow the product mastery policy from the learning loop, which is not an official exam threshold.
## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: parking
Audience: first-time Missouri Class F applicants, from teens on an instruction permit to adult newcomers, who must know the no-parking list and the parking skills checked on the road test.
Why it matters to a first-timer: the no-parking zones and disability parking conditions are plain recall items [A01], and the hill and parallel parking steps carry over to the driving skills test [A02].
Level: short list recall plus one ordered procedure; low to moderate load.
Prerequisites: the sharing-the-road chapter for watching cyclists near parked cars.

Misconceptions: a handicapped placard does not let anyone park in a reserved space when the person it belongs to is not being carried; the striped aisle next to a reserved space is not extra parking; you may park on either side of a one-way street but not on the left side of a two-way street.
Mnemonics: brake, gear, keys, lock before you walk away; for parallel parking think right, left, right as you back in and straighten.
Glossary: International Symbol of Access means the wheelchair symbol marking reserved parking; access aisle means the striped space beside a reserved space used for getting in and out; parallel parking means parking in line with the curb between two vehicles.
Dynamic facts: the handicapped parking fine range is statutory and can change, and cities may post their own parking limits and curb colors that must be checked locally.
