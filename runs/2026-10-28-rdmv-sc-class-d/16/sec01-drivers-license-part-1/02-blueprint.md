## A — Exam authority for the license classes, the beginner's permit, the graduated license and required tests section (South Carolina Class D knowledge test)
Issuer: the South Carolina Department of Motor Vehicles (SCDMV) publishes the South Carolina Driver's Manual; the front cover names the agency and the manual [A01].
Source document: the front cover carries the edition line Revised 06/2024 [A01].
Required tests: an original license or an upgrade to a different class needs vision, knowledge and skills tests, and vision and knowledge tests can be taken at any SCDMV branch [A02].
Every first license test includes vision, knowledge and skills tests except the moped license, which has no skills test [A03].
Knowledge test content: traffic laws, road signs and driving safety rules [A04].
Test conduct: manuals, testing aids, written material and electronic devices are banned, and leaving the test area early or talking to anyone fails the test [A02].
Question count, passing score and time limit: the manual states none of them, so all three stay unknown and are never shown to learners (verify SC-LIC1-A-COUNT, SC-LIC1-A-PASS, SC-LIC1-A-TIME).
Edition freshness: the issuer page links this Revised 06/2024 file, but a possibly newer unlinked upload exists on the issuer site and was not compared (verify SC-LIC1-A-FRESH).
This section feeds the knowledge test: this is the licensing chapter itself: which class a first-time Class D applicant needs, the beginner's permit age, supervision and holding period, the teen graduated license stages and their curfews, and the vision, knowledge and skills test rules.
Practice quizzes follow the product mastery policy from the learning loop, which is not an official exam threshold.
## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: license classes, the beginner's permit, the graduated license and required tests
Audience: every first-time South Carolina applicant for a beginner's permit and a Class D license, with extra rules for applicants aged fifteen to seventeen.
Why it matters to a first-timer: this section says that a never-licensed driver must start with a beginner's permit and what that permit allows [A01], and which tests an original license requires and how the knowledge test is run [A02].
Level: procedural recall with many exact thresholds (weights, ages, hours, curfew times, holding periods); low conceptual load but high confusion risk between teen stages.
Prerequisites: none; this is the first content section of the manual.

Misconceptions: a Class D license covers mopeds and three-wheel vehicles but never a motorcycle with a sidecar; the permit holding period applies at every age, not only to teens; the waiver extends solo driving to midnight but never past it; an unbuckled seat belt fails the skills test outright; full privileges arrive automatically without a branch visit.
Mnemonics: forty and ten for teen practice, forty hours total with ten after dark; for the permit, hold it one hundred eighty days and apply from day one hundred eighty-one; the curfew ladder is alone until six in the evening (eight in daylight saving time), an adult of twenty-one until midnight, a listed supervisor after that.
Glossary: GVWR means the gross vehicle weight rating set for a vehicle; beginner's permit means the learner permit every never-licensed driver starts with; conditional license means the first teen stage from age fifteen and a half; special restricted license means the stage for sixteen-year-olds; PDLA means the school attendance, driver's education and practice certification; third-party tester means an SCDMV-authorized school that gives tests.
