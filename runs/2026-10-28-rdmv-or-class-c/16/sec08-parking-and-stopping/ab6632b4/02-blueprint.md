## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: parallel, angle and hill parking, disabled parking, no-parking places and emergency parking
Audience: every first-time Oregon Class C applicant; parking rules are everyday driving law and a common source of exact-distance knowledge-test items.
Why it matters to a first-timer: the hill wheel directions reverse between uphill and downhill [A02], the no-parking list bans stopping as well as parking [A03], and the fixed no-parking distances differ by object [A04].
Level: exact-number and list recall (curb distance, no-parking distances, warning-device distance) plus direction recall for hill parking; moderate load.
Prerequisites: the sharing-the-road sections, especially railroad crossings, crosswalks and fire hydrant awareness, and the signs section for parking signs and curb markings.

Misconceptions: a quick stop in a disabled space is still illegal and still fined; the no-parking list applies even if you stay in the car; uphill with a curb the wheels turn away from the curb, not toward it; the hydrant, crosswalk, signal and railroad distances are different numbers, not one shared distance; a breakdown lets you stop in a banned place only for a short time and only without creating a hazard.
Mnemonics: down to the curb, up to the lane, no curb to the edge; brake set and in park or gear on every hill; engine off, key out, brakes on, flashers on before you walk away.
Glossary: parallel parking means parking in line with the curb in the direction of traffic; angle parking means parking at a slant to the curb or aisle; double parking means stopping on the road side of a parked vehicle; Disabled Person Parking Permit means the Oregon permit that allows parking in disabled spaces; hazard lights means the flashers that warn other drivers of a stopped vehicle.
Dynamic facts: the disabled parking fine range is a money amount that can change with law, so it is shown as printed in the current manual and re-checked each edition.
