## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: space cushion, following distance, blind spots, signaling and passing
Audience: first-time Arizona drivers and permit holders preparing for the written test in English, who will soon practice lane changes and passing on real roads.
Why it matters to a first-timer: most rear-end crashes come from following too closely, which the counting rule is meant to prevent [A01], and the law sets a minimum signal distance before every turn [A03].
Level: practical recall of seconds and feet values plus ordered steps for lane changes and passing; a few numbers are easy to confuse.
Prerequisites: the scanning and steering pages of the safe driving practices chapter that precede this section.

Mnemonics: Count it out to the fixed point — finish the count before you get there; pass left, return when both headlights show in the mirror.
Glossary: space cushion means the open space kept around your vehicle on every side; blind spot means a side area your mirrors cannot show; tailgating means following another vehicle too closely; committing to a turn means visibly beginning the turn rather than only signaling it.
