Misconceptions:
- Below point zero eight you are safe to drive: false, impairment begins below that level and even one drink affects judgment.
- Coffee or a cold shower speeds up sobering: false, only time works, at least an hour per drink.
- Refusing the breath test avoids all penalties: false, refusal costs the license for at least six months under implied consent.
- Prescription medicine cannot count as impaired driving: false, driving after prescription or over the counter drugs can be as deadly as alcohol and is treated the same.
- A loud radio and open window keep a drowsy driver safe: false, they do not keep you awake; pull over and rest.

Mnemonics:
- Eight for all, two for the young: the point zero eight and point zero two thresholds.
- An hour a drink, nothing less: the only way alcohol leaves the body.
- Refuse and lose six: refusing the implied consent test costs six months.

Glossary:
- Blood alcohol concentration: the measured share of alcohol in the blood, legally impaired at point zero eight and, under twenty-one, a violation at point zero two
- Implied consent: the agreement every licensed driver makes to take an alcohol or drug test when an officer who suspects impairment asks
- Administrative suspension: a civil process that can take a license away before a criminal conviction
- Designated driver: a person who agrees not to drink or use drugs and takes responsibility for driving the others home
- Depressant: a drug such as alcohol that slows the thought process rather than lifting it
