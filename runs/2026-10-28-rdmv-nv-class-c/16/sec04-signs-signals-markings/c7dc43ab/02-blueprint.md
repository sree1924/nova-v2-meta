## A — Exam authority for the signs, signals, highway markings, railroad crossings and school zones section (Nevada Basic Class C driver license knowledge test)
Issuer: the Nevada Department of Motor Vehicles publishes the Nevada Driver's Handbook and says the knowledge test for a Nevada license draws on what the handbook contains [A01].
Source document: the Nevada Driver's Handbook whose front cover carries the form and edition line DMV 700 March 2024 [A02].
Edition freshness: this edition was already about two and a half years old when it was staged, so whether it is still the current one stays open until the DMV confirms it (verify NV-SIGN-A-FRESH).
Knowledge test format: it checks your grasp of highway signs and markings, traffic laws and safe driving practices, on automated testing equipment or on paper; an oral exam can be requested, and Basic Class C testing is also offered in Spanish [A03].
Vision: every applicant has vision checked against minimum standards, and needing glasses or contact lenses to drive puts a restriction on the license [A03].
Question count, passing standard and time limit: the handbook states none of them, so all three stay unknown and are never shown to learners (verify NV-SIGN-A-COUNT, NV-SIGN-A-PASS, NV-SIGN-A-TIME).
This section feeds the knowledge test: the knowledge test checks highway signs and markings directly, so this section carries the core traffic-control content: sign colors and shapes, stop and yield duties, every light and arrow signal, pedestrian signals, pavement lines, center turn lanes, railroad crossings and school zones.

Misconceptions: a flashing yellow arrow does not give you the right-of-way, oncoming traffic still goes first; a green arrow protects the turn but pedestrians in the crosswalk still have priority; a dark traffic light is a full stop, not a free pass; a left on red is legal only between two one-way streets; a yield sign does not require a full stop when the way is clear.
Mnemonics: octagon eight for stop, pentagon five for school; red stops, yellow warns, orange works, blue serves, brown plays; two hundred feet in, fifty feet out of the center lane; fifteen feet from the train, fifty feet to park.
Glossary: crossbuck means the X-shaped sign at the tracks themselves; stop line means the wide white line where you stop before the intersection; center turn lane means the shared middle lane used only for left turns; protected turn means a turn made on a green arrow with conflicting traffic held; regulatory sign means a sign stating a law you must obey.
Dynamic facts: posted school-zone hours, work-zone fine amounts and any locally signed exceptions vary by location; read the posted signs.
