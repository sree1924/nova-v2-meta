## A — Exam authority for the points, suspensions and restoration section (North Carolina regular Class C)
Issuer: the North Carolina Department of Transportation, Division of Motor Vehicles (NCDMV) publishes the handbook and administers the license tests [A01].
Source document: North Carolina Driver Handbook, back cover revision note Revised May 2025 [A03].
Test structure for an original license: vision check, traffic signs test, knowledge test on traffic laws and safe driving, and an on-road test, each passed separately [A02].
This section feeds the knowledge test: license point values and thresholds, point-system and offense-based suspensions, hearings and appeals, unpaid-fine revocations, provisional-licensee suspensions and restoration fees.
Not stated in the handbook: the knowledge-test question count, passing score and time limit; they stay unknown and are never shown to learners (verify NC-PTS-A-EXAM).
Practice quizzes follow the product mastery policy from the learning loop, which is not an official exam threshold.
## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: points, suspensions and restoration
Audience: first-time North Carolina applicants preparing for the regular Class C knowledge test, in English, including drivers under eighteen who face a separate provisional suspension ladder [A06].
Level: heavy numeric recall of point values, thresholds, suspension periods and fees; many near-identical numbers, so contrast drills matter more than concept teaching.
Prerequisites: the graduated licensing section (provisional licensee status) and the alcohol section (chemical-test refusal and DWI revocation, referenced by the offense table and the DWI restoration fee).
Sensitivity: suspension and fee content is legal material stated as printed; the dossier gives no legal advice about hearings, appeals or court motions.

Misconceptions: license points and insurance points are separate systems, so reinstatement clears only license points; a point-system suspension is not the only route to losing a license, because many single offenses carry their own suspension; paying or contesting a ticket does not erase the offense; commercial drivers carry higher point values for the same act.
Mnemonics: clinic, then suspension, then a lower bar after reinstatement; for under-eighteen drivers the count is second, third, fourth violation inside one year.
Glossary: points means values added to a driving record on conviction; suspension and revocation mean loss of the driving privilege for a period; restoration fee means the charge to clear a suspension; provisional licensee means a driver under eighteen; Motion for Relief means the court form asking to reduce or waive a fine.
Dynamic facts: fees, the hearing phone line and web payment options can change and are flagged as dynamic; confirm on the NCDMV and court websites.
