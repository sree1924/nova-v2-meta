Misconceptions: a driver behind a stopped school bus must always stop, even on a multi-lane highway, and only opposite-direction drivers on a multi-lane road may continue; a disability placard alone does not allow use of a disability space unless the named person is driving or riding; the shoulder is never a passing lane, even when paved; when being passed you keep a steady speed rather than speeding up; uphill beside a curb is the one hill case where the wheels turn away from the curb.
Mnemonics: downhill or uphill with no curb, wheels to the right; uphill with a curb, wheels to the street's center; eighteen inches is the farthest you may park from the curb; signal at least one hundred feet before you pass; pass until the whole front of the other car shows in your rearview mirror.
Glossary: multi-lane highway means, for the school bus rule, a road of four lanes with at least two in each direction; acceleration lane means the lane alongside the main roadway used to reach traffic speed; deceleration lane means the lane to the right of the roadway, before the ramp, used to slow down; shoulder means the hard surface on the right kept for emergency stops; placard means the disability parking permit displayed in a vehicle.
