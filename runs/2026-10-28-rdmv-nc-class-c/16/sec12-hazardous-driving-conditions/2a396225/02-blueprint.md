## A — Exam authority for the hazardous driving conditions section (North Carolina regular Class C)
Issuer: the North Carolina Department of Transportation, Division of Motor Vehicles (NCDMV) publishes the handbook and administers the license tests [A01].
Source document: North Carolina Driver Handbook, back cover revision note Revised May 2025 [A03].
Test structure for an original license: vision check, traffic signs test, knowledge test on traffic laws and safe driving, and an on-road test, each passed separately [A02].
This section feeds the knowledge test: work zone limits, signs and the posted speeding penalty, the headlight law and night driving, sun glare, rain, hydroplaning, flooded roads, the wipers-on headlights-on rule, fog, snow and ice, and wildlife collisions.
Not stated in the handbook: the knowledge-test question count, passing score and time limit; they stay unknown and are never shown to learners (verify NC-HAZ-A-EXAM).

Misconceptions: braking during a hydroplane makes the skid worse, so ease off the gas instead; driving slowly is not always safest in a work zone, since slower-than-traffic driving causes rear-end crashes; water over a road is often deeper than it looks; chains and snow tires do not make ice safe; fog and snowstorms call for low beams, not high beams; one deer usually means more are near.
Mnemonics: orange means work zone; wipers on, lights on; off the gas, hands straight, no brakes for a hydroplane; standing water on the road means find another way.
Glossary: work zone means the stretch between the first and last work zone signs; flagger means a vested worker directing traffic with a paddle; hydroplaning means tires riding on a film of water instead of the road; low beams means dipped headlights for city, fog and snow; eye shine means light reflected from an animal's eyes at the road edge.
Dynamic facts: work zone penalty amounts are posted on site and are not printed in the handbook, so they are not stated here.
