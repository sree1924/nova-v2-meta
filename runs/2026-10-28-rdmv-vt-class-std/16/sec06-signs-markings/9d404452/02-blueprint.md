FACT_ID: sec06-F27 | anchor: A04

Source: [A04 -> src:rdmv-vt-class-std:vermont-department-of-motor-vehicles-age:handbook:pdf-0037]

Misconceptions:
- A broken line anywhere in the center means you may pass: false, only a broken line on your own side permits passing; a solid line on your side forbids it.
- A two way left turn lane may be used to get ahead of traffic: false, it is not a travel lane and is entered only briefly, with the whole vehicle, just before a left turn.
- Work zone speed limits apply only when workers are visible: false, work zone rules apply around the clock and speeding fines there are doubled.
- A flagger's paddle is advisory: false, a flagger has legal authority to control traffic and must be obeyed.
- Changing lanes while completing a turn is acceptable if clear: false, it is illegal; change lanes only after the turn is complete.

Mnemonics:
- Yellow diamond warns, white rectangle rules, orange works: the three sign families by color and shape.
- Broken on my side, I may pass; solid on my side, I stay: the passing line rule.
- Yellow on the left or you are wrong: the lane check in temporary work zone markings.

Glossary:
- Crossbuck: the X shaped sign placed at every railroad crossing to mark where the tracks are
- Regulatory sign: a white rectangular sign stating what a driver must or must not do
- Warning sign: a yellow or green diamond shaped sign marking a permanent danger that calls for slowing down
- Two way left turn lane: a center lane shared by both directions for left turns only, never for through travel
- Yield line: a painted row of white triangles pointing at approaching traffic that marks where to stop when yielding
- Channeling devices: cones, drums and barricades that keep traffic out of work areas and guide it through a work zone
- Flashing arrow panel: a lighted sign that directs drivers into the correct lane or, in caution mode, shows a bar or four corner lights meaning slow down
