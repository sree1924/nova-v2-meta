## A — Exam authority for the towing, winter driving, skids, hydroplaning, vehicle failures and emergencies section (Minnesota Class D written test)
Issuer: the Minnesota Department of Public Safety, whose Driver and Vehicle Services division licenses drivers, permits the printing of the Minnesota Driver’s Manual [A01].
Source document: Minnesota Driver’s Manual, with the edition line June 2026 printed on the front cover [A02].
Written test format: multiple choice and true-or-false questions on Minnesota traffic laws and road signs, taken on paper or on a computer, with English, several other languages and American Sign Language at computer test stations [A03].
Passing standard: a score of 80 percent, and only one written test may be taken per day [A03].
Retest fee: after two consecutive failed written tests, a fee of $10 is charged for the third and every later attempt [A03].
Vision screening: normal or corrected vision of at least 20/40 and peripheral vision of at least 105 degrees [A03].
Question count: the manual does not state how many questions the written test has, so the count stays unknown and is never shown to learners (verify MN-DRIVEB-A-COUNT).
Time limit: the manual does not state one, so it stays unknown and is never shown to learners (verify MN-DRIVEB-A-TIME).
This section feeds the written test: towing and trailer size, lighting and brake limits, recreational vehicle combinations, winter driving, snowplows and winter survival, staying alert, skids and anti-lock brakes, rain, fog and hydroplaning, blowouts, steering and brake failure, deer and oncoming threats, stalls on rail tracks, floods and submersion, vehicle fires and overheating, light, wiper, hood and accelerator failures, power lines and carbon monoxide.

## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: towing, winter driving, skids, hydroplaning, vehicle failures and emergencies
Audience: first-time Minnesota Class D applicants preparing for winter roads, and drivers who will tow campers or trailers.
Why it matters to a first-timer: the trailer limits [A01], winter survival and fatigue rules [A03] and the rail stall and flood rules [A06] are life-safety items and appear on the written test.
Level: moderate; most items are ordered response steps where the trap is the instinctive wrong move (pumping ABS, leaving a stranded car, swerving back onto the pavement).
Prerequisites: the first half of the driving conditions chapter and the vehicle equipment chapter.
