## A — Exam authority for the sharing the road: pedestrians, bicyclists, motorcyclists and commercial vehicles section (South Carolina Class D knowledge test)
Issuer: the South Carolina Department of Motor Vehicles (SCDMV) publishes the South Carolina Driver's Manual; the front cover names the agency and the manual [A01].
Source document: the front cover carries the edition line Revised 06/2024 [A01].
Required tests: an original license or an upgrade to a different class needs vision, knowledge and skills tests, and vision and knowledge tests can be taken at any SCDMV branch [A02].
Every first license test includes vision, knowledge and skills tests except the moped license, which has no skills test [A03].
Knowledge test content: traffic laws, road signs and driving safety rules [A04].
Test conduct: manuals, testing aids, written material and electronic devices are banned, and leaving the test area early or talking to anyone fails the test [A02].
Question count, passing score and time limit: the manual states none of them, so all three stay unknown and are never shown to learners (verify SC-SHR-A-COUNT, SC-SHR-A-PASS, SC-SHR-A-TIME).
Edition freshness: the issuer page links this Revised 06/2024 file, but a possibly newer unlinked upload exists on the issuer site and was not compared (verify SC-SHR-A-FRESH).
This section feeds the knowledge test: this section is a core knowledge-test source: yielding to pedestrians in marked, unmarked and mid-block crosswalks, the full stop for a guide dog or white cane, school zone speed, bicyclists as vehicles and bike lane rules, the full lane and four-second gap for motorcycles, and commercial vehicle no zones.

## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: sharing the road: pedestrians, bicyclists, motorcyclists and commercial vehicles
Audience: every first-time South Carolina Class D applicant; pedestrian and bicycle duties apply from the first permit drive.
Why it matters to a first-timer: the duty to yield to pedestrians in any crosswalk and to stop fully for a guide dog or white cane is set out here [A01], along with giving a motorcycle a full lane [A05].
Level: mostly rule recall with scenario judgment about yielding and spacing, and one following-distance value.
Prerequisites: the right-of-way, crosswalk and space-management rules from the earlier sections.

Misconceptions: a green light does not end your duty to yield to pedestrians in the intersection; an unmarked crosswalk is still a crosswalk; a bicyclist is a vehicle with the same rights as a car; a flashing motorcycle signal may be left on by mistake; if you cannot see a truck driver's face in the mirror, the driver cannot see you.
Mnemonics: white cane or guide dog means a full stop; bikes are vehicles, motorcycles get the whole lane; four seconds behind a motorcycle, more when wet; no face in the mirror, no view of you.
Glossary: mid-block crosswalk means a crosswalk between intersections marked by signs and pavement markings; bike lane means a lane designated for bicycles; no zone means the blind-spot area around a large truck or bus; commercial vehicle means a generally larger, less maneuverable vehicle such as a large truck or bus.
