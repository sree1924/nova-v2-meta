## A — Exam authority for the Maryland MVA knowledge test

Issuer: the Maryland Department of Transportation Motor Vehicle Administration (MVA) sets the licensing requirements and gives the knowledge test [A07].
Publication: the manual's copyright page names the MDOT/MVA as the owner of the Maryland Driver's Manual [A02].
Edition: the copyright page prints the form line "DL-002 (10-26)" and the words "October 2026 Edition" [A02]; the front cover prints no edition, so this run's source liveness is unconfirmed and the current edition and effective date stay unconfirmed by the issuer (verify — sec04-A-EDITION).
License class: the manual's licensing chapter calls the noncommercial license for cars, pickup trucks and vans a Class C driver's license [A09].
Exam path: a first license requires passing a vision screening, a knowledge test and a driving skills test [A07].
Knowledge test: it covers motor vehicle laws, safe driving practices and traffic signs, is taken at an MVA location, and the manual says it holds all the information that is tested [A07].
Scoring: the manual's knowledge-test paragraph prints a question count and a time allowance, but while the source edition is unconfirmed neither figure is asserted here; the manual states no passing score or percent anywhere (verify — sec04-A-SCORING).
Question format: the manual does not describe the item format of the knowledge test (verify — sec04-A-FORMAT).
Practice: the MVA website hosts an Online Driver Test Tutorial with practice questions [A07]; practice mastery targets in this product are product policy from the accepted learning loop, never an official passing line.
Section focus: this section covers traffic signals for vehicles, including steady and flashing lights and arrows, and the crosswalk signals that control pedestrians. [A17]
Dynamic facts (updatable): MVA phone numbers, appointment booking, website addresses, office locations, accepted-document lists and fees can change between editions and are kept out of stable knowledge; why_dynamic: administrative details set by the MVA outside the printed rules of the road.
## B — Audience and scope

Audience: first-time Maryland applicants who have never held a driver's license and are preparing in English for the MVA knowledge test for a noncommercial Class C license; this includes teens entering the Rookie Driver graduated licensing path, adults licensing for the first time, and holders of out-of-country licenses who must still test.
Level: plain-English reading level; no prior driving vocabulary is assumed, so every term from the manual is defined where it first appears.
Prerequisites: right-of-way and turning rules from manual Section Three, which the signal rules build on.
Section scope: manual Section Four, part A, on traffic signals: vehicle signal colors and arrows, steady and flashing signals, signals that are dark or not working, lane-use and special signals, and crosswalk and pedestrian signals.
Out of scope: commercial and motorcycle licensing content, document-by-document identity paperwork beyond what the manual prints, and anything not printed on the selected manual pages.
## C — Traffic signals: obeying control devices, steady and flashing lights, arrows, lane-use signals and crosswalk signals

FACT_ID: sec04-F04 | anchor: A17

FACT_ID: sec04-F06 | anchor: A17

FACT_ID: sec04-F08 | anchor: A17

FACT_ID: sec04-F09 | anchor: A17
A steady yellow signal means the light is changing from green to red, giving you time to stop safely and letting vehicles already in the intersection clear it; if you are too close to stop safely, continue through carefully. [A17]
Source: [A17 -> src:rdmv-md-c:maryland-department-of-transportation-mo:handbook:pdf-0017]

FACT_ID: sec04-F10 | anchor: A17
On a steady green signal, first check that no vehicles are in the intersection, then go straight or turn with caution unless a sign or signal prohibits the turn, yielding to pedestrians and vehicles already there. [A17]

FACT_ID: sec04-F11 | anchor: A18
A steady red arrow means that if you want to go the way it points, you stop completely before the stop line, crosswalk or intersection and stay stopped while the arrow is red. [A18]
Source: [A18 -> src:rdmv-md-c:maryland-department-of-transportation-mo:handbook:pdf-0018]

FACT_ID: sec04-F12 | anchor: A18
A steady yellow arrow means permission to turn that way is ending, so slow down and proceed with caution. [A18]
Source: [A18 -> src:rdmv-md-c:maryland-department-of-transportation-mo:handbook:pdf-0018]

FACT_ID: sec04-F13 | anchor: A18
A steady green arrow lets you go with caution in the direction it points, but you must still yield to all pedestrians and vehicles already in the intersection. [A18]
Source: [A18 -> src:rdmv-md-c:maryland-department-of-transportation-mo:handbook:pdf-0018]

FACT_ID: sec04-F14 | anchor: A18
At a flashing red signal, stop completely at the stop line, or before the crosswalk and intersection, yield to all traffic and pedestrians, and go when the way is clear. [A18]
Source: [A18 -> src:rdmv-md-c:maryland-department-of-transportation-mo:handbook:pdf-0018]

FACT_ID: sec04-F15 | anchor: A18
At a railroad crossing with a flashing red signal, stop completely even if no train is visible, and go only after the flashing stops and the way is clear. [A18]
Why it matters: at a rail crossing the flashing red is not a stop-and-go; you wait until it stops flashing.
Source: [A18 -> src:rdmv-md-c:maryland-department-of-transportation-mo:handbook:pdf-0018]

FACT_ID: sec04-F16 | anchor: A18
A flashing yellow signal means you must slow down and proceed with caution. [A18]
Source: [A18 -> src:rdmv-md-c:maryland-department-of-transportation-mo:handbook:pdf-0018]

FACT_ID: sec04-F17 | anchor: A18
A flashing red arrow means stop completely before the stop line, crosswalk or intersection, yield to all traffic and pedestrians, then go in the arrow's direction when clear. [A18]
Source: [A18 -> src:rdmv-md-c:maryland-department-of-transportation-mo:handbook:pdf-0018]

FACT_ID: sec04-F18 | anchor: A18
Reversible lanes carry traffic one way at some hours and the other way at other hours depending on traffic volume, and lane use signals, signs and markings show which way you may go. [A18]
Source: [A18 -> src:rdmv-md-c:maryland-department-of-transportation-mo:handbook:pdf-0018]

FACT_ID: sec04-F19 | anchor: A18
Never drive in a lane under a red X signal, but you may drive in a lane under a green arrow signal. [A18]
Source: [A18 -> src:rdmv-md-c:maryland-department-of-transportation-mo:handbook:pdf-0018]

FACT_ID: sec04-F20 | anchor: A18
A steady yellow X over a lane means leave that lane as soon as you safely can. [A18]
Source: [A18 -> src:rdmv-md-c:maryland-department-of-transportation-mo:handbook:pdf-0018]

FACT_ID: sec04-F21 | anchor: A18
A flashing yellow X or two-way left-turn arrows mean the lane is for left turns, and you will probably share it with left-turning drivers coming the other way. [A18]
Source: [A18 -> src:rdmv-md-c:maryland-department-of-transportation-mo:handbook:pdf-0018]

FACT_ID: sec04-F22 | anchor: A19
A crosswalk signal halts traffic so pedestrians can cross safely; a pedestrian activates it with a push button on a roadside pole, and it signals drivers to stop before telling pedestrians to cross. [A19]
Source: [A19 -> src:rdmv-md-c:maryland-department-of-transportation-mo:handbook:pdf-0019]

FACT_ID: sec04-F23 | anchor: A19
For drivers at a crosswalk signal, flashing yellow means slow down, solid yellow means get ready to stop, solid red means stop, and flashing red means stop and then proceed with caution. [A19]
Source: [A19 -> src:rdmv-md-c:maryland-department-of-transportation-mo:handbook:pdf-0019]

Glossary: traffic control device — a signal, sign or pavement marking that regulates, warns or guides traffic [A17]. Stop line — a wide white line across the road marking where to stop [A17]. Reversible lane — a lane whose direction changes by time of day [A18]. Lane use signal — an overhead signal showing whether a lane is open to you [A18].
Memory aid: red X means never, yellow X means exit, flashing yellow X means left turns only, green arrow means go [A18].
Misconception: a steady green light is not a signal to go without looking; you first check the intersection and still yield to anyone already in it [A17].
