## A — Exam authority for the getting familiar with your vehicle section (Tennessee Class D knowledge test)
Issuer: the Tennessee Department of Safety and Homeland Security, Driver Services Division, publishes the Tennessee Comprehensive Driver License Manual and takes written comments on it [A01].
Edition statement: the manual says its information was current as of July 1, 2022, and later law, rule or policy changes override anything outdated in it [A01].
Freshness: that content date is old and no issuer page confirms it as the current edition, so every fact here is held under freshness debt until rechecked (verify TN-VEH-A-FRESH).
Knowledge test: a multiple-choice exam drawn from Sections B and C of the manual, split roughly equally across traffic signs and signals, safe driving principles, rules of the road, and drugs and alcohol [A02].
Format: given on computerized testing machines at Driver Service Centers, with Spanish, Korean and Japanese versions where computerized testing is offered, and oral tests by appointment for applicants who cannot read [A02].
Vision: a screening of at least 20/40 in each eye and in both eyes together, with or without corrective lenses, comes before any license or permit [A02].
Retake rule: after failing any knowledge test you must wait at least one day before trying again [A03].
Question count, passing score and time limit: the manual states none of them, so all three stay unknown and are never shown to learners (verify TN-VEH-A-EXAM).
This section feeds the knowledge test: this chapter opens Section B, the first of the two sections the knowledge test is drawn from, and covers pre-drive checks, seat, head restraint and mirror setup, belts, starting the engine, transmissions, hand position and steering methods, backing and stopping, carbon monoxide, and the hands-free phone and in-car video rules; its sample questions target seat distance, hand position and head restraints.
Practice quizzes follow the product mastery policy from the learning loop, which is not an official exam threshold.
## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: getting familiar with your vehicle
Audience: every first-time Tennessee Class D or learner permit applicant, especially someone who has not yet driven, plus parents preparing a teen for supervised practice.
Why it matters to a first-timer: the manual names Sections B and C as the study material for the permit and license tests [A01], and this chapter sets the seat, mirror and hand-position habits the sample questions test [A02].
Level: practical recall with a few exact values (seat distance, clock positions, curb distance, clutch speed); low conceptual load but many look-alike steps.
Prerequisites: none beyond the licensing chapters; this is the first study chapter of Section B.

Mnemonics: nine and three for the hands; ten to twelve inches from the wheel; seat first, then mirrors; look where you want to go.
Glossary: counter-steering means reversing your hand movements to straighten out after a turn; riding the clutch means resting a foot on the clutch pedal when not shifting; steering wheel interlock means a lock that freezes the wheel when the ignition is turned to lock; push-pull means the steering method where each hand stays on its own side of the wheel.
