## A — Exam authority for the driving responsibilities section (Tennessee Class D knowledge test)
Issuer: the Tennessee Department of Safety and Homeland Security, Driver Services Division, publishes the Tennessee Comprehensive Driver License Manual and takes written comments on it [A01].
Edition statement: the manual says its information was current as of July 1, 2022, and later law, rule or policy changes override anything outdated in it [A01].
Freshness: that content date is old and no issuer page confirms it as the current edition, so every fact here is held under freshness debt until rechecked (verify TN-RESP-A-FRESH).
Knowledge test: a multiple-choice exam drawn from Sections B and C of the manual, split roughly equally across traffic signs and signals, safe driving principles, rules of the road, and drugs and alcohol [A02].
Format: given on computerized testing machines at Driver Service Centers, with Spanish, Korean and Japanese versions where computerized testing is offered, and oral tests by appointment for applicants who cannot read [A02].
Vision: a screening of at least 20/40 in each eye and in both eyes together, with or without corrective lenses, comes before any license or permit [A02].
Retake rule: after failing any knowledge test you must wait at least one day before trying again [A03].
Question count, passing score and time limit: the manual states none of them, so all three stay unknown and are never shown to learners (verify TN-RESP-A-EXAM).
This section feeds the knowledge test: this chapter sits in Section B of the manual, from which the knowledge test is drawn, and most closely matches the rules of the road area; it covers the Problem Driver Pointer System, the grounds for suspension and revocation, license surrender and hearings, address changes, the Non-Resident Violator Compact, reinstatement, the point system and its chart, restricted licenses, medical review and re-examination, financial responsibility, crash reporting and conduct at a crash scene; its sample questions target the crash report deadline, what to do when passing a crash you cannot help with, and the grounds for suspension.
Practice quizzes follow the product mastery policy from the learning loop, which is not an official exam threshold.
## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: driving responsibilities
Audience: every first-time Tennessee Class D or learner permit applicant, and especially drivers under eighteen, who face lower point thresholds and school-related license loss.
Why it matters to a first-timer: the grounds for losing a license [A01], the point thresholds [A02] and the crash reporting duties [A05] decide whether a new license survives its first mistakes.
Level: rule recall with many deadlines, point values and dollar thresholds, plus procedure recall for crash scenes; moderate to high load.
Prerequisites: the license and graduated license chapters, and the alcohol chapter for DUI, implied consent and under-twenty-one rules.
