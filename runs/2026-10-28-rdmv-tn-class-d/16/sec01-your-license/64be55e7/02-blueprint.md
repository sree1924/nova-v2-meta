## A — Exam authority for the license classes, eligibility and credentials section (Tennessee Class D knowledge test)
Issuer: the Tennessee Department of Safety and Homeland Security, Driver Services Division, publishes the Tennessee Comprehensive Driver License Manual and takes written comments on it [A01].
Edition statement: the manual says its information was current as of July 1, 2022, and later law, rule or policy changes override anything outdated in it [A01].
Freshness: that content date is old and no issuer page confirms it as the current edition, so every fact here is held under freshness debt until rechecked (verify TN-LIC-A-FRESH).
Knowledge test: a multiple-choice exam drawn from Sections B and C of the manual, split roughly equally across traffic signs and signals, safe driving principles, rules of the road, and drugs and alcohol [A02].
Format: given on computerized testing machines at Driver Service Centers, with Spanish, Korean and Japanese versions where computerized testing is offered, and oral tests by appointment for applicants who cannot read [A02].
Vision: a screening of at least 20/40 in each eye and in both eyes together, with or without corrective lenses, comes before any license or permit [A02].
Retake rule: after failing any knowledge test you must wait at least one day before trying again [A03].
Question count, passing score and time limit: the manual states none of them, so all three stay unknown and are never shown to learners (verify TN-LIC-A-EXAM).
This section feeds the knowledge test: the license class chart, who must hold a Tennessee license, the Class D, learner permit and hardship rules, ID-only and temporary credentials, exemptions, ineligibility and the penalties for driving unlicensed or while revoked.
Practice quizzes follow the product mastery policy from the learning loop, which is not an official exam threshold.
## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: license classes, eligibility and credentials
Audience: first-time Tennessee applicants for the Class D regular operator license, including teens heading for a learner permit and new residents who must switch licenses.
Why it matters to a first-timer: the chapter sets the minimum age and the three tests for Class D [A02] and the learner permit rules that come first for younger applicants [A03].
Level: administrative recall of classes, ages, deadlines and penalties; low conceptual load but several look-alike class and endorsement letters.
Prerequisites: none; this is the opening chapter of Section A and sets up the application, graduated license and examination chapters that follow.
