| sec01-R05 | PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED | Page 6 states the 8-hour requirement but does not state the 2-hour parent-teaching requirement. | Connecticut law requires first-time applicants of any age to take the 8-hour Safe Driving Practices course. |
| sec02-F25 | PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED | The cited page states the legal-authorization rule but does not state the tell-family and ct.gov/dmv information, which is on the next page. | For adults 18 or more the donor registry counts as legal donation authorization. |
| sec03-F04 | PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED | The page ties the monthly/before-long-trip timing to checking tires for wear and damage, not specifically to checking pressure. | Worn tires increase stopping distance and hinder turning on wet roads; check pressure per manual/door jamb, and check tires for wear and damage monthly and before long trips. |
| sec03-F05 | PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED | The page says tread below that level greatly reduces grip, but does not state that a bare head means replace the tire. | Penny test places Lincoln's head in the groove: if any part of the head is covered tread is safe, if below that grip in adverse conditions is greatly reduced. |
| sec03-F28 | PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED | The page does not limit the peace-officer/firefighter/ambulance-driver handheld exception to on-duty use. | Drivers of any age may call emergency responders, hospitals/clinics and police/fire in emergencies, and drivers 18+ who are peace officers, firefighters or ambulance drivers may use handheld devices. |
| sec03-R07 | PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED | The page says low tread greatly reduces grip, but does not order new tires on a fully visible head. | If any part of Lincoln's head is covered tread is safe; if tread gets below that, grip in adverse conditions is greatly reduced. |
| sec04-F22 | PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED | The page reports crash shares, not that motorcyclists mostly die from left-hook crashes. | Approximately one-half of motorcycle crashes involve another vehicle and nearly 40 percent were caused by the other vehicle turning left in front of the motorcyclist. |
| sec04-F30 | PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED | Page 21 does not state trucks crawl uphill; that grade detail is not on the cited page. | Trucks accelerate more slowly than cars, need more room to maneuver and more stopping distance, and are harder to take evasive action with, so avoid the No-Zone. |
| sec04-R01 | PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED | Page 16 states the 12-second rule but does not state the separate four-second sight-distance rule. | Safe drivers look at least 12 seconds ahead, about one block in the city or four blocks on the highway. |
| sec04-R02 | PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED | The page also requires checking oncoming turning traffic, pedestrians, and not turning immediately after passing a bicycle. | Left turns yield to all oncoming traffic including bicycles with an extra look, and right on red requires stopping, checking left, oncoming turning traffic and pedestrians, and obeying any prohibiting sign. |
| sec04-R08 | PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED | The page allows more exceptions than crossings and emergencies, including alley/driveway access, official duties, and police direction. | Slow bikes may be passed in a no-passing zone if safe, but do not stop, park or drive in a bike lane except to enter/leave an alley or driveway, for official duties, when directed, or in an emergency. |
| sec07-F08 | PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED | The page attributes nearly 6 times risk specifically to night shift, not jointly to 60-plus-hour workers. | Night shift increases risk by nearly 6 times; rotating-shift and more than 60 hours a week workers need to be particularly careful. |
| sec07-R06 | PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED | The page gives nearly 6 times only for night shift, not for 60-hour weeks which are only flagged for particular care. | Apnea is 7 times risk; night shift is nearly 6 times, while rotating-shift and over 60 hours need particular care. |
| sec07-R10 | PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED | The page says to let someone else drive for illness and emotions, but for medications only to avoid them and check labels or doctor. | Rumble strips require immediate safe pull-off; illness and emotions say let someone else drive, while medications say avoid sedating medications and check labels or doctor. |
| sec08-F20 | PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED | Omits serious-injury evading and drug-DUI scope stated on page. | Someone under 18 who commits negligent homicide, evading after death or serious injury, or DUI of alcohol or drugs cannot be youthful offender. |
| sec08-R09 | PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED | Omits serious-injury evading and drug-DUI scope in bar list. | Under 18, negligent homicide, evading after death or serious injury, and DUI of alcohol or drugs bar youthful offender status; other listed offenses are disclosed to DMV. |
| sec08-R10 | PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED | Misstates older-driver age as past 25 and omits at least qualifier. | Age 24 or younger needs two or more counts, age 25 or older needs three or more, and a further violation within three years suspends at least 30 days. |
