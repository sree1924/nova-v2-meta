Misconceptions: braking hard during a hydroplane makes it worse, so you lift off the gas and do not brake; low beams, not high beams, work best in fog; a wrong-way driver is usually in the left or HOV lane even though they entered from the right; locked wheels are freed by easing off the brake slightly, not by releasing it completely.
Mnemonics: Lift, do not brake, when you hydroplane; never cross a flooded wash; low beams in fog, rain, dust and glare; ease off the gas and steer away from a wrong-way driver.
Glossary: wash means a dry creek bed that can flood suddenly after heavy rain; highway hypnosis means a trance-like state from long, monotonous highway driving; wrong-way driver means a driver traveling against traffic on a divided highway; inoperable means no longer able to be driven.
Dynamic facts: the flood-response liability rule is statutory and can be amended, and ADOT's overhead wrong-way alert system and the freeways it covers can change.
