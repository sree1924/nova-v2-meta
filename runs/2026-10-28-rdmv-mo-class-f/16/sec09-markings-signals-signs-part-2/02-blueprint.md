## A — Exam authority for the regulatory, railroad, work zone and guide signs section (Missouri Class F operator license written test)
Issuer: the Missouri Department of Revenue publishes the Missouri Driver Guide [A01].
Source document: Missouri Driver Guide, with the front cover reading Revised August 2026 [A02].
Written test: 25 multiple-choice questions, all drawn from information in the guide [A03].
Passing standard: 20 of the 25 questions must be answered correctly, which is 80 percent [A03].
The driver examination has four parts (written, vision, road sign and driving skills) and is given by the Missouri State Highway Patrol; the Class F written test is not open book and leaves out the commercial vehicle chapter [A04].
Time limit: the guide does not state one, so it stays unknown and is never shown to learners (verify MO-SIGN-A-TIME).
This section feeds the written test: stop, yield, wrong way, do not enter, one way, lane control and keep right signs, speed limits and the interstate minimum, railroad crossing duties, work zone types and fines, guide and service signs, route and exit numbering and emergency reference markers.
Practice quizzes follow the product mastery policy from the learning loop, which is not an official exam threshold.

Misconceptions: a posted speed limit is not a safe speed in every condition, and the law requires slowing when conditions demand it; a crossbuck alone means yield, but flashing lights or a lowered gate mean stop; driving very slowly on an interstate is not automatically legal; a stop line placed past the sign is still where you stop.
Mnemonics: odd goes up and down the map, even goes across; even first digit goes around the city, odd first digit goes into it.
Glossary: crossbuck means the white X-shaped sign at a railroad crossing; lane control sign means an arrow sign assigning directions to lanes; flagger means a worker directing traffic through a work zone; flashing arrow panel means a lighted arrow board on a work vehicle showing which lane to use; spur means an interstate branch leading into a city; emergency reference marker means a small mile marker on the interstate shoulder.
Dynamic facts: default and posted speed limits, the interstate minimum speed and work zone fines are set by statute or the highway commission and can change, so posted signs and current law control.
