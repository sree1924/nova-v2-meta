## A — Exam authority for the traffic lights, pedestrian and lane signals, ramp meters, pavement markings, special lanes and traffic officers section (Minnesota Class D written test)
Issuer: the Minnesota Department of Public Safety, whose Driver and Vehicle Services division licenses drivers, permits the printing of the Minnesota Driver’s Manual [A01].
Source document: Minnesota Driver’s Manual, with the edition line June 2026 printed on the front cover [A02].
Written test format: multiple choice and true-or-false questions on Minnesota traffic laws and road signs, taken on paper or on a computer, with English, several other languages and American Sign Language at computer test stations [A03].
Passing standard: a score of 80 percent, and only one written test may be taken per day [A03].
Retest fee: after two consecutive failed written tests, a fee of $10 is charged for the third and every later attempt [A03].
Vision screening: normal or corrected vision of at least 20/40 and peripheral vision of at least 105 degrees [A03].
Question count: the manual does not state how many questions the written test has, so the count stays unknown and is never shown to learners (verify MN-SIGNSB-A-COUNT).
Time limit: the manual does not state one, so it stays unknown and is never shown to learners (verify MN-SIGNSB-A-TIME).
This section feeds the written test: the meaning of steady and flashing red, yellow and green lights and arrows, turns on red, pedestrian Walk and Don't Walk signals, lane use control signals, freeway ramp meters, white and yellow pavement lines, turn, center, reversible and carpool lanes, pavement warning messages and traffic officer signals.

## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: traffic lights, pedestrian and lane signals, ramp meters, pavement markings, special lanes and traffic officers
Audience: first-time Minnesota Class D applicants; signal and pavement marking questions are part of the road sign portion of the written test.
Why it matters to a first-timer: the red and yellow light rules [A01], the flashing yellow and green arrow turn rules [A02] and the white and yellow line meanings [A04] are among the most frequently drilled items.
Level: moderate; the main risk is mixing up steady and flashing versions of the same color and the X and arrow lane signals.
Prerequisites: the turn and passing rules in the traffic laws chapter and the first half of the signs chapter.

Misconceptions: a steady yellow light is a warning to stop if you safely can, not a signal to hurry through; a green light does not remove the duty to yield to pedestrians and oncoming traffic; a flashing yellow arrow is not a protected turn; an officer's hand signal overrides a green light.
Mnemonics: green arrow guarded, flashing yellow yield; steady yellow X get out, flashing yellow X turn left, red X keep out; one long whistle stop, short whistles go; dashes allow, solids forbid, doubles forbid for everyone.
Glossary: lane use control signal means an overhead light above a lane that shows whether the lane may be used; ramp meter means a signal on a freeway entrance ramp that releases one car per green; reversible lane means a lane whose direction changes, often for rush hour; protected turn means a turn made on a green arrow that should be shielded from oncoming or crossing traffic; carpool lane means a lane limited to vehicles with at least two people.
