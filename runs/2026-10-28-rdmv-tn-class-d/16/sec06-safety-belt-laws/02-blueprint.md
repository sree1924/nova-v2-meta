## A — Exam authority for the safety belts, child restraints and air bags section (Tennessee Class D knowledge test)
Issuer: the Tennessee Department of Safety and Homeland Security, Driver Services Division, publishes the Tennessee Comprehensive Driver License Manual and takes written comments on it [A01].
Edition statement: the manual says its information was current as of July 1, 2022, and later law, rule or policy changes override anything outdated in it [A01].
Freshness: that content date is old and no issuer page confirms it as the current edition, so every fact here is held under freshness debt until rechecked (verify TN-BELT-A-FRESH).
Knowledge test: a multiple-choice exam drawn from Sections B and C of the manual, split roughly equally across traffic signs and signals, safe driving principles, rules of the road, and drugs and alcohol [A02].
Format: given on computerized testing machines at Driver Service Centers, with Spanish, Korean and Japanese versions where computerized testing is offered, and oral tests by appointment for applicants who cannot read [A02].
Vision: a screening of at least 20/40 in each eye and in both eyes together, with or without corrective lenses, comes before any license or permit [A02].
Retake rule: after failing any knowledge test you must wait at least one day before trying again [A03].
Question count, passing score and time limit: the manual states none of them, so all three stay unknown and are never shown to learners (verify TN-BELT-A-EXAM).
This section feeds the knowledge test: this chapter belongs to Section B, which the knowledge test draws on, and covers who must wear a belt, the rear-facing, forward-facing and booster seat bands, driver and parent responsibility and penalties, belt fit, air bag seating, pickup bed limits and the unattended-child offense; its sample questions target car seat direction, lap belt position and where children twelve and under should ride.
Practice quizzes follow the product mastery policy from the learning loop, which is not an official exam threshold.
## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: safety belts, child restraints and air bags
Audience: every first-time Tennessee Class D or learner permit applicant, and especially parents and anyone who will drive with children.
Why it matters to a first-timer: the belt and child seat rules [A01] and the air bag seating rules [A03] are law the driver answers for on every trip, and the chapter's sample questions test them directly.
Level: recall of look-alike age, weight and height bands plus a few penalty classes; low conceptual load but high confusion risk between the seat bands.
Prerequisites: the vehicle-setup chapter, which introduces belt fastening and seat distance from the wheel.
