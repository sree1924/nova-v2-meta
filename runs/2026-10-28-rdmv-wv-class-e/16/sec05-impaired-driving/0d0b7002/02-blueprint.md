FACT_ID: sec05-F12 | anchor: A02

FACT_ID: sec05-F30 | anchor: A03

Source: [A03 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0033]

FACT_ID: sec05-F31 | anchor: A03

FACT_ID: sec05-F32 | anchor: A03
The best way to reduce drowsiness is more sleep, and a drowsy driver should not drive but pull over to a safe area for a short nap, since as little as ten to twenty minutes of sleep can make a big difference [A03].

FACT_ID: sec05-F33 | anchor: A03
Drivers should avoid driving at the times they feel sleepy, often early morning, late afternoon or night, and should let a well-rested person drive, carpool, use public transportation, call a taxi or ask a family member or friend to drive [A03].

FACT_ID: sec05-F34 | anchor: A03

FACT_ID: sec05-F35 | anchor: A03
Signs that you yourself are drowsy include falling asleep at stop lights, yawning, rubbing the eyes, watery eyes, heavy eyelids, not remembering the last few miles, missing road signs or exits, unplanned lane changes, head nodding and driving off the road or onto the rumble strips [A03].
