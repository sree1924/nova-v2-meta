## A — Exam authority for the sharing the road section (Missouri Class F operator license written test)
Issuer: the Missouri Department of Revenue publishes the Missouri Driver Guide [A01].
Source document: Missouri Driver Guide, with the front cover reading Revised August 2026 [A02].
Written test: 25 multiple-choice questions, all drawn from information in the guide [A03].
Passing standard: 20 of the 25 questions must be answered correctly, which is 80 percent [A03].
The driver examination has four parts (written, vision, road sign and driving skills) and is given by the Missouri State Highway Patrol; the Class F written test is not open book and leaves out the commercial vehicle chapter [A04].
Time limit: the guide does not state one, so it stays unknown and is never shown to learners (verify MO-SHARE-A-TIME).
This section feeds the written test: lane and passing space for motorcycles, truck blind spots, passing and stopping distances, pedestrian right-of-way, bicycle and moped positioning, licensing and equipment, electric bicycle classes, funeral processions, road rage and reporting drunk drivers.
Practice quizzes follow the product mastery policy from the learning loop, which is not an official exam threshold.
## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: sharing the road
Audience: first-time Missouri Class F applicants learning how to share space with motorcycles, trucks, pedestrians and cyclists, from teens on an instruction permit to adult newcomers.
Why it matters to a first-timer: truck blind spots and stopping distances [A02] and the duty to yield to pedestrians even on green [A03] are the safety rules most tied to serious crashes, and rider equipment rules come with exact distances [A06].
Level: mostly recall of duties plus a set of look-alike distances and speeds for reflectors and electric bicycle classes; moderate load.
Prerequisites: the rules-of-the-road chapter on right-of-way, passing and turning.

Misconceptions: a green light does not let a driver go ahead while pedestrians are still in the intersection; a car may not share a lane with a motorcycle; Missouri requires a helmet for motorcycle riders but not for moped or bicycle riders; a truck that looks slow is often closer and faster than it appears.
Mnemonics: no mirror, no driver, no view, so if you cannot see the trucker in the mirror the trucker cannot see you; full lane for every two-wheeler when passing.
Glossary: No-Zone means the blind spots around a truck or bus; bicycle lane means a striped part of the road set aside for bicycles; motorized bicycle means a small-engine moped of the kind defined by state law; road rage means an uncontrolled display of anger by a driver; funeral procession means an organized line of vehicles following a funeral, using flashing emergency lights.
Dynamic facts: the littering fine and jail limits are statutory and can change, and the phone number for reporting a highway emergency should be checked against current Highway Patrol guidance.
