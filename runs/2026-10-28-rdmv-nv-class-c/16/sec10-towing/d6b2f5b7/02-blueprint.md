Audience: first-time Nevada Basic Class C applicants, especially those who will tow a travel, boat, utility or horse trailer within the Class C limit.

Misconceptions: an empty trailer or truck does not stop shorter, it usually needs more distance; backing a trailer is not steered like a car, the wheel goes the opposite way; a posted bridge height is not a guarantee; the trailer hand brake does not cure a trailer skid, it causes one; swinging wide left before a right turn is the wrong move.
Mnemonics: ten thousand pounds and seventy feet for Class C; heavy low, heavy forward; fifty miles, then three hours or one-fifty; back to the driver's side; brakes off to stop the slide.
Glossary: GVWR means gross vehicle weight rating; GCWR means gross combination weight rating; tongue means the front bar of a trailer that carries the coupler; kingpin means the pin on a fifth-wheel trailer that locks into the hitch jaws; off-tracking means rear wheels or a trailer cutting inside the path of the front wheels; jackknife means a trailer swinging around toward the tow vehicle; rearward amplification means the whipping that grows toward the last trailer.
Dynamic facts: Class C towing limits and endorsement requirements are set by law and the study guide lives on the DMV website, so confirm them against current Nevada DMV material.
