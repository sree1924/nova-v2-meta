## A — Exam authority for the Washington Driving Knowledge Exam

Issuer: the Washington State Department of Licensing (DOL) publishes the official Washington State Driver Guide [A15].
Edition: the guide prints the form line "520-400 (R/4/25)" on its inside front pages [A04]; the cover carries no edition date, and the current edition and effective date remain unconfirmed by the issuer (verify — sec13-A-EDITION).
Currency: the guide warns that it may not reflect the latest changes by the Washington Legislature and points readers to dol.wa.gov for the current version [A15].
Exam path: a new driver must pass two exams, the Driving Knowledge Exam on Washington's rules of the road and then the Driving Skills Exam [A32].
Order and timing: the knowledge exam must be passed before the skills exam, and after passing it you have two years to complete the skills exam [A32].
Where to test: exams can be taken at an approved driver training school exam location or by appointment at a DOL driver licensing office scheduled through License Express [A33].
Scoring: the guide states no question count, no passing score or percent, and no time limit for the knowledge exam; all three stay unknown and no number is asserted here (verify — sec13-A-SCORING).
Question format: the guide does not describe the item format of the knowledge exam (verify — sec13-A-FORMAT).
Practice mastery targets in this product are product policy from the accepted learning loop, never an official passing line.
Dynamic facts (updatable): DOL phone numbers, website services, office processes and fees can change between editions and are kept out of stable knowledge; why_dynamic: administrative details set by DOL outside the printed rules of the road.
## B — Audience and scope

Audience: first-time Washington applicants studying for the Driving Knowledge Exam in English who must handle night driving, curves and Washington's rain, snow and ice, including teens on the permit path and adults licensing for the first time.
Level: plain-English reading level; no prior driving vocabulary is assumed, so every term from the guide is defined where it first appears.
Prerequisites: basic reading plus the earlier chapters on vehicle control and on speed and space, since curve handling and traction reuse balanced weight, braking and following-distance ideas taught there.
Section scope: guide sections on focus and attention, the three types of vision, path of travel, line of sight and information gathering, road and driving conditions, night driving and headlight use, curves and the laws of motion, slippery roads, snow and ice, black ice, skidding and hydroplaning.
Out of scope: chain and traction-tire requirement details for specific passes and dates (not printed on these pages), vehicle repair, commercial and motorcycle content, and anything not printed on the selected guide pages.
