Misconceptions: a green light does not let you go before the intersection is clear of vehicles and pedestrians already in it; a yellow light is not a signal to speed up, beating the light is illegal; at a dark signal with two vehicles arriving together, it is the driver on the left who yields; the law never grants the right-of-way, it only says who must yield.
Mnemonics: Flashing red equals stop sign, flashing yellow equals slow; dark signal means all-way stop, left yields to right.
Glossary: inoperative signal means a traffic light that is dark or not working; T intersection means a junction where one street ends at a cross street; right-of-way means the legal duty of one road user to let another go first; pedestrian-activated signal means a crosswalk signal a pedestrian turns on by pushing a button.
Dynamic facts: signal meanings are stable statute; local signs can forbid a right turn on red at a given intersection, so read the posted signs.
