## A — Exam authority for the testing and the drive test section (Oregon Class C non-commercial knowledge test)
Issuer: Oregon Driver and Motor Vehicle Services (DMV), part of the Oregon Department of Transportation, publishes the Oregon Driver Manual to help drivers get ready for Oregon licensing tests [A04].
Source document: Oregon Driver Manual, with the edition span 2026 - 2027 printed on the front cover [A01].
Scope: the manual covers only the Class C non-commercial driving privilege [A03].
Knowledge test: the Class C knowledge test has 35 multiple-choice questions [A02].
Passing standard: you must answer 28 of the 35 questions correctly, which is exactly 80 percent [A02].
Delivery: the knowledge test can be taken online through the DMV online service or at a DMV office, and the office version is offered in several languages with or without audio [A02].
Time limit: the manual does not state one, so it stays unknown and is never shown to learners (verify OR-TEST-A-TIME).
This section feeds the knowledge test: this is the testing chapter itself: the three tests for an original privilege, fee and proof at every test, the knowledge test's length, passing standard, office and online rules, retest limits, the drive test content, waivers, conduct rules and test vehicle equipment.
Practice quizzes follow the product mastery policy from the learning loop, which is not an official exam threshold.
## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: testing and the drive test
Audience: every first-time Oregon applicant for a Class C non-commercial driving privilege who is about to take the vision and knowledge tests and book a drive test, including teens testing online with a proctor and new residents who may qualify for a drive test waiver.
Why it matters to a first-timer: this chapter says what DMV tests for an original privilege and what to bring to every test [A02], how the knowledge test is scored and run [A03], and what the car must have before a drive test can start [A05].
Level: procedural recall with a few exact thresholds (question count, passing count, retest waits, proctor ages, waiver windows); low conceptual load.
Prerequisites: none inside the manual; this is the first chapter and frames every later study chapter.

Misconceptions: passing the knowledge test is not the whole job, because DMV also tests vision and driving skill for an original privilege; the online test is not unlimited, since it caps attempts and then sends you to an office; a family member or translator cannot ride along on the drive test; a car that fails the equipment check ends the appointment before any driving is graded.
Mnemonics: Vision, Knowledge, Skill is the order of the three tests; pay, prove, then test describes every visit; bring a car that Starts, Signals, Stops and Sounds its horn.
Glossary: driving privilege means the legal permission to drive, issued as a permit or license; Class C means the non-commercial driving privilege this manual covers; standby service means testing at an office without an appointment; proctor means the adult who supervises a minor's online test; Third Party Tester means a certified business that gives drive tests outside DMV offices.
Dynamic facts: test fees, office hours, the DMV online service portal, the scheduling phone line and the Third Party Tester listing can change; confirm them on the Oregon DMV website.

## D — Accuracy notes and source flags: testing and the drive test
Every C and C2 fact cites exactly one physical manual page from the selected range; nothing relies on outside knowledge.
The passing standard is printed as a count, twenty-eight correct out of thirty-five questions; the eighty percent figure in part A is that exact ratio and is not a separately printed percentage [A03].
The office retest rule is conditional: a same-day retest depends on the office's testing capacity, so it is not stated as a guaranteed right [A03].
The online attempt limits (two per day, four in all) apply to the online test only; the manual states no total attempt cap for office testing, so none is asserted [A04].
The manual gives no time limit for the knowledge test and no fee amounts; fees are sent to the DMV fee web page, so no value is stated (time limit held as debt in part A).
The scheduling phone number, the online service name, the three-week booking suggestion and the Third Party Tester web page are service details that can change and are isolated as dynamic [A06].
