## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: advanced driver assistance systems and autonomous vehicles
Audience: every first-time Nevada Basic Class C applicant, especially those learning in newer vehicles fitted with assistance features.
Why it matters to a first-timer: the handbook stresses that these systems assist but never drive the vehicle [A01], and it bars convenience features such as adaptive cruise control during the testing process [A04].
Level: concept recognition with clear category boundaries (warning, safety-critical assistance, convenience) and few numeric values.
Prerequisites: sec05 and sec06 for following distance, backing and lane-change habits, which these systems support but do not replace.

FACT_ID: sec07-F24 | anchor: A05

Misconceptions: driver assistance does not mean the car drives itself; a forward collision warning alone does not brake for you, while automatic emergency braking does; a rear camera or back-up warning does not replace mirrors and head checks; lane keeping assist fails when lane lines are faint or covered; convenience features like adaptive cruise control are not allowed during testing.
Mnemonics: warn, then assist, then convenience; warnings tell, assists act, conveniences are off during the test; faster beeps mean closer, a steady tone means stop.
Glossary: ADAS means advanced driver assistance systems; passive system means a feature that only warns and does not act; space cushion means the open space kept around your vehicle; adaptive cruise control means cruise control that keeps a set following gap; SAE means the Society of Automotive Engineers, which defines driving-automation levels from zero to five.
Dynamic facts: which assistance features a vehicle has, and how each one warns, differ by make and model, so the owner's manual governs.
