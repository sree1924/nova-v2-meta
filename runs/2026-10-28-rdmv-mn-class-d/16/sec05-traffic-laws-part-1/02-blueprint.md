## A — Exam authority for the speed limits, signaling, lanes, turns, roundabouts, J-turns, U-turns and passing section (Minnesota Class D written test)
Issuer: the Minnesota Department of Public Safety, whose Driver and Vehicle Services division licenses drivers, permits the printing of the Minnesota Driver’s Manual [A01].
Source document: Minnesota Driver’s Manual, with the edition line June 2026 printed on the front cover [A02].
Written test format: multiple choice and true-or-false questions on Minnesota traffic laws and road signs, taken on paper or on a computer, with English, several other languages and American Sign Language at computer test stations [A03].
Passing standard: a score of 80 percent, and only one written test may be taken per day [A03].
Retest fee: after two consecutive failed written tests, a fee of $10 is charged for the third and every later attempt [A03].
Vision screening: normal or corrected vision of at least 20/40 and peripheral vision of at least 105 degrees [A03].
Question count: the manual does not state how many questions the written test has, so the count stays unknown and is never shown to learners (verify MN-TRAFFICA-A-COUNT).
Time limit: the manual does not state one, so it stays unknown and is never shown to learners (verify MN-TRAFFICA-A-TIME).
This section feeds the written test: the keep-right rule, the basic speed law and default speed limits, the speeding fine and revocation thresholds, the emergency vehicle surcharge, the signaling distance, lane changes and blind spots, left and right turns and turns on red, roundabouts, J-turns, divided highway crossings, U-turns, right-lane travel, passing and backing.
Practice quizzes follow the product mastery policy from the learning loop, which is not an official exam threshold.
## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: speed limits, signaling, lanes, turns, roundabouts, J-turns, U-turns and passing
Audience: first-time Minnesota Class D applicants preparing for the written test, and permit holders practicing turns, roundabouts and passing with a supervisor.
Why it matters to a first-timer: the default speed limits and basic speed law [A01], the signaling distance and speeding penalties [A02], and the passing distances and no-passing rules [A08] are among the most frequently drilled numeric items in the manual.
Level: moderate; many look-alike distances (one hundred, seven hundred and one thousand feet) and speed numbers make confusion the main risk rather than concept difficulty.
Prerequisites: the vehicle equipment chapter (signals and mirrors) and basic familiarity with lane markings.

Mnemonics: alley ten, town thirty, everywhere else fifty-five; signal a hundred feet ahead and be back in your lane a hundred feet before an oncoming car; see seven hundred feet to pass, be seen from a thousand feet to U-turn; in a roundabout yield, go counterclockwise and do not stop.
Glossary: basic speed law means the rule that speed must suit current conditions; blind spot means an area around the vehicle the mirrors cannot show; roundabout means a circular intersection where traffic flows counterclockwise around a centre island; J-turn means a right turn followed by a U-turn at a median opening used instead of crossing a divided highway; combination vehicle means a vehicle towing another unit.
