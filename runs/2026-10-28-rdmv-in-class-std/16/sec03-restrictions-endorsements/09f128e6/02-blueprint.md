## A — Exam authority for the restrictions, endorsements and small vehicles section (Indiana learner's permit and driver's license knowledge exam)
Issuer: the Indiana Bureau of Motor Vehicles (BMV) publishes the Indiana Driver's Manual; both names appear on the front cover [A02].
Source document: the front cover prints no edition; the back cover reads Published July 2026 and points readers to IN.gov/BMV for updates [A04].
Knowledge exam: a multiple-choice, computer-based exam drawn from Indiana law, defensive driving practices and the manual, which also tests reading of regulatory, warning and traffic signs and signals [A01].
Exam structure: the exam has two components, one on common signs (Chapter 6) and one on traffic rules and laws (Chapters 6, 7 and 8) [A03].
Passing standard: 80 percent or higher correct on each component [A03].
Branch logistics: arrive at least 30 minutes before the branch closes to test that day; after a failure, wait until the next business day to retake [A01].
Question count: the manual gives no number of questions for either component or in total, so it stays unknown and is never shown to learners (verify IN-RESTRICT-A-COUNT).
Time limit: the manual does not state one, so it stays unknown (verify IN-RESTRICT-A-TIME).
This section feeds the knowledge exam: this chapter explains the restriction and endorsement codes printed on a credential, especially the vision restrictions a first-time applicant can receive at the screening, and which small vehicles (autocycles, motor driven cycles) a license or ID card covers.
Practice quizzes follow the product mastery policy from the learning loop, which is not an official exam threshold.
## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: restrictions, endorsements and small vehicles
Audience: first-time Indiana applicants who may leave the vision screening with a restriction code, plus applicants curious about autocycles, motor driven cycles, motorcycles and for-hire driving.
Why it matters to a first-timer: the vision restrictions and code chart decide what a new credential allows [A01], and the autocycle and MDC rules say which small vehicles a license or ID card already covers [A03].
Level: code-letter and threshold recall (ages, engine size, speed cap, weight bands, renewal windows); low conceptual load but many look-alike codes.
Prerequisites: the first chapter's vision screening and learner's permit rules.

Misconceptions: an autocycle needs no motorcycle endorsement even though it has three wheels; letter B means glasses on a license restriction but motor driven cycle on an ID card endorsement; a for-hire endorsement never authorizes a commercial motor vehicle; a valid permit or license already covers motor driven cycle riding without an MDC endorsement.
Mnemonics: B for bifocals (glasses or contacts), F for field-of-view aids (mirrors or camera), G for go by daylight; MDC means fifteen, fifty cc and thirty-five tops.
Glossary: restriction means a condition the holder must meet while driving; endorsement means an added privilege such as motorcycle or for-hire; autocycle means an enclosed three-wheeler driven with a wheel and pedals; motor driven cycle means a registered small-engine cycle of no more than fifty cubic centimeters; Ride Safe Indiana means the state's approved motorcycle training program.
Dynamic facts: endorsement fees, Ride Safe Indiana provider lists, the RideSafeIndiana.com and IN.gov/BMV pages and renewal channels can change; confirm them on IN.gov/BMV.
