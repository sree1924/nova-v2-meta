## A — Exam authority for the driving test requirements section (Wisconsin Class D regular driver license knowledge test)
Issuer: the Wisconsin Department of Transportation and its Division of Motor Vehicles (DMV), which offers the Motorists Handbook as a resource that helps train and qualify new drivers [A03].
Source document: Wisconsin Motorists Handbook, with 2026 printed on the front cover [A01]; the back cover carries form number BDS126 with 2026 [A04].
Knowledge test: 50 questions on traffic laws, and 40 correct answers (80 percent) are needed to pass [A02].
Highway signs test: a separate test of 15 questions, with 12 correct answers (80 percent) needed to pass [A02].
Vision screening: regular car and motorcycle applicants need visual acuity of at least 20/40 in one eye, corrected or not, and a 70-degree field of vision from the center of each eye [A02].
Duration: the handbook says the knowledge test takes about 45 minutes to finish, but it states no formal time limit [A02].
Time limit: unknown, so no limit is ever shown to learners (verify WI-TEST-A-TIME).
Delivery: the signs and knowledge tests need no appointment and are free at a DMV customer service center; applicants aged 15 to 17 can also sit the knowledge test online for a $10 fee [A02].
This section feeds the knowledge test: this is the testing chapter itself: the vision screening standards, the separate highway signs test, the knowledge test's length, scoring, cost and online option, and the appointment-only skills test with its vehicle check and graded maneuvers.
Practice quizzes follow the product mastery policy from the learning loop, which is not an official exam threshold.

Misconceptions: the highway signs test is a separate test from the knowledge test, not a few sign questions inside it; only the skills test needs an appointment, while the signs and knowledge tests are walk-in; the sharpness standard needs to be reached in only one eye, although the field-of-vision rule is measured from each eye; the car itself is inspected before the road test, so a broken light or horn can stop the test before it begins.
Mnemonics: both written tests share the same eighty percent bar, so think twelve of fifteen for signs and forty of fifty for laws; twenty-forty in one eye and seventy degrees from each eye for vision.
Glossary: skills test means the road test or driving test with a DMV examiner; visual acuity means sharpness of vision; field of vision means how wide an area you can see while looking straight ahead; Certificate of Vision Examination by Competent Authority means the form a vision specialist completes after a failed DMV screening.
Dynamic facts: the online test fee, the age range for online testing, customer service center hours and the DMV web addresses for practice tests and road test scheduling can change; confirm them on the Wisconsin DMV website.

## D — Accuracy notes and source flags: driving test requirements
Every C and C2 fact cites exactly one physical handbook page from the selected range; nothing relies on outside knowledge.
The knowledge-test count and passing standard are printed in the same paragraph, and the signs test paragraph uses the same percentage with a different count [A01].
The handbook gives an expected duration for the knowledge test, about forty-five minutes, rather than a stated time limit, so no limit is asserted [A01] (verify WI-TEST-A-TIME).

The vision paragraph names regular car and motorcycle applicants together; no separate standard for other classes is stated on these pages [A01].
The online test fee, online age range, service center hours and DMV web addresses are service details that can change and are isolated as dynamic.
