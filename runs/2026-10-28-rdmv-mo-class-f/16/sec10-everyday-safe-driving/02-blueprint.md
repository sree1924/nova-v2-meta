## A — Exam authority for the everyday safe driving section (Missouri Class F operator license written test)
Issuer: the Missouri Department of Revenue publishes the Missouri Driver Guide [A01].
Source document: Missouri Driver Guide, with the front cover reading Revised August 2026 [A02].
Written test: 25 multiple-choice questions, all drawn from information in the guide [A03].
Passing standard: 20 of the 25 questions must be answered correctly, which is 80 percent [A03].
The driver examination has four parts (written, vision, road sign and driving skills) and is given by the Missouri State Highway Patrol; the Class F written test is not open book and leaves out the commercial vehicle chapter [A04].
Time limit: the guide does not state one, so it stays unknown and is never shown to learners (verify MO-SAFE-A-TIME).
This section feeds the written test: seat belt and child restraint law, air bag safety, defensive driving, posture and steering, the three second following rule, space behind and beside you, stopping distance, signaling, horn use, the slow-driving law, carbon monoxide, deer crashes, tire checks and conduct at a traffic stop.
Practice quizzes follow the product mastery policy from the learning loop, which is not an official exam threshold.
## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: everyday safe driving
Audience: first-time Missouri Class F applicants, from teens on an instruction permit to adult newcomers, including parents who will carry child passengers.
Why it matters to a first-timer: seat belt duties and the child restraint tiers are law with exact thresholds [A01] [A02], and stopping distance and signaling distance are classic test items [A04].
Level: a broad mix of law recall, threshold logic and habits; moderate load, with the restraint tiers the hardest part.
Prerequisites: the license chapters for the intermediate license, and the highway chapter on keeping pace with traffic.

Misconceptions: air bags do not replace seat belts; signing a ticket is not admitting guilt; slower is not always safer, and blocking traffic by driving too slowly is illegal; braking hard to warn off a tailgater raises your crash risk.
Mnemonics: one-thousand one, one-thousand two, one-thousand three for the following gap; reaction plus braking equals stopping.
Glossary: child passenger restraint system means a car seat sized for a young child; booster seat means a raised seat that positions an adult belt on a child; reaction distance means the distance covered before you brake; braking distance means the distance covered while braking to a stop; hand-over-hand means a steering method crossing one hand over the other; tailgating means following another vehicle too closely.
Dynamic facts: the child restraint thresholds and seat belt rules are statutory and can change, deer crash timing reflects seasonal patterns stated in the guide, and agency phone numbers and web pages for child seat help may change.
