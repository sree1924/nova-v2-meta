## A — Exam authority for the indicators, placards, renewal and amendments section (Indiana learner's permit and driver's license knowledge exam)
Issuer: the Indiana Bureau of Motor Vehicles (BMV) publishes the Indiana Driver's Manual; both names appear on the front cover [A02].
Source document: the front cover prints no edition; the back cover reads Published July 2026 and points readers to IN.gov/BMV for updates [A04].
Knowledge exam: a multiple-choice, computer-based exam drawn from Indiana law, defensive driving practices and the manual, which also tests reading of regulatory, warning and traffic signs and signals [A01].
Exam structure: the exam has two components, one on common signs (Chapter 6) and one on traffic rules and laws (Chapters 6, 7 and 8) [A03].
Passing standard: 80 percent or higher correct on each component [A03].
Branch logistics: arrive at least 30 minutes before the branch closes to test that day; after a failure, wait until the next business day to retake [A01].
Question count: the manual gives no number of questions for either component or in total, so it stays unknown and is never shown to learners (verify IN-RENEW-A-COUNT).
Time limit: the manual does not state one, so it stays unknown (verify IN-RENEW-A-TIME).
This section feeds the knowledge exam: this section covers what a new credential can carry (donor heart, military indicators), boating and disability parking rules tied to the credential, and the expiration, late-renewal, amendment and replacement deadlines a new driver must keep after licensing.
Practice quizzes follow the product mastery policy from the learning loop, which is not an official exam threshold.
## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: indicators, placards, renewal and amendments
Audience: new and current Indiana credential holders, including teens who will soon face the probationary expiry, adults who move or change names, boaters, veterans and people needing disability parking.
Why it matters to a first-timer: expiration and late-renewal rules decide when a lapsed license means retesting [A03], and the address and name deadlines apply as soon as a new driver moves or marries [A04].
Level: deadline and threshold recall (day counts, month windows, horsepower and age limits, placard lifetimes); low conceptual load.
Prerequisites: the first chapter's credential types and probationary license rules; the points chapter explains the active-point trigger.

Misconceptions: a valid placard does not allow parking in the striped access aisle; most credentials expire on the birthday, not on the issue anniversary; a late renewal brings a penalty even when no exam is required; a mailing address change does not update the principal address, which needs a branch visit.

Glossary: indicator means a marker printed on a credential such as the donor heart or a military symbol; principal address means the legal residence address; interim credential means a temporary credential issued while living outside Indiana; administrative penalty means the charge for renewing after expiration; Disabled Hoosier Veteran plate means the Indiana license plate for qualifying disabled veterans.
