Misconceptions:
- Signaling at the last moment is fine as long as the signal is on: false, the law requires the signal not less than one hundred feet ahead, which three to five seconds of signaling covers.
- Turning the wheels toward the turn while waiting to go left is efficient: false, keep the wheels straight so a rear end collision cannot push you into oncoming traffic.
- Four seconds is always enough following distance: false, poor roads, poor visibility and higher speeds call for more than four seconds.
- The posted limit is the safe speed: false, conditions such as curves, construction, weather and pedestrians require a slower speed, and even ten miles per hour can be unsafe.
- At a four way stop the driver on the right always goes first: false, the first vehicle to arrive goes first; the yield to the right applies only when vehicles arrive together.

Mnemonics:
- Nine and three or eight and four: the two recommended hand positions on the wheel.
- See, think, move, work: the four things that must happen to stop, from seeing the danger to the brakes working.
- Right front left for a left, left front right and left again for a right: the two intersection search orders.
- Fifty on the road, sixty-five on the interstate: Vermont's two maximum speeds unless posted lower.

Glossary:
- Reaction distance: the distance a vehicle travels between the moment the driver sees danger and the moment the brake is pressed
- Braking distance: the distance a vehicle travels from the moment the brakes are applied until it stops
- Four second method: counting the seconds between the vehicle ahead passing a checkpoint and your own vehicle reaching it, with four or more being safe
- Visual search pattern: an orderly, repeating way of scanning every direction for hazards and changes near the intended path
- Yield right-of-way sign: an intersection sign requiring a speed slow enough to stop and entry only when the way is clear
