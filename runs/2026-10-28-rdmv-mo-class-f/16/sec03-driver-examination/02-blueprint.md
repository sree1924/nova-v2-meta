## A — Exam authority for the the four-part driver examination section (Missouri Class F operator license written test)
Issuer: the Missouri Department of Revenue publishes the Missouri Driver Guide [A01].
Source document: Missouri Driver Guide, with the front cover reading Revised August 2026 [A02].
Written test: 25 multiple-choice questions, all drawn from information in the guide [A03].
Passing standard: 20 of the 25 questions must be answered correctly, which is 80 percent [A03].
The driver examination has four parts (written, vision, road sign and driving skills) and is given by the Missouri State Highway Patrol; the Class F written test is not open book and leaves out the commercial vehicle chapter [A04].
Time limit: the guide does not state one, so it stays unknown and is never shown to learners (verify MO-EXAM-A-TIME).
This section feeds the written test: this is the examination chapter itself: who must test, the Class F written test's size and closed-book rule, the acuity and side-vision standards, the road sign test, the test vehicle requirements, the driving maneuvers the examiner grades, the point limit, retest rules and exam fraud.
Practice quizzes follow the product mastery policy from the learning loop, which is not an official exam threshold.
## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: the four-part driver examination
Audience: every first-time Missouri Class F applicant about to sit the written, vision and road sign tests and then book a Highway Patrol driving test, plus newcomers whose out-of-state license qualifies for the shortened route.
Why it matters to a first-timer: this chapter says what the written test covers and which applicants must take all four parts [A01], and how the driving test is graded and retaken [A04].
Level: procedural recall with a handful of exact thresholds (question count, vision standards, parking dimensions, point limit, retest count); low conceptual load.
Prerequisites: the license classes, GDL steps and document rules of the first chapter.
