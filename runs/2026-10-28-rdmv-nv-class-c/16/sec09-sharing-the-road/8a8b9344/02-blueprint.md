## A — Exam authority for the sharing the road (trucks and buses, motorcycles, mopeds, school buses, bicycles and pedestrians) section (Nevada Basic Class C driver license knowledge test)
Issuer: the Nevada Department of Motor Vehicles publishes the Nevada Driver's Handbook and says the knowledge test for a Nevada license draws on what the handbook contains [A01].
Source document: the Nevada Driver's Handbook whose front cover carries the form and edition line DMV 700 March 2024 [A02].
Edition freshness: this edition was already about two and a half years old when it was staged, so whether it is still the current one stays open until the DMV confirms it (verify NV-SHAR-A-FRESH).
Knowledge test format: it checks your grasp of highway signs and markings, traffic laws and safe driving practices, on automated testing equipment or on paper; an oral exam can be requested, and Basic Class C testing is also offered in Spanish [A03].
Vision: every applicant has vision checked against minimum standards, and needing glasses or contact lenses to drive puts a restriction on the license [A03].
Question count, passing standard and time limit: the handbook states none of them, so all three stay unknown and are never shown to learners (verify NV-SHAR-A-COUNT, NV-SHAR-A-PASS, NV-SHAR-A-TIME).
This section feeds the knowledge test: the knowledge test checks traffic laws and safe driving practices, and this section carries the rules for every other road user: truck and bus no-zones, motorcycle lane sharing and dimming, moped limits, the school bus stop law, cyclist lanes and reckless-driving exposure, truck-bed riders, crosswalk and pedestrian yielding, and care for blind or visually impaired pedestrians.

## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: sharing the road (trucks and buses, motorcycles, mopeds, school buses, bicycles and pedestrians)
Audience: every first-time Nevada Basic Class C applicant; these rules govern how a car driver behaves around larger and smaller road users.
Why it matters to a first-timer: the truck no-zones explain why most car-truck crashes are caused by car drivers [A01], and the school bus stop law is a strict statute with a divided-highway exception [A03].
Level: rule recall with a few exact values (two abreast, a quarter mile, five feet, a few seconds) and several confusable exceptions.
Prerequisites: sec05 for right-of-way and sec06 for passing and cyclist passing distance, which this section builds on.

Misconceptions: most car-truck crashes are not the truck driver's fault, car drivers cause most of them; seeing the truck in your mirror is not enough, you must see the driver's face in their mirror to be seen; a crosswalk does not need paint to exist; on a divided highway, oncoming traffic does not stop for a school bus; motorcycles may share a lane two abreast only with consent; mopeds need registration but not insurance.
Mnemonics: no face, no space beside a truck; whole front in the mirror before you merge back; red lights on the bus, everybody stops, except across a divided highway; two abreast with consent; quarter mile for the moped left turn.
Glossary: no-zone means the blind-spot area around a large truck or bus; squeeze play means a car slipping between a wide-turning truck and the curb; endorsement means an extra privilege added to a license, such as for motorcycles; moped means a small motor vehicle limited in horsepower and top speed; unmarked crosswalk means the legal crossing at any intersection even without paint.
