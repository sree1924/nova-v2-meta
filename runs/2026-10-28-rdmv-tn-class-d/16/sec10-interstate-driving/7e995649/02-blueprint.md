## A — Exam authority for the interstate driving section (Tennessee Class D knowledge test)
Issuer: the Tennessee Department of Safety and Homeland Security, Driver Services Division, publishes the Tennessee Comprehensive Driver License Manual and takes written comments on it [A01].
Edition statement: the manual says its information was current as of July 1, 2022, and later law, rule or policy changes override anything outdated in it [A01].
Freshness: that content date is old and no issuer page confirms it as the current edition, so every fact here is held under freshness debt until rechecked (verify TN-INTER-A-FRESH).
Knowledge test: a multiple-choice exam drawn from Sections B and C of the manual, split roughly equally across traffic signs and signals, safe driving principles, rules of the road, and drugs and alcohol [A02].
Format: given on computerized testing machines at Driver Service Centers, with Spanish, Korean and Japanese versions where computerized testing is offered, and oral tests by appointment for applicants who cannot read [A02].
Vision: a screening of at least 20/40 in each eye and in both eyes together, with or without corrective lenses, comes before any license or permit [A02].
Retake rule: after failing any knowledge test you must wait at least one day before trying again [A03].
Question count, passing score and time limit: the manual states none of them, so all three stay unknown and are never shown to learners (verify TN-INTER-A-EXAM).
This section feeds the knowledge test: the manual maps interstate driving to its rules of the road practice topic, and this chapter covers trip preparation, entering and merging, lane use, following distance, speed and traffic flow, HOV lanes, exiting, diamond, cloverleaf and weaving interchanges, prohibited moves, interstate hypnosis, congestion habits, the Move Over Law, breakdowns, crash scenes and the emergency kit; its sample questions target merging speed, emergency crossovers and the alertness interstate driving demands.
Practice quizzes follow the product mastery policy from the learning loop, which is not an official exam threshold.
## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: interstate driving
Audience: every first-time Tennessee Class D or learner permit applicant, including drivers who have not yet driven at highway speed.
Why it matters to a first-timer: merging [A01], interchange and exit rules [A03] and the Move Over Law [A05] govern the highest-speed driving a new driver faces and appear in the sample questions.
Level: procedural recall plus scenario judgment (which ramp, which lane, when to slow); moderate load with a few exact values such as following seconds, HOV hours and break intervals.
Prerequisites: the rules of the road chapters, which cover speed limits, following distance, lane use and the first Move Over description.

Misconceptions: you speed up, not slow down, to enter an interstate; stopping at the end of an on-ramp is dangerous, not cautious; a lane added sign is not a merge sign; slowing in your lane satisfies the Move Over Law only when changing lanes is unsafe; a missed exit never justifies backing up or using a crossover.
Mnemonics: speed up to merge; two seconds, four when it is dark, wet or fast; every two hours or a hundred miles, take a break; *THP for the Highway Patrol.
Glossary: interchange means a set of ramps connecting an interstate with another road; acceleration lane means the ramp lane used to reach interstate speed; deceleration lane means the exit lane used to slow down; interstate hypnosis means drowsiness caused by long monotonous driving.
Dynamic facts: HOV hours and fines, the *THP service, HELP truck coverage and the Move Over Law details can change; confirm them on official Tennessee sources before showing them to learners.
