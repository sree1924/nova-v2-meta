## C — Defensive driving and weather (part two)

FACT_ID: sec14-F19 | anchor: A04
Turning on your headlights during rain or reduced visibility is required by law and increases your visibility to others [A04].
Source: [A04 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0073]

FACT_ID: sec14-F20 | anchor: A04
Wet pavement is most dangerous at the start of a light rain, when oil, dirt and water form a slippery film that weakens road contact on curves and lengthens stopping distance, so reduce your speed [A04].
Source: [A04 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0073]

FACT_ID: sec14-F21 | anchor: A04
On wet pavement tires wipe the road like a windshield wiper at speeds up to thirty-five miles per hour, a standard passenger vehicle begins to hydroplane partially at about thirty-five miles per hour, and at fifty-five miles per hour the tires ride entirely on the water film, leaving no friction to brake, accelerate or corner [A04].
Source: [A04 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0073]

FACT_ID: sec14-F22 | anchor: A04
In a severe rainstorm tires lose all contact with the road at fifty-five miles per hour, and a gust of wind, a curve or a slight turn can then create an uncontrollable skid [A04].
Source: [A04 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0073]

FACT_ID: sec14-F23 | anchor: A04
To reduce hydroplaning slow down in rainstorms, on slushy roads and in standing water, keep tires properly inflated and replace them when the tread wear indicators show, because deep treads let water escape and can prevent complete hydroplaning at normal highway speeds [A04].
Source: [A04 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0073]

FACT_ID: sec14-F24 | anchor: A04
Never drive across a flooded roadway, because as little as six inches of water can float some smaller vehicles and two feet of water will carry most vehicles away, after which the vehicle may roll and fill with water, trapping everyone inside [A04].
Source: [A04 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0073]

FACT_ID: sec14-F25 | anchor: A04
Motorists trapped in their vehicles account for nearly half of all flash flood deaths in the United States, victims often drive straight off the edge of an eroded road without knowing it is gone, and flooding is especially hard to recognize at night [A04].
Source: [A04 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0073]

FACT_ID: sec14-F26 | anchor: A04
If your vehicle stalls on a flooded roadway leave it immediately and seek higher ground, because it is better to be wet than dead [A04].
Source: [A04 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0073]

FACT_ID: sec14-F27 | anchor: A04
Keep snow tires or chains ready in winter but remember that neither allows safe driving on snow or ice at normal speeds, so slow down and keep a safe distance, since stopping on snow or ice can take up to twelve times the normal distance and bridges and overpasses may be icy when all other pavement is clear [A04].
Source: [A04 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0073]

FACT_ID: sec14-F28 | anchor: A04
Keep every window clear of ice and snow, keep the windshield wipers and defroster in good working order, and never start driving until all windows are clear and completely defrosted [A04].
Source: [A04 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0073]

FACT_ID: sec14-F29 | anchor: A05
When stopping or slowing on ice or packed snow apply the brakes gently and smoothly, a vehicle with anti-lock brakes keeps its steering while braking, but in a vehicle without them you cannot steer unless you pump the brakes by applying and releasing them several times, and you release the brakes at the first hint that the wheels are slipping [A05].
Source: [A05 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0074]

FACT_ID: sec14-F30 | anchor: A05
When starting on snow or ice keep engine speed low, accelerate slowly and smoothly, let out a manual clutch slowly and shift smoothly, and once moving test the brakes at low speed to learn how slippery the road is and adjust your speed to it [A05].
Source: [A05 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0074]

FACT_ID: sec14-F31 | anchor: A05
Strong winds can down power lines and trees and blow debris into your path, and crosswinds can be strong enough to force your vehicle into the oncoming lane or off the roadway [A05].
Source: [A05 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0074]

FACT_ID: sec14-F32 | anchor: A05
Fog is extremely hazardous and smoke from factories, forest fires or burning leaves acts the same way, so in fog or dense smoke reduce speed, be ready for an emergency stop, use low-beam headlights and the wipers, and if your vision is impaired pull off the road, stop, leave the lights on and turn on the hazard lights [A05].
Source: [A05 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0074]

FACT_ID: sec14-F33 | anchor: A05
Road conditions to consider are mainly matters of construction such as curves, hills, pavement width, multiple lanes, road surfaces, poorly marked or unmarked roads, shoulders and drop-offs [A05].
Source: [A05 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0074]

FACT_ID: sec14-F34 | anchor: A05
You cannot safely drive ten feet or ten miles in an unsafe vehicle, so West Virginia requires every vehicle to be inspected every two years at a station displaying the official state inspection sign, and the windshield sticker showing the month and year of the next inspection must never be removed [A05].
Source: [A05 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0074]
