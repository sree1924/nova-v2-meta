Misconceptions: when a pass goes wrong you brake and drop back rather than speeding up to finish; the lane-change signal is required for at least one hundred feet, like a turn signal; you never change lanes or pass while approaching or in an intersection or crosswalk; an abandoned pass is judged safe to end only when the overtaken vehicle shows in the interior mirror; in a roundabout you look left and yield to circulating traffic and signal right to leave; in an emergency stop you pump and steer rather than locking the wheels; waiting to turn left you keep the wheels straight, not turned.

Mnemonics: One thousand one, one thousand two is the following interval; look left, go right of the island, signal right to leave the roundabout; brake and drop back when a pass fails; pump and steer in an emergency; see the rear tires of the car ahead when you stop.

Glossary: roundabout (a one-way circular intersection where entering traffic yields to circulating traffic); lane hopping (continual lane changing); following interval (the time gap to the vehicle ahead, measured by counting); blind spot (the area beside the vehicle not shown in the mirrors); downshifting (selecting a lower gear while moving); double clutching (matching engine speed for a non-synchronized downshift).

Dynamic facts: none; the maneuver procedures and the unattended-child rule are stable content of this printing.
