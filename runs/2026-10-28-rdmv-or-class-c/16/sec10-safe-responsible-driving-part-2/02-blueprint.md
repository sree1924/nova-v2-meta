## A — Exam authority for the distraction, road rage, fatigue, health, impairment, external passengers and equipment failures section (Oregon Class C non-commercial knowledge test)
Issuer: Oregon Driver and Motor Vehicle Services (DMV), part of the Oregon Department of Transportation, publishes the Oregon Driver Manual to help drivers get ready for Oregon licensing tests [A04].
Source document: Oregon Driver Manual, with the edition span 2026 - 2027 printed on the front cover [A01].
Scope: the manual covers only the Class C non-commercial driving privilege [A03].
Knowledge test: the Class C knowledge test has 35 multiple-choice questions [A02].
Passing standard: you must answer 28 of the 35 questions correctly, which is exactly 80 percent [A02].
Delivery: the knowledge test can be taken online through the DMV online service or at a DMV office, and the office version is offered in several languages with or without audio [A02].
Time limit: the manual does not state one, so it stays unknown and is never shown to learners (verify OR-RISK-A-TIME).

## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: distraction, road rage, fatigue, health, impairment, external passengers and equipment failures
Audience: every first-time Oregon Class C applicant; the phone, impairment and open container laws are heavily tested and apply from the first day of driving.
Why it matters to a first-timer: phone use rules depend on the driver's age [A01], the implied consent fail level differs for drivers under and over the drinking age [A04], and each equipment failure has its own short response sequence [A06].
Level: rule and number recall (age limits, blood alcohol levels, sound distance) plus step-sequence recall for equipment failures; moderate to heavy load.
Prerequisites: the safe and responsible driving part one section, especially defensive driving and collision avoidance.

Misconceptions: an adult may use a phone while driving only through a hands-free accessory, and a driver under eighteen may not use one at all; a BAC under the presumed-impaired level does not protect you from a DUII arrest; DUII covers medicines and marijuana, not just alcohol; a resealed bottle still counts as an open container; slamming the brakes after a blowout is wrong, you slow gradually; a pickup bed is not a legal seat for minors.
Mnemonics: grip, slow, pull off for a blowout; pump, downshift, parking brake, warn, shoulder for failed brakes; under twenty-one means zero; seal broken means open.
Glossary: DUII means driving under the influence of intoxicants; BAC means blood alcohol concentration; implied consent means agreeing, by driving, to a breath, blood or urine test when police ask; zero tolerance means any amount of alcohol fails the test for a driver under twenty-one; hands-free accessory means a device that lets an adult use a phone with minimal finger use.
Dynamic facts: none in these pages; the device, blood alcohol and open container rules are fixed statements of the current manual and can change only with new law.
