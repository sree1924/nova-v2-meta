## A — Exam authority for the special driving conditions and your vehicle section (Tennessee Class D knowledge test)
Issuer: the Tennessee Department of Safety and Homeland Security, Driver Services Division, publishes the Tennessee Comprehensive Driver License Manual and takes written comments on it [A01].
Edition statement: the manual says its information was current as of July 1, 2022, and later law, rule or policy changes override anything outdated in it [A01].
Freshness: that content date is old and no issuer page confirms it as the current edition, so every fact here is held under freshness debt until rechecked (verify TN-SPEC-A-FRESH).
Knowledge test: a multiple-choice exam drawn from Sections B and C of the manual, split roughly equally across traffic signs and signals, safe driving principles, rules of the road, and drugs and alcohol [A02].
Format: given on computerized testing machines at Driver Service Centers, with Spanish, Korean and Japanese versions where computerized testing is offered, and oral tests by appointment for applicants who cannot read [A02].
Vision: a screening of at least 20/40 in each eye and in both eyes together, with or without corrective lenses, comes before any license or permit [A02].
Retake rule: after failing any knowledge test you must wait at least one day before trying again [A03].
Question count, passing score and time limit: the manual states none of them, so all three stay unknown and are never shown to learners (verify TN-SPEC-A-EXAM).
This section feeds the knowledge test: this chapter sits in Section C of the manual, which the exam page names alongside Section B as the source of the knowledge test, and most closely matches the safe driving principles area; it covers collision avoidance by stopping, turning or speeding up, braking with and without antilock brakes, evasive steering, animal and deer crashes, the causes of crashes, driver fitness, vehicle maintenance checks and road and traffic conditions; no chapter sample questions are printed in these pages.
Practice quizzes follow the product mastery policy from the learning loop, which is not an official exam threshold.
## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: special driving conditions and your vehicle
Audience: every first-time Tennessee Class D or learner permit applicant, including new drivers who will soon be responsible for maintaining their own car.
Why it matters to a first-timer: emergency braking and evasive steering [A01], fitness to drive [A04] and basic vehicle checks such as tread and tire pressure [A05] decide whether a new driver can escape or prevent a crash.
Level: procedure recall (brake and steer sequences) plus checklist recall for health and maintenance, with a few exact values such as the hand positions, the seizure-free period and the tread depth; moderate load.
Prerequisites: the vehicle familiarity chapter on controls and equipment and the night and bad-weather chapter on skids and winter braking.
