## A — Exam authority for the application documents, fees, renewal and address rules section (Tennessee Class D knowledge test)
Issuer: the Tennessee Department of Safety and Homeland Security, Driver Services Division, publishes the Tennessee Comprehensive Driver License Manual and takes written comments on it [A01].
Edition statement: the manual says its information was current as of July 1, 2022, and later law, rule or policy changes override anything outdated in it [A01].
Freshness: that content date is old and no issuer page confirms it as the current edition, so every fact here is held under freshness debt until rechecked (verify TN-APP-A-FRESH).
Knowledge test: a multiple-choice exam drawn from Sections B and C of the manual, split roughly equally across traffic signs and signals, safe driving principles, rules of the road, and drugs and alcohol [A02].
Format: given on computerized testing machines at Driver Service Centers, with Spanish, Korean and Japanese versions where computerized testing is offered, and oral tests by appointment for applicants who cannot read [A02].
Vision: a screening of at least 20/40 in each eye and in both eyes together, with or without corrective lenses, comes before any license or permit [A02].
Retake rule: after failing any knowledge test you must wait at least one day before trying again [A03].
Question count, passing score and time limit: the manual states none of them, so all three stay unknown and are never shown to learners (verify TN-APP-A-EXAM).
This section feeds the knowledge test: what every first-time applicant must bring (citizenship or legal presence, two identity items, two residency proofs, a Social Security number or affidavit, and the teen school and driving certifications), plus fees, new-resident rules, renewal, late fees and the address-change deadline.

## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: application documents, fees, renewal and address rules
Audience: first-time Tennessee Class D applicants and their parents assembling documents, plus new and returning residents converting an out-of-state license.
Why it matters to a first-timer: a missing original document ends the visit before any test, so the checklist [A01] and the new-resident deadlines [A05] decide whether the applicant can test at all.

Misconceptions: an out-of-state license proves who you are but not that you are a citizen; telling the Post Office about a move does not update your Department of Safety record; an expired license has no grace period; photocopies are refused even when they look official.
Mnemonics: two and two — two identity items and two residency proofs; Primary first for originals; report a new address before ten days pass.
Glossary: Primary identification means a document such as a certified birth certificate or passport that can stand first; Secondary identification means supporting items such as work IDs or tax forms; TDL and TID mean the temporary license and ID tied to authorized stay; Code Thirty means the military non-expiring license code.
Dynamic facts: every fee, the payment methods, websites and mailing addresses can change; confirm them on the Department of Safety and Homeland Security website before showing them to learners.
