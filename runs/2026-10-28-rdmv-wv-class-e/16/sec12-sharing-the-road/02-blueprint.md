## C — Sharing the road (part one)

FACT_ID: sec12-F01 | anchor: A01
Vehicle-pedestrian collisions account for nearly twenty percent of traffic fatalities, so drivers must use extreme care near special hazards such as school zones, bus stops, playgrounds and parks, and may sound the horn to warn pedestrians when necessary [A01].

FACT_ID: sec12-F02 | anchor: A01
Pedestrians have the right-of-way when they are in a crosswalk, when they cross a sidewalk that crosses an alley entrance or driveway, and when a blind person crosses with a white or metal cane, with or without a red tip, or led by a guide dog [A01].

FACT_ID: sec12-F03 | anchor: A01
Pedestrians must use crosswalks at intersections, look both ways before crossing, walk on the left side facing oncoming traffic and on the shoulder if possible where there is no sidewalk, obey walk and don't walk signals, and at night wear white or light-colored clothing or carry a light [A01].

FACT_ID: sec12-F05 | anchor: A01

## C — Sharing the road (part two)

FACT_ID: sec12-F17 | anchor: A03
At a corner a motorcycle is hard to see because parked vehicles and trees hide it and you are normally looking for cars and trucks, so check carefully as you leave the corner and keep looking all the way across the intersection [A03].
Source: [A03 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0062]

FACT_ID: sec12-F18 | anchor: A03
When following a motorcycle stay farther back than you would behind a car because it can slow or stop much faster, and when passing go all the way into the other lane, since the rider has the legal right to the entire lane and may need to swerve, then wait until you are well down the road before moving back [A03].
Source: [A03 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0062]

FACT_ID: sec12-F19 | anchor: A03
Slow-moving vehicles such as farm vehicles and construction equipment are often driven on or along highways and must display the triangular slow-moving vehicle emblem, and regular traffic must allow for their slow speed and make sure a pass is safe before attempting it [A03].
Source: [A03 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0062]

FACT_ID: sec12-F20 | anchor: A03
Heavy trucks, which typically weigh eighty thousand pounds and are driven by trained specially licensed drivers, need three times a car's stopping distance when fully loaded at fifty-five miles per hour, are longer, heavier and harder to maneuver, need much more room to turn, and have larger blind spots [A03].
Source: [A03 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0062]

FACT_ID: sec12-F21 | anchor: A03
A truck's blind spots, called the No-Zone, are dangerous because the truck driver cannot see vehicles there, the larger the vehicle the larger the blind spots, you should not stay in the No-Zone longer than needed to pass safely, and if you cannot see the driver's reflection in the truck's side mirror you are in a blind spot [A03].
Source: [A03 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0062]

FACT_ID: sec12-F22 | anchor: A04
Animal riders may use most public highways but not interstates, they have the same rights and duties as motor vehicle users, and a driver approaching a horse must take every precaution against frightening it and must never sound the horn near a horse [A04].
Source: [A04 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0063]

FACT_ID: sec12-F23 | anchor: A04
When passing a bicyclist slow down, make sure the rider is aware of you and leave plenty of room, wait for approaching traffic to pass if there is no room, dim your lights for an oncoming bicyclist at night, and take extra care in the morning and afternoon when bicyclists travel between home, work, school or play [A04].
Source: [A04 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0063]

FACT_ID: sec12-F24 | anchor: A04
Bicyclists are expected to know and obey all traffic laws, but whether or not a bicyclist is riding lawfully you always give the rider the benefit of the doubt, because a bicycle is small and light and almost any collision will injure or kill the rider [A04].
Source: [A04 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0063]

FACT_ID: sec12-F25 | anchor: A04
Truck safety practices include respecting the differences between trucks and cars, avoiding the No-Zone, giving trucks plenty of room including extra room for right turns and for backing up, allowing two seconds after passing before merging right in dry conditions and at least four to six seconds in wet conditions, and calling the authorities about unsafe truck driving [A04].
Source: [A04 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0063]

FACT_ID: sec12-F26 | anchor: A04
Never linger in the No-Zone, never underestimate a truck's size or speed, never slam on your brakes in front of a truck, never cut off a truck to reach an exit or turn, never tailgate a truck, and never pass a truck on the right during a turn [A04].
Source: [A04 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0063]

FACT_ID: sec12-F27 | anchor: A05
Bicyclists must signal turns and stops unless both hands are needed on the handlebars for balance, keep within a single lane when riding side by side without holding up traffic, never hang onto another vehicle, yield to pedestrians, obey the same rules as other drivers, and ride in the same direction as other vehicles [A05].
Source: [A05 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0064]

FACT_ID: sec12-F28 | anchor: A05
A bicyclist may carry a passenger only on an attached seat and may not carry anything that interferes with control of the bicycle or prevents keeping at least one hand on the handlebars [A05].
Source: [A05 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0064]

FACT_ID: sec12-F29 | anchor: A05
Every bicycle must have a red rear reflector, brakes strong enough to stop one wheel on dry, level, clean pavement, a bell or other device audible for at least one hundred feet with no siren or whistle permitted, and for riding after dark a white headlight visible at least five hundred feet to the front [A05].
Source: [A05 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0064]

FACT_ID: sec12-F30 | anchor: A05
Be alert for joggers in unexpected places such as curves, hilltops and corners, and expect heavy jogging traffic near parks and jogging paths [A05].
Source: [A05 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0064]

FACT_ID: sec12-F31 | anchor: A05
Be extra careful in residential areas, school zones and wherever children are likely to be, teach your own children the rights and duties of pedestrians early, and watch for children who may have run behind the vehicle when backing in or out of a driveway [A05].
Source: [A05 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0064]

FACT_ID: sec12-F32 | anchor: A05
Traffic does not have to stop for a public bus, but you should watch for passengers crossing the street as they get on and off, look out for children who may forget the rule, and use caution when passing a bus that is loading or unloading [A05].
Source: [A05 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0064]
