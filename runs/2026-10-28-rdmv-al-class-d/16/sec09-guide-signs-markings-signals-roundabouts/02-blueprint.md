## A — Exam authority for the Alabama driver license knowledge test

Issuer: the Alabama Law Enforcement Agency, Driver License Division, issues Alabama driver licenses [A07] and publishes the Alabama Driver Manual.
Edition: the front cover of the manual prints the edition line "November 2024" under the Driver License Division name [A01]; no effective date is printed, so the effective date stays unconfirmed (verify — sec09-A-EFFECTIVE-DATE).
Exam path: the manual describes a vision screening, a knowledge test with questions on Alabama traffic laws, road signs and safe-driving rules taken from this manual, and a road test given by a driver license examiner [A13].
Learner path: a fifteen-year-old who passes the required exam receives a Class D learner license with the Y restriction [A11].
Delivery: automated testing is available to every applicant taking the knowledge test [A13], and oral and foreign-language versions exist [A11].
Scoring: the manual states no question count, no passing score or percent, and no time limit for the knowledge test; all three stay unknown and no number is asserted here (verify — sec09-A-SCORING).
Question format: beyond automated delivery, the manual does not describe the item format of the knowledge test (verify — sec09-A-FORMAT).
Section link: this dossier section covers guide sign colors and mileposts, centerline, lane, edge and crosswalk markings, steady and arrow traffic signals, railroad signals, lane use control signals, and how to drive one-lane and two-lane roundabouts, all drawn from the manual the test is built on [A13].
Practice mastery targets in this product are product policy from the accepted learning loop, never an official passing line.
Dynamic facts (updatable): the knowledge test fee, office locations, appointments and online services are set by ALEA and can change between editions, so they are kept out of stable knowledge [A13]; why_dynamic: administrative details outside the printed rules of the road.
## B — Audience and scope

Audience: first-time Alabama applicants who have never held a license and are preparing in English for the driver license knowledge test; this section matters most to first-time applicants learning to read road markings and signals, and to drivers meeting Alabama's growing number of roundabouts.
Level: plain-English reading level; no prior driving vocabulary is assumed, so each term from the manual is defined where it first appears.
Prerequisites: the sign types and shape and color coding of the previous section.
Section scope: manual chapter five, second part: the roundabout warning sign, guide sign colors, highway route markers and mileposts, the four types of pavement markings, centerlines and passing lines, lane dividers, edge striping, solid, wide and double white lines, crosswalks, pavement messages, two-lane and multi-lane roadway marking patterns, the two-way left turn lane, steady red, yellow and green signals, railroad flashing lights and gates, green and yellow turn arrows, lane use control signals, and the general rules, steps, pedestrian and bicycle rules, emergency-vehicle rules, truck aprons and two-lane lane choice for roundabouts.
Out of scope: commercial, motorcycle-manual and vessel testing content, office logistics and fees, and anything not printed on the selected manual pages.
