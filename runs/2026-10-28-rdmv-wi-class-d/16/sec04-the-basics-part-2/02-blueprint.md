## A — Exam authority for the the basics part two: turning, passing, backing, parking and communication section (Wisconsin Class D regular driver license knowledge test)
Issuer: the Wisconsin Department of Transportation and its Division of Motor Vehicles (DMV), which offers the Motorists Handbook as a resource that helps train and qualify new drivers [A03].
Source document: Wisconsin Motorists Handbook, with 2026 printed on the front cover [A01]; the back cover carries form number BDS126 with 2026 [A04].
Knowledge test: 50 questions on traffic laws, and 40 correct answers (80 percent) are needed to pass [A02].
Highway signs test: a separate test of 15 questions, with 12 correct answers (80 percent) needed to pass [A02].
Vision screening: regular car and motorcycle applicants need visual acuity of at least 20/40 in one eye, corrected or not, and a 70-degree field of vision from the center of each eye [A02].
Duration: the handbook says the knowledge test takes about 45 minutes to finish, but it states no formal time limit [A02].
Time limit: unknown, so no limit is ever shown to learners (verify WI-MANEUVER-A-TIME).
Delivery: the signs and knowledge tests need no appointment and are free at a DMV customer service center; applicants aged 15 to 17 can also sit the knowledge test online for a $10 fee [A02].
This section feeds the knowledge test: turning lanes, passing limits, hill and parallel parking, headlight rules and signaling distances are traffic-law content for the knowledge test, and the Y-turn, parking and backing also appear on the skills test.
Practice quizzes follow the product mastery policy from the learning loop, which is not an official exam threshold.

Misconceptions: keeping the wheel turned while waiting to turn left is dangerous, because a rear-end hit would push you into oncoming traffic; a paved shoulder is not a passing lane except around a stopped or left-turning vehicle; mirrors and a backup camera are not enough when backing; missing an exit never justifies backing up; uphill with a curb is the one hill case where the wheels turn left.
Mnemonics: uphill with a curb, wheels left; every other hill case, wheels right; pass when both headlights show in the mirror; Y-turn is right, left, back, go; left arm straight for left, bent up for right, down for stop.
Glossary: U-turn means one continuous U-shaped turn reversing direction; Y-turn means a multi-step turnaround on a narrow street; blind spot means an area beside you that mirrors do not show; parallel parking means parking in line between two vehicles along the curb; low beams means normal headlights; high beams means brights; hazards means flashing warning lights.
Dynamic facts: whether a particular intersection allows a U-turn depends on local signs, so always check the posted signs where you drive.
