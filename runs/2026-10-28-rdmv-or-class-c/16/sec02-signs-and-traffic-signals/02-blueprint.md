## A — Exam authority for the signs and traffic signals section (Oregon Class C non-commercial knowledge test)
Issuer: Oregon Driver and Motor Vehicle Services (DMV), part of the Oregon Department of Transportation, publishes the Oregon Driver Manual to help drivers get ready for Oregon licensing tests [A04].
Source document: Oregon Driver Manual, with the edition span 2026 - 2027 printed on the front cover [A01].
Scope: the manual covers only the Class C non-commercial driving privilege [A03].
Knowledge test: the Class C knowledge test has 35 multiple-choice questions [A02].
Passing standard: you must answer 28 of the 35 questions correctly, which is exactly 80 percent [A02].
Delivery: the knowledge test can be taken online through the DMV online service or at a DMV office, and the office version is offered in several languages with or without audio [A02].
Time limit: the manual does not state one, so it stays unknown and is never shown to learners (verify OR-SIGN-A-TIME).
This section feeds the knowledge test: road signs are named on the knowledge test paragraph as a test topic; this section covers the obey-all-signs rule, prohibitive and other regulatory signs, transit and HOV lane signs, yellow warning signs, route, guide, safety corridor, recreation and service signs, and every steady, flashing, arrow and dark signal.

## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: signs and traffic signals
Audience: every first-time Oregon Class C applicant; sign and signal meanings are core knowledge for any new driver and the knowledge test names road signs as a topic.
Why it matters to a first-timer: each sign family has its own color and shape that must be recognized at a glance [A01], and signal lights decide who may move through an intersection, including when a turn on red is allowed [A09].
Level: high-volume recognition and recall with a visual component; moderate load, and sign recognition needs pictures rather than words alone.
Prerequisites: the testing chapter; right-of-way detail follows in the turns and intersections section.

Misconceptions: a dark signal is not a free pass, it works like a stop sign on every approach; a flashing yellow arrow is not a protected turn, oncoming traffic has a green light; a yellow light is a warning to stop if you safely can, not a signal to speed up; a red arrow still allows the same turns on red as a red ball.
Mnemonics: red forbids, yellow warns, white rules, green guides, blue serves, brown shows the sights; eight sides stop you, three sides yield.
Glossary: regulatory sign means a sign that sets a legal rule; warning sign means a yellow sign about a hazard ahead; advisory speed means a posted safe speed for a ramp or curve; safety corridor means a signed highway stretch with a high serious-crash record.
Dynamic facts: electronic speed and advisory signs change with conditions, and safety corridor designations can change; confirm on Oregon DMV and ODOT websites.
