## A — Exam authority for the road signs: warning, regulatory, construction, destination, service, mile marker, route number and railroad crossing signs section (Wisconsin Class D regular driver license knowledge test)
Issuer: the Wisconsin Department of Transportation and its Division of Motor Vehicles (DMV), which offers the Motorists Handbook as a resource that helps train and qualify new drivers [A03].
Source document: Wisconsin Motorists Handbook, with 2026 printed on the front cover [A01]; the back cover carries form number BDS126 with 2026 [A04].
Knowledge test: 50 questions on traffic laws, and 40 correct answers (80 percent) are needed to pass [A02].
Highway signs test: a separate test of 15 questions, with 12 correct answers (80 percent) needed to pass [A02].
Vision screening: regular car and motorcycle applicants need visual acuity of at least 20/40 in one eye, corrected or not, and a 70-degree field of vision from the center of each eye [A02].
Duration: the handbook says the knowledge test takes about 45 minutes to finish, but it states no formal time limit [A02].
Time limit: unknown, so no limit is ever shown to learners (verify WI-SIGNS-A-TIME).
Delivery: the signs and knowledge tests need no appointment and are free at a DMV customer service center; applicants aged 15 to 17 can also sit the knowledge test online for a $10 fee [A02].
This section feeds the knowledge test: sign colors, shapes and required actions are the content of the separate fifteen-question highway signs test, and sign rules such as stop, yield, no passing and railroad crossings also appear on the knowledge test.
Practice quizzes follow the product mastery policy from the learning loop, which is not an official exam threshold.
## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: road signs: warning, regulatory, construction, destination, service, mile marker, route number and railroad crossing signs
Audience: every first-time Wisconsin Class D applicant, since the highway signs test is taken alongside the knowledge test.
Why it matters to a first-timer: the chapter teaches the color and shape code for warning signs [A01], the work-zone rules that double fines and limit phone use [A05], and the railroad crossing rules and sign types [A09].
Level: mostly visual recognition plus short rules; many look-alike colors and shapes make it a confusion-heavy chapter.
Prerequisites: the basics sections on right-of-way and speed; no earlier sign knowledge is assumed.

Misconceptions: a worker or officer directing traffic outranks the light or sign, not the other way around; the speed limit is a maximum for good conditions, not a speed you are owed in bad weather; a crossing whose lights are dark still needs a look both ways; a missing number-of-tracks sign does not prove there is only one track; a work zone allows phone use only to report an emergency.
Mnemonics: yellow warns, red and white rules, orange means workers, green and brown guide you, blue serves you; eight sides means stop, an upside-down triangle means yield, a pennant means no passing, an orange triangle means slow mover; badge for U.S., milk jug for state, red-and-blue shield for interstate.
Glossary: regulatory sign means a sign stating a rule you must obey; warning sign means a yellow sign alerting you to a hazard ahead; crossbuck means the X-shaped railroad crossing sign; pennant means the sideways triangle used for no passing zones; flagger means a work-zone worker directing traffic with a sign or flag; reference marker means a mile marker sign.
Dynamic facts: the distance on a Road Work Ahead sign and the miles on a brown destination sign change from sign to sign, so read the value posted rather than memorizing one.
