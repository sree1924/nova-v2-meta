## A — Exam authority for the Alabama driver license knowledge test

Issuer: the Alabama Law Enforcement Agency, Driver License Division, issues Alabama driver licenses [A07] and publishes the Alabama Driver Manual.
Edition: the front cover of the manual prints the edition line "November 2024" under the Driver License Division name [A01]; no effective date is printed, so the effective date stays unconfirmed (verify — sec08-A-EFFECTIVE-DATE).
Exam path: the manual describes a vision screening, a knowledge test with questions on Alabama traffic laws, road signs and safe-driving rules taken from this manual, and a road test given by a driver license examiner [A13].
Learner path: a fifteen-year-old who passes the required exam receives a Class D learner license with the Y restriction [A11].
Delivery: automated testing is available to every applicant taking the knowledge test [A13], and oral and foreign-language versions exist [A11].
Scoring: the manual states no question count, no passing score or percent, and no time limit for the knowledge test; all three stay unknown and no number is asserted here (verify — sec08-A-SCORING).
Question format: beyond automated delivery, the manual does not describe the item format of the knowledge test (verify — sec08-A-FORMAT).
Section link: this dossier section covers the three sign types and their shape and color coding, regulatory signs including the four-way stop, yield, wrong-way, turn, lane-use, parking and speed signs, and warning signs for road conditions, crossings, cyclists and work zones, all drawn from the manual the test is built on [A13].
Practice mastery targets in this product are product policy from the accepted learning loop, never an official passing line.
Dynamic facts (updatable): the knowledge test fee, office locations, appointments and online services are set by ALEA and can change between editions, so they are kept out of stable knowledge [A13]; why_dynamic: administrative details outside the printed rules of the road.
## B — Audience and scope

Audience: first-time Alabama applicants who have never held a license and are preparing in English for the driver license knowledge test; this section matters most to every first-time applicant, because sign meanings are core knowledge-test material, and to drivers new to work zones and shared bicycle lanes.
Level: plain-English reading level; no prior driving vocabulary is assumed, so each term from the manual is defined where it first appears.
Prerequisites: the turning and parking rules of earlier sections, which several regulatory signs enforce.
Section scope: manual chapter five, first part: why drivers must know signs, signals and markings, symbol signs, shape and color coding, the three sign types, regulatory signs with the four-way stop order, yield, wrong-way and one-way entry, railroad, turn prohibitions, no parking, handicapped parking, emergency stopping, lane-use, speed limit and minimum speed, school zone, no passing, no turn on red, keep-right and one-way signs, and warning signs for curves, side roads, bumps, shoulders, narrowing roads, bridges, controls ahead, advisory speeds, railroads, lane ends, merges, clearance, two-way traffic, slippery roads, animals, pedestrians, schools, bicycles, sharrows and work zones with flashing arrow panels.
Out of scope: commercial, motorcycle-manual and vessel testing content, office logistics and fees, and anything not printed on the selected manual pages.
