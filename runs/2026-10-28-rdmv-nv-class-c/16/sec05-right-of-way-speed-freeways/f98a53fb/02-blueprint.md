## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: right-of-way, speed control, freeway driving, anti-lock brakes and stopping
Audience: every first-time Nevada Basic Class C applicant; right-of-way and speed rules are central traffic-law content.
Why it matters to a first-timer: the handbook names failure to yield as the leading crash cause in Nevada [A01], and rear-end collisions as the most common crash type, which ties speed and following space to stopping distance [A07].
Level: rule recall with several situational orderings (who goes first), a set of example speed limits, and a few distance and timing values; moderate conceptual load around braking physics.
Prerequisites: sec04 for signs, signals and lane markings, since the yielding and freeway rules build on them.

Misconceptions: having the right-of-way does not let you force your way through, the law only names who must yield; the posted limit is a maximum for good conditions, not a safe speed in every condition; stopping on the on-ramp before merging is usually more dangerous, not safer; with four-wheel ABS you hold the pedal down hard instead of pumping it; doubling speed quadruples braking distance rather than doubling it.
Mnemonics: yield right, yield to whoever is already in; red X closed, yellow X careful or merge, green arrow open; with ABS, stomp, stay and steer; twice the speed, four times the braking.
Glossary: Basic Rule means driving at a speed reasonable for current conditions whatever the posted limit; ramp meter means a signal on an on-ramp that lets cars onto the freeway one at a time; HOV lane means a high-occupancy carpool lane; deceleration lane means the exit lane where you slow down; ABS means anti-lock braking system; hydroplaning means tires riding on water and losing grip.
Dynamic facts: HOV lane hours, posted speed limits and any overhead variable speed limits change by segment and by day, so read the posted and overhead signs.
