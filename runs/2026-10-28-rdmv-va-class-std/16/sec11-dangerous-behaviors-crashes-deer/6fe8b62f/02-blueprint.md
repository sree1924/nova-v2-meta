## C — Dangerous behaviors, crashes, deer

FACT_ID: sec11-F01 | anchor: A01
Cap long drives with a rest stop at least every two hours, nap 10 to 20 minutes at a safe spot, share wheel time with a companion while a rested person drives, and shun the 10 p.m. to 6 a.m. window [A01].
Source: [A01 -> src:rdmv-va-s:virginia-department-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0026]

FACT_ID: sec11-F02 | anchor: A01
Cracked windows, gum, loud radio, and caffeine or energy drinks cannot fend off drowsiness; drowsy driving counts as impaired driving, and a drowsy driver you spot deserves a safe stop or a passenger's call to police with location, vehicle, and actions [A01].
Source: [A01 -> src:rdmv-va-s:virginia-department-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0026]

FACT_ID: sec11-F03 | anchor: A01
Drivers twenty-one and up reach DUI at .08 BAC or higher, with impairment alone able to convict lower; under twenty-one, .02 up to .08 means illegal consumption and .08 or more means DUI; drug impairment draws DUI-grade penalties [A01].
Source: [A01 -> src:rdmv-va-s:virginia-department-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0026]

FACT_ID: sec11-F04 | anchor: A01
From 10 PM to 2 AM three of ten drivers run drunk, over a third coming from someone's home; nearly 50 percent of DUI arrests hit social-to-moderate drinkers; a lifetime alcohol-crash coin flip faces you; one drink dents vision, judgment, and coordination and multiplies crash odds seven times over sober [A01].
Source: [A01 -> src:rdmv-va-s:virginia-department-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0026]

FACT_ID: sec11-F05 | anchor: A01
Twelve ounces of beer equal a liquor shot or a five-ounce wine glass; only passing time sobers, never coffee, cold showers, or workouts; since drink erodes the very judgment needed, lock in the no-drive call before the first sip [A01].
Source: [A01 -> src:rdmv-va-s:virginia-department-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0026]

FACT_ID: sec11-F06 | anchor: A01
Driving wants your whole attention: shelve phones, computers, and like devices except to report a crash or emergency, and pull fully over before any distracting act [A01].
Source: [A01 -> src:rdmv-va-s:virginia-department-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0026]

FACT_ID: sec11-F07 | anchor: A01
Virginia bars holding a phone or wireless device at the wheel except in a driver emergency or while lawfully parked or stopped; navigation use stands only while entering nothing and holding nothing [A01].
Source: [A01 -> src:rdmv-va-s:virginia-department-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0026]

FACT_ID: sec11-F08 | anchor: A01
Distraction causes roughly 17 percent of Virginia crashes: the straying car enters peril, recognition lags precious seconds, and an emergency move follows; young distracteds add weak control, tailgating, and yield failures to raw inexperience [A01].
Source: [A01 -> src:rdmv-va-s:virginia-department-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0026]

FACT_ID: sec11-F09 | anchor: A01
Dodge distraction by fixing eyes on the road with hands on the wheel, presetting every control before rolling, and leaving the cell phone alone [A01].
Source: [A01 -> src:rdmv-va-s:virginia-department-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0026]

FACT_ID: sec11-F10 | anchor: A01
Yawns, nods, leaden lids, lost miles, skipped signs, stray lanes, shoulder runs, and rumble hits flag drowsiness, which clouds judgment and can drop you asleep; bank sleep first, skip booze and heavy meals, and mind dulling medicines [A01].
Source: [A01 -> src:rdmv-va-s:virginia-department-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0026]

FACT_ID: sec11-F11 | anchor: A02
Before a drinking event, book a sober ride via alternate transport or a sober driver; alcohol stacked on drugs multiplies both, so a single drink atop even aspirin or cold medicine can hit like several [A02].
Source: [A02 -> src:rdmv-va-s:virginia-department-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0027]

FACT_ID: sec11-F12 | anchor: A02
Nearly every drug can dull driving, legal ones included: headache, hay fever, cold, allergy, and nerve pills, syrups, drops, sprays, and tablets slow you; marijuana stays DUI-illegal behind the wheel past twenty-one; read labels, quiz your pharmacist or doctor, and treat no drug as harmless [A02].
Source: [A02 -> src:rdmv-va-s:virginia-department-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0027]

FACT_ID: sec11-F13 | anchor: A02
In a crash, halt at the scene or near it without blocking traffic, clear drivable cars when nobody is hurt and it is safe, step out shielded by your car, help the hurt without moving them absent training or fire danger, and summon police fast since property, injury, and fatal crashes all require reports [A02].
Source: [A02 -> src:rdmv-va-s:virginia-department-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0027]

FACT_ID: sec11-F14 | anchor: A02
Trade names, addresses, license and plate numbers, injured and witness contacts, and owners' insurance policy numbers on the spot, ring your insurer at once, and know officers file DMV reports on any injury or death and damage over $3,000, entered on every involved driver's record [A02].
Source: [A02 -> src:rdmv-va-s:virginia-department-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0027]

FACT_ID: sec11-F15 | anchor: A02
After striking an empty car or property, hunt reasonably for its owner, else pin an easy-to-find note carrying your name, phone, date, time, and damage sketch, and still tell police within 24 hours [A02].
Source: [A02 -> src:rdmv-va-s:virginia-department-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0027]

FACT_ID: sec11-F16 | anchor: A02
Tens of thousands of Virginia deer, elk, and bear strikes land yearly: stay sharp at dusk and dawn through fall, brake for grouped animals near the road, horn them off, and when contact is certain hold the lane under firm braking to a controlled stop instead of swerving, then report a large-animal hit to police [A02].
Source: [A02 -> src:rdmv-va-s:virginia-department-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0027]
