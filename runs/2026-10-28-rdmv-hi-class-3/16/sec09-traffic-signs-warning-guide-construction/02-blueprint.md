Misconceptions: a yield sign requires a stop only when other traffic has the right of way, while a stop sign always requires a complete stop; a reduced speed ahead sign is only a notice, and the new limit begins at the next speed limit sign; the no passing zone pennant stands on the left side of the road, not the right; orange is the work zone color, while yellow is the general warning color; barricade stripes sloping down to the right mean bear right.

Mnemonics: Red prohibits, yellow warns, orange means work, black and white commands, green blue and brown guide; eight sides stop, three sides yield, a pennant on the left says no passing; stripes slope the way you go.

Glossary: regulatory sign (a sign requiring an action, typically black on white or red on white); exclusion sign (a red circle, with or without a slash, that prohibits something); pennant (the sideways triangle used only for no passing zones); channelizing device (a barricade, panel, drum, cone or tube that steers traffic through a work zone); flagperson (a worker in orange who controls traffic with a flag or paddle); sequencing arrow panel (a lighted panel whose arrow steps toward the lane to use).

Dynamic facts: none; sign colors, shapes and work zone conventions are national standards printed as stable knowledge in this edition.
