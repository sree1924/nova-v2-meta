## A — Exam authority for the accidents and emergency situations section (Indiana learner's permit and driver's license knowledge exam)
Issuer: the Indiana Bureau of Motor Vehicles (BMV) publishes the Indiana Driver's Manual; both names appear on the front cover [A02].
Source document: the front cover prints no edition; the back cover reads Published July 2026 and points readers to IN.gov/BMV for updates [A04].
Knowledge exam: a multiple-choice, computer-based exam drawn from Indiana law, defensive driving practices and the manual, which also tests reading of regulatory, warning and traffic signs and signals [A01].
Exam structure: the exam has two components, one on common signs (Chapter 6) and one on traffic rules and laws (Chapters 6, 7 and 8) [A03].
Passing standard: 80 percent or higher correct on each component [A03].
Branch logistics: arrive at least 30 minutes before the branch closes to test that day; after a failure, wait until the next business day to retake [A01].
Question count: the manual gives no number of questions for either component or in total, so it stays unknown and is never shown to learners (verify IN-CRASH-A-COUNT).
Time limit: the manual does not state one, so it stays unknown (verify IN-CRASH-A-TIME).

## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: accidents and emergency situations
Audience: first-time Indiana permit and license applicants who need to know their legal duties after a crash and the safe responses to common emergencies.
Why it matters to a first-timer: the duties to stop, move the vehicle, exchange information and help the injured are legal requirements [A01], emergency escapes such as a car in water are counterintuitive [A02], and traffic stop conduct protects both driver and officer [A04].
Level: duty and procedure recall (what you must do, in what order) plus judgment about collision-avoidance choices.
Prerequisites: the points and insurance chapter for the Certificate of Compliance and chemical test penalties; the safe-operation chapter for skids and warning devices.
