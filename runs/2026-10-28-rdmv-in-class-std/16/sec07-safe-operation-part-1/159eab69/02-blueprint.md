## A — Exam authority for the safe vehicle operation part one (lanes, passing, turning, speed, weather, night, impaired, distracted and aggressive driving, rural roads and interstates) section (Indiana learner's permit and driver's license knowledge exam)
Issuer: the Indiana Bureau of Motor Vehicles (BMV) publishes the Indiana Driver's Manual; both names appear on the front cover [A02].
Source document: the front cover prints no edition; the back cover reads Published July 2026 and points readers to IN.gov/BMV for updates [A04].
Knowledge exam: a multiple-choice, computer-based exam drawn from Indiana law, defensive driving practices and the manual, which also tests reading of regulatory, warning and traffic signs and signals [A01].
Exam structure: the exam has two components, one on common signs (Chapter 6) and one on traffic rules and laws (Chapters 6, 7 and 8) [A03].
Passing standard: 80 percent or higher correct on each component [A03].
Branch logistics: arrive at least 30 minutes before the branch closes to test that day; after a failure, wait until the next business day to retake [A01].
Question count: the manual gives no number of questions for either component or in total, so it stays unknown and is never shown to learners (verify IN-SAFEONE-A-COUNT).
Time limit: the manual does not state one, so it stays unknown (verify IN-SAFEONE-A-TIME).
This section feeds the knowledge exam: this is the first half of the safe-operation chapter, which the traffic rules and laws component draws on together with the sign and emergency chapters.

## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: safe vehicle operation part one (lanes, passing, turning, speed, weather, night, impaired, distracted and aggressive driving, rural roads and interstates)
Audience: first-time Indiana permit and license applicants learning the everyday rules of the road, including teens who will practice under supervision.
Why it matters to a first-timer: no-passing places and passing distances [A02], the statewide speed limits [A04] and the headlight and low-beam distances [A08] are concrete rules a new driver must apply on the first drive.
Level: rule and number recall (speed limits, distances, seconds of following gap) plus judgment items on right of way, skids and weather.
Prerequisites: the traffic signs and signals chapter for sign meanings and signal right of way; the points chapter for the penalties tied to these behaviors.

Misconceptions: a broken yellow line does not mean passing is always safe, it only allows passing when the way is clear; with anti-lock brakes you do not pump the pedal; the faster lane is for passing, not for cruising slowly; entering an interstate does not give you the right of way over traffic already on it; high beams make fog worse, not better.
Mnemonics: yellow divides opposite directions, white divides the same direction; seventy rural interstate, sixty rural divided, fifty-five urban and county, thirty residential, fifteen alleys; low beams at five hundred feet facing and two hundred feet following; three acts make aggressive driving.
Glossary: blind spot means the area a mirror does not show, checked by a brief head turn; roundabout means a circular intersection where traffic flows counterclockwise and entering traffic yields; ABS means anti-lock brake system; hydroplaning means tires riding on a film of water, oil and dirt instead of the road; acceleration lane means the ramp lane used to reach highway speed before merging.
Dynamic facts: speed limits shown are statutory defaults that posted limits can override, and the school zone hours are described as common hours that local authorities can change; confirm posted limits on the road.
