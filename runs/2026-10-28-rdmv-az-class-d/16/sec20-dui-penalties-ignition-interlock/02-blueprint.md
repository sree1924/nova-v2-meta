Misconceptions: a BAC under the legal limit does not guarantee you will not be convicted; refusing a test does not avoid a penalty, since refusal brings its own automatic loss of privilege; extreme DUI is a separate, harsher category starting at a higher alcohol level; aggravated DUI is a prison offense, not just a longer jail term; an interlock must be blown into while driving as well as before starting.
Mnemonics: Point zero eight loses the license, point one five is extreme, point two zero is the top tier; refuse once, lose a year.
Glossary: implied consent means agreeing in advance to alcohol and drug testing by accepting a license; extreme DUI means DUI at a high alcohol concentration; aggravated DUI means a DUI with an aggravating factor such as a suspended license or a passenger under fifteen; ignition interlock means a breath tester that prevents the engine from starting.
Dynamic facts: DUI fines, jail minimums, revocation periods and interlock license eligibility are statutory and change often, so every penalty figure must be rechecked against current Arizona law before release.
