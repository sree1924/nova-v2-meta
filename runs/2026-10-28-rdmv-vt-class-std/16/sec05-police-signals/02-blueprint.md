Misconceptions:
- A yellow light means speed up to beat the red: false, it warns of the coming red, and only a driver too close to stop safely continues through with caution.
- A green light gives a left turning driver priority: false, the left turner yields to oncoming vehicles unless a solid green arrow is showing.
- Right on red is automatic: false, it requires a complete stop, a scan, yielding to pedestrians and vehicles, and no sign forbidding it.
- Only police and ambulances trigger the move over duty: false, maintenance vehicles like snowplows must be yielded to and amber lit tow trucks get the same caution.
- When police stop you, step out to speak with the officer: false, stay in the vehicle unless asked to get out.

Mnemonics:
- Eight sides stop, three sides yield, round for rails: the three basic sign shapes.
- Solid green goes, flashing yellow yields, red arrow rests: the three left turn arrows.
- Dark means stop sign all around: a signal with no lights is a four way stop.

Glossary:
- Move over law: the requirement to pull right and stop for approaching emergency vehicles and to slow down, and on four lane roads change lanes, for stopped emergency, police, maintenance or towing vehicles
- Flashing yellow arrow: a left turn signal that permits the turn only after yielding to oncoming traffic and pedestrians
- Protected left turn: a left turn made on a solid green arrow while oncoming traffic faces a red light
- Stop line: the painted line before an intersection where a driver facing a red light or stop sign must stop, ahead of the crosswalk
