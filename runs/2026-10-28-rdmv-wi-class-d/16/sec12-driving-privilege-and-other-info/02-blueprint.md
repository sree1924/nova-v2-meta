## A — Exam authority for the your driving privilege and other important information: points, habitual offenders, occupational licenses, reinstatement, seat belts, child seats and insurance section (Wisconsin Class D regular driver license knowledge test)
Issuer: the Wisconsin Department of Transportation and its Division of Motor Vehicles (DMV), which offers the Motorists Handbook as a resource that helps train and qualify new drivers [A03].
Source document: Wisconsin Motorists Handbook, with 2026 printed on the front cover [A01]; the back cover carries form number BDS126 with 2026 [A04].
Knowledge test: 50 questions on traffic laws, and 40 correct answers (80 percent) are needed to pass [A02].
Highway signs test: a separate test of 15 questions, with 12 correct answers (80 percent) needed to pass [A02].
Vision screening: regular car and motorcycle applicants need visual acuity of at least 20/40 in one eye, corrected or not, and a 70-degree field of vision from the center of each eye [A02].
Duration: the handbook says the knowledge test takes about 45 minutes to finish, but it states no formal time limit [A02].
Time limit: unknown, so no limit is ever shown to learners (verify WI-PRIVILEGE-A-TIME).
Delivery: the signs and knowledge tests need no appointment and are free at a DMV customer service center; applicants aged 15 to 17 can also sit the knowledge test online for a $10 fee [A02].
This section feeds the knowledge test: the demerit point threshold, habitual offender counts, occupational license limits, seat belt and child seat rules and the liability insurance duty are traffic-law content for the knowledge test, and the audio and language options apply to the knowledge and signs tests themselves.

Misconceptions: points are not just added up forever, the suspension trigger is reaching the threshold within a rolling year; you can lose a Wisconsin driving privilege without ever holding a Wisconsin license; an occupational license is not a full license and covers only work, school, household and treatment trips within daily and weekly hour caps; air bags do not replace seat belts; tucking the shoulder belt under your arm is wrong; borrowing a car does not shift the insurance duty to its owner alone.
Mnemonics: twelve points in twelve months means you are parked; four major or twelve minor in five years makes a habitual offender; the occupational license runs twelve a day and sixty a week; car seat to four, booster to eight; lap on the hips, shoulder on the chest, a fist of room.
Glossary: demerit points means points added to your record for traffic convictions; suspension means a temporary withdrawal of the driving privilege; revocation means a cancellation of the privilege that requires reinstatement; habitual traffic offender means a driver who reaches the major or minor conviction counts within five years; occupational license means a restricted license for essential trips; liability insurance means coverage for harm you cause to others.
Dynamic facts: the DMV website keeps the current lists of violation points, test languages and occupational license eligibility, and the driver education grant is described as a new program, so check those pages before relying on details.
