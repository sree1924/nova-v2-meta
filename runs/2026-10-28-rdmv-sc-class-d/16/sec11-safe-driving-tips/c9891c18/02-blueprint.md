Misconceptions: braking hard once you are already in a curve can cost traction, so the slowing happens before the curve; shallow-looking floodwater can still hide dips and wash out the road; total stopping distance includes the time to notice and react, not only braking; the four-second gap is a minimum, not a target, and grows in bad conditions; the horn is for communicating, not for venting anger.
Mnemonics: look twenty to thirty seconds ahead, follow at least four seconds behind; six inches stalls a car, two feet of moving water carries it away; perceive, react, then brake; signal one hundred feet before you move.
Glossary: visual search means a regular pattern of checking ahead, to the sides and behind; space cushion means the open space you keep around your vehicle; hydroplaning means tires riding on a film of water instead of the road; perception time means the time to recognize you must stop; reaction time means the time to move your foot to the brake; braking distance means the distance the brakes need to stop the vehicle; flash flood means a flood that arrives quickly after heavy rain.
