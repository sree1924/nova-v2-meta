## A — Exam authority for the railroad crossings, city, rural and interstate driving section (North Carolina regular Class C)
Issuer: the North Carolina Department of Transportation, Division of Motor Vehicles (NCDMV) publishes the handbook and administers the license tests [A01].
Source document: North Carolina Driver Handbook, back cover revision note Revised May 2025 [A03].
Test structure for an original license: vision check, traffic signs test, knowledge test on traffic laws and safe driving, and an on-road test, each passed separately [A02].
This section feeds the knowledge test: the railroad signal statute and crossing habits, the Blue Sign emergency system, bicycle lane, city, rural and secondary road hazards, interstate entry, exit, breakdown and wrong-way rules, and headlight and taillight standards.

Misconceptions: emergency vehicles do not outrank a train; a crossbuck with no gates still requires yielding; a gate coming down behind or ahead of you will not trap you, so keep moving; once one train passes, another may follow; on an interstate, a missed exit is never fixed by backing up; slowing before the deceleration lane is a mistake.
Mnemonics: slow, look, listen, alert, watch at every crossing; get out and get away, then find the Blue Sign; accelerate on the ramp, decelerate off the road.
Glossary: crossbuck means the X-shaped railroad crossing sign; Blue Sign means the Emergency Notification System sign with the crossing ID and railroad emergency phone; acceleration lane means the entry lane for matching interstate speed; deceleration lane means the exit lane for slowing down; secondary road means a local road not built for highway speeds.
Dynamic facts: emergency and toll phone numbers can change and are flagged as dynamic; confirm on the railroad sign itself or the official website.

FACT_ID: sec10-R01 | anchor: A01
The stopping zone at a railroad crossing is between 15 and 50 feet from the nearest rail, closer than 15 being too near and farther than 50 too far. [A01]

FACT_ID: sec10-R02 | anchor: A01

Why it matters: group crossing distances; confusable warning distance versus stopping distance.
Source: [A01 -> pdf-0062]

FACT_ID: sec10-R03 | anchor: A02
Before the signals start you stop short of the lowering gates, but if you are already on the tracks when they start you keep moving and do not stop or back up. [A02]
Why it matters: group gates; difficulty high; confusable approaching versus already crossing.
Source: [A02 -> pdf-0063]

FACT_ID: sec10-R04 | anchor: A02

Why it matters: group crossing controls; difficulty medium.
Source: [A02 -> pdf-0063]

FACT_ID: sec10-R05 | anchor: A03

Source: [A03 -> pdf-0064]

FACT_ID: sec10-R07 | anchor: A05
On entry you speed up in the acceleration lane to match traffic, while on exit you move into the deceleration lane before slowing down. [A05]

FACT_ID: sec10-R08 | anchor: A05

Why it matters: group interstate stops; difficulty low.
Source: [A05 -> pdf-0066]

FACT_ID: sec10-R09 | anchor: A06
A missed exit is handled by driving on to the next one, while entering the wrong way is handled by pulling onto the right shoulder, stopping and turning around when clear. [A06]
