## A — Exam authority for the sharing the road: pedestrians, emergency vehicles, school buses, motorcycles, bicycles and truck no-zones section (Wisconsin Class D regular driver license knowledge test)
Issuer: the Wisconsin Department of Transportation and its Division of Motor Vehicles (DMV), which offers the Motorists Handbook as a resource that helps train and qualify new drivers [A03].
Source document: Wisconsin Motorists Handbook, with 2026 printed on the front cover [A01]; the back cover carries form number BDS126 with 2026 [A04].
Knowledge test: 50 questions on traffic laws, and 40 correct answers (80 percent) are needed to pass [A02].
Highway signs test: a separate test of 15 questions, with 12 correct answers (80 percent) needed to pass [A02].
Vision screening: regular car and motorcycle applicants need visual acuity of at least 20/40 in one eye, corrected or not, and a 70-degree field of vision from the center of each eye [A02].
Duration: the handbook says the knowledge test takes about 45 minutes to finish, but it states no formal time limit [A02].
Time limit: unknown, so no limit is ever shown to learners (verify WI-SHARING-A-TIME).
Delivery: the signs and knowledge tests need no appointment and are free at a DMV customer service center; applicants aged 15 to 17 can also sit the knowledge test online for a $10 fee [A02].
This section feeds the knowledge test: the school bus stopping distance and divided-highway exception, the emergency vehicle and move-over rules, the bicycle passing distance and the truck no-zones are traffic-law content for the knowledge test.

Misconceptions: a stopped school bus on the far side of an undivided road still requires you to stop; only a divided highway with a barrier removes that duty; you do not stop inside an intersection for an emergency vehicle; a bicyclist may use the whole lane, so you do not squeeze past inside the same lane; a truck driver cannot see a car tucked close behind the trailer even though the car can see the truck; a motorcycle ahead can stop quicker than you, so following closely is risky.
Mnemonics: lights and sirens mean pull right and stop; move over or slow down for a stopped squad car; red lights on the bus mean stop well back and wait for the flashing to end; three feet for a bike; near a big truck, speed up or drop back.
Glossary: blind spot means an area where another driver cannot see you; no-zone means the large blind area around a big truck; divided highway means a road with a barrier between opposite directions; emergency vehicle means a vehicle responding with emergency lights and sirens; pedestrian means a person on foot.
