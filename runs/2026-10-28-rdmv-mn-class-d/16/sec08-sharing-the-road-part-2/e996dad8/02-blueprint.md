## A — Exam authority for the motorcycles, vulnerable road users, commercial vehicles, railroad crossings, work zones and the zipper merge section (Minnesota Class D written test)
Issuer: the Minnesota Department of Public Safety, whose Driver and Vehicle Services division licenses drivers, permits the printing of the Minnesota Driver’s Manual [A01].
Source document: Minnesota Driver’s Manual, with the edition line June 2026 printed on the front cover [A02].
Written test format: multiple choice and true-or-false questions on Minnesota traffic laws and road signs, taken on paper or on a computer, with English, several other languages and American Sign Language at computer test stations [A03].
Passing standard: a score of 80 percent, and only one written test may be taken per day [A03].
Retest fee: after two consecutive failed written tests, a fee of $10 is charged for the third and every later attempt [A03].
Vision screening: normal or corrected vision of at least 20/40 and peripheral vision of at least 105 degrees [A03].
Question count: the manual does not state how many questions the written test has, so the count stays unknown and is never shown to learners (verify MN-SHAREB-A-COUNT).
Time limit: the manual does not state one, so it stays unknown and is never shown to learners (verify MN-SHAREB-A-TIME).

Misconceptions: motorcycle lane splitting and filtering are legal in Minnesota within set limits, so blocking a rider is wrong; a truck that looks slow may be moving faster than it seems; merging early is not the preferred method when a lane closes in heavy traffic; a stopped train is not the only danger, because a second train may follow on another track.
Mnemonics: split at no more than twenty-five and never more than fifteen over the traffic; trucks cannot see twenty feet in front or two hundred behind; stop ten from the rail and clear ten beyond it; NEVs stay on thirty-five or slower streets.
Glossary: lane splitting means a motorcycle passing within a lane in moving traffic; lane filtering means a motorcycle moving between stopped vehicles; no zone means a blind spot around a commercial vehicle; neighborhood electric vehicle means a small electric vehicle limited to low-speed streets; zipper merge means using both lanes to the merge point and taking turns; Emergency Notification System means the blue sign at a rail crossing with a number for reporting problems.
Dynamic facts: the work zone speeding fine is an amount set by law that can change; recheck it against an official DPS source before each release.
