## A — Exam authority for the speed, passing, right-of-way, intersections, highways and turns section (New Jersey Class D basic license knowledge test)
Issuer: the New Jersey Motor Vehicle Commission (MVC) publishes the New Jersey Driver Manual, which prepares applicants for the knowledge portion of the driver test [A01].
Source document: New Jersey Driver Manual, with 2026 printed on the front cover [A02].
Knowledge test: 50 questions, plus a survey question about organ donation [A03].
Passing standard: 80 percent, which means 40 of the 50 questions must be answered correctly [A04].
Vision screening is required for every motorist, and basic license applicants need 20/50 vision with or without corrective lenses [A04].
Time limit: the manual does not state one, so it stays unknown and is never shown to learners (verify NJ-ROAD-A-TIME).
This section feeds the knowledge test: the statutory speed limits and driving to conditions, double fines, center line passing rules and Safe Corridors, keeping right, yielding, the full stop for pedestrians in crosswalks and its penalties, controlled and uncontrolled intersections and traffic circles, highway entry, exit and weave lanes, right turn on red and left-turn positioning.
Practice quizzes follow the product mastery policy from the learning loop, which is not an official exam threshold.
## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: speed, passing, right-of-way, intersections, highways and turns
Audience: first-time New Jersey Class D applicants and anyone preparing for the rules-of-the-road questions on the knowledge test.
Why it matters to a first-timer: speed limits are among the most tested exact figures [A01], and the crosswalk stop rule carries some of the heaviest penalties for a new driver [A05].
Level: rule recall plus scenario judgment (who yields, where to pass, which lane to turn into); high load because many look-alike limits and yielding rules sit side by side.
Prerequisites: signals, turning and reversing from the previous section.

Misconceptions: a posted limit is not a safe speed in bad conditions; a yield or slowdown is not enough for a pedestrian in a crosswalk, the law demands a full stop; the right shoulder is never a passing lane; a dark traffic signal is not a free pass, it becomes an all-way stop; in a weave lane the entering driver, not the exiting one, yields.
Mnemonics: twenty-five for school, business and homes, thirty-five for the suburbs, fifty for open country, fifty-five for state highways and interstates, sixty-five only where posted; the left yields to the right; broken line on your side means you may pass.
Glossary: acceleration lane means the extra lane at a highway entrance used to reach traffic speed; deceleration lane means the extra lane at an exit used to slow down; weave lane means a single lane used both to enter and to leave an expressway; Safe Corridors means designated state highways where fines are doubled; uncontrolled intersection means one with no signal or regulatory sign.
Dynamic facts: the list of Safe Corridor highways changes as the Commissioner of Transportation designates or removes them, and fines and penalties can change; confirm them on the New Jersey Department of Transportation and MVC websites.
