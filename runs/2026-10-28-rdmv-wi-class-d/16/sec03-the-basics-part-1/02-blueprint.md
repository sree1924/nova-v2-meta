## A — Exam authority for the the basics part one: right-of-way, speed, intersections, stopping and space section (Wisconsin Class D regular driver license knowledge test)
Issuer: the Wisconsin Department of Transportation and its Division of Motor Vehicles (DMV), which offers the Motorists Handbook as a resource that helps train and qualify new drivers [A03].
Source document: Wisconsin Motorists Handbook, with 2026 printed on the front cover [A01]; the back cover carries form number BDS126 with 2026 [A04].
Knowledge test: 50 questions on traffic laws, and 40 correct answers (80 percent) are needed to pass [A02].
Highway signs test: a separate test of 15 questions, with 12 correct answers (80 percent) needed to pass [A02].
Vision screening: regular car and motorcycle applicants need visual acuity of at least 20/40 in one eye, corrected or not, and a 70-degree field of vision from the center of each eye [A02].
Duration: the handbook says the knowledge test takes about 45 minutes to finish, but it states no formal time limit [A02].
Time limit: unknown, so no limit is ever shown to learners (verify WI-BASICS-A-TIME).
Delivery: the signs and knowledge tests need no appointment and are free at a DMV customer service center; applicants aged 15 to 17 can also sit the knowledge test online for a $10 fee [A02].
This section feeds the knowledge test: right-of-way order, default speed limits, the four-second following rule, sight distance and space cushions are core traffic-law content for the knowledge test.
Practice quizzes follow the product mastery policy from the learning loop, which is not an official exam threshold.
## B — Audience, level and prerequisites: the basics part one: right-of-way, speed, intersections, stopping and space
Audience: every first-time Wisconsin Class D applicant, including permit holders practicing with a supervisor who need these habits before the skills test.
Why it matters to a first-timer: the chapter sets the yielding order at intersections and roundabouts [A01], the default speed limits and the four-second rule [A02], and the stopping, sight distance and space habits that prevent crashes [A04].
Level: mixed recall and judgment; several confusable numbers (seconds, miles per hour) and scenario-based yielding rules.
Prerequisites: the driving test requirements and license progression sections; no earlier traffic-law knowledge is assumed.

Misconceptions: the vehicle on the right does not always go first, since arrival order decides at a four-way stop; having the right-of-way does not excuse you from searching left, right and left again; driving slowly is not automatically safe, because it can also cause crashes and tickets; the posted limit is a ceiling, not a target, in poor conditions; a white cane or guide dog user gets the right-of-way even outside the intersection.
Mnemonics: count one thousand one through one thousand four for the gap and add a second per hazard; twenty-five for homes, fifteen for schools with children, thirty-five for the edge of town; look ten to fifteen seconds ahead and check behind every six to eight; left, right, left before you go.
Glossary: right-of-way means the rule for who goes first and who waits; uncontrolled intersection means one with no signs or signals; four-way stop means an intersection where all directions stop; acceleration lane means the on-ramp lane used to reach traffic speed; deceleration lane means the off-ramp lane used to slow down; coasting means rolling in neutral; tailgating means following too closely.
Dynamic facts: posted speed limits vary by road and can change, so the default residential, school zone and outlying limits apply only where no other limit is posted.
