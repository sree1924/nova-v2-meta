Misconceptions: a broken yellow line allows passing but still puts you in the lane of oncoming traffic, so it is not a casual lane change; double solid yellow lines may be crossed only to turn left, never to pass; doubled work-zone fines are tied to posted signs; a curved arrow without the word ONLY does not force a turn when a straight arrow is also painted.
Mnemonics: Yellow divides opposite directions, white divides the same direction; broken means you may cross with care, solid means stay put.
Glossary: mile post means a roadside reference marker on Arizona state highways used to report your location; flagger means a work-zone worker in orange who directs traffic with a flag or a stop and slow panel; flashing arrow panel means a lighted arrow board telling you to merge; regulate means to control traffic by rule.
Dynamic facts: work-zone fine rules are statutory and can be amended, and work-zone layouts, electronic message sign content and portable signs change with each project.
