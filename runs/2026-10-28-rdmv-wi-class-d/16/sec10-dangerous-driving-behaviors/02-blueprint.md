Issuer: the Wisconsin Department of Transportation and its Division of Motor Vehicles (DMV), which offers the Motorists Handbook as a resource that helps train and qualify new drivers [A03].
Source document: Wisconsin Motorists Handbook, with 2026 printed on the front cover [A01]; the back cover carries form number BDS126 with 2026 [A04].
Knowledge test: 50 questions on traffic laws, and 40 correct answers (80 percent) are needed to pass [A02].
Highway signs test: a separate test of 15 questions, with 12 correct answers (80 percent) needed to pass [A02].
Vision screening: regular car and motorcycle applicants need visual acuity of at least 20/40 in one eye, corrected or not, and a 70-degree field of vision from the center of each eye [A02].
Duration: the handbook says the knowledge test takes about 45 minutes to finish, but it states no formal time limit [A02].
Time limit: unknown, so no limit is ever shown to learners (verify WI-BEHAVIOR-A-TIME).
Delivery: the signs and knowledge tests need no appointment and are free at a DMV customer service center; applicants aged 15 to 17 can also sit the knowledge test online for a $10 fee [A02].
This section feeds the knowledge test: the zero alcohol limit for drivers under twenty-one, the controlled substance rule, implied consent and PAC test refusal, and the permit and probationary phone ban are traffic-law content for the knowledge test.

Mnemonics: under twenty-one means zero point zero zero; refuse the test, lose the license; permit and probationary drivers phone only for emergencies; yawn, drift or rumble means pull over and rest.
Glossary: PAC means prohibited alcohol concentration; implied consent means the Wisconsin law requiring you to take a PAC test when an officer asks, with penalties for refusing; controlled substance means a drug regulated by law; breathalyzer means a breath test device that measures alcohol; hands-free means using a phone without holding it; rumble strip means grooves at the road edge that vibrate when driven over.
