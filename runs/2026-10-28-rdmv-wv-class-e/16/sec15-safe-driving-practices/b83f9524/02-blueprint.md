## C — Safe driving practices (part one)

FACT_ID: sec15-F01 | anchor: A01
Keeping the vehicle in proper driving condition is the driver's job, and the common defects that cause crashes are faulty tires, brakes, lights, windshield wipers, defrosters, mufflers and exhaust systems, horns, mirrors and unusable seat belts [A01].

FACT_ID: sec15-F02 | anchor: A01
Make sure you are physically and mentally ready to drive, because the most common driver conditions that affect safe operation are fatigue, alcohol, emotional state, and defective eyesight or hearing [A01].

FACT_ID: sec15-F03 | anchor: A01
Fatigue from lack of sleep or driving too long dulls the mind and slows reaction time, so a tired driver who is alone should pull off the road and rest, and when other licensed drivers are aboard drivers should change as soon as one becomes tired [A01].

FACT_ID: sec15-F04 | anchor: A01
On a long trip take a ten-minute rest break at least every two hours and spend it outside the vehicle, and rolling the window down, turning the radio on or singing can reduce the monotony of driving [A01].

FACT_ID: sec15-F05 | anchor: A01
Emotional states such as anger, worry or fear should be brought under control, and the driver should regain self-control before driving [A01].

FACT_ID: sec15-F06 | anchor: A01
Rush hours bring congested traffic and hurried drivers, usually inbound to metropolitan areas between seven and nine in the morning and outbound between four and six in the afternoon, while rush hours in industrial areas follow the changes of work shifts [A01].

FACT_ID: sec15-F07 | anchor: A01
Rush-hour traffic is heaviest on weekdays, weekends bring drivers making longer trips and driving longer hours, and summer vacation travel and holiday weekends increase traffic volume along with tired, hurried and drunk drivers [A01].

FACT_ID: sec15-F08 | anchor: A01
Rural driving brings slow-moving farm machinery, stray livestock, game animals and motorists at higher speeds, urban driving brings more pedestrians, bicycles, intersections and stop-and-go traffic, and a defensive driver always notes the heavy trucks and buses that complicate passing and slow traffic on hills [A01].

FACT_ID: sec15-F09 | anchor: A02
The vehicle ahead may stop suddenly and without warning, so stay alert, anticipate a sudden stop and keep a safe following distance to avoid a crash [A02].

FACT_ID: sec15-F10 | anchor: A02
You have a responsibility to a tailgating driver behind you to signal whenever you change your driving pattern, because what you do affects that driver, especially if you slow down or stop suddenly [A02].

FACT_ID: sec15-F11 | anchor: A02

FACT_ID: sec15-F12 | anchor: A02

## C — Safe driving practices (part two)

FACT_ID: sec15-F15 | anchor: A03
The ten steps to passing safely are to keep a safe following distance, check oncoming traffic, check the traffic behind, signal before the lane change, move to the left lane, accelerate, warn other drivers with the horn or by flashing the lights, signal the lane change back, merge right when the passed vehicle's headlights appear in your rear view mirror, and maintain a safe legal speed [A03].
Source: [A03 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0077]

FACT_ID: sec15-F16 | anchor: A03
Everyone in a passenger vehicle must wear a federally approved safety belt, the driver is responsible for seeing that all passengers are buckled, the only legal exception is a person eighteen or older riding in the back seat, and a medical exception may be issued with proof of the condition [A03].
Source: [A03 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0077]

FACT_ID: sec15-F17 | anchor: A03
Wearing a safety belt cuts the chance of injury or death in a collision by forty-five to fifty percent because the belt spreads crash forces over the strongest parts of the body and helps occupants ride down the crash, the lap belt fitting snugly over the lower hips and the shoulder belt worn with it for upper-body protection [A03].
Source: [A03 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0077]

FACT_ID: sec15-F18 | anchor: A03
Properly used safety belts keep you from being thrown forward or out of the vehicle and hold you in the proper driving position on bumpy or twisting roads [A03].
Source: [A03 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0077]

FACT_ID: sec15-F19 | anchor: A03
A driver transporting a child under eight in a passenger automobile, van or pickup truck not operated for hire must secure the child in a child passenger safety device that meets federal standards while the vehicle moves on a street or highway, but a child under eight who is at least four feet nine inches tall may be protected by a safety belt alone [A03].
Source: [A03 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0077]

FACT_ID: sec15-F20 | anchor: A04
Head restraints reduce the chance of whiplash injury in rear-end collisions and, when adjustable, should fit against the back of the head rather than the neck [A04].
Source: [A04 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0078]

FACT_ID: sec15-F21 | anchor: A04
The anti-lock brake system prevents wheel lockup during a quick stop so the driver keeps control, and with anti-lock brakes the driver must keep steering while pushing hard on the brake, because pumping anti-lock brakes reduces the system's ability to stop and can lose all brake force [A04].
Source: [A04 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0078]

FACT_ID: sec15-F22 | anchor: A04
It is a misconception that anti-lock brakes stop a vehicle much shorter than regular brakes, since in a sudden stop they improve performance by only about ten percent at best, they are not super brakes that stop on a dime, and drivers should not take extra risks because of them [A04].
Source: [A04 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0078]

FACT_ID: sec15-F23 | anchor: A04
Dual air bags were required on all passenger vehicles beginning in nineteen ninety-eight and on all new light trucks in nineteen ninety-nine to protect the driver and front passenger in a crash, and while they are credited with saving thousands of lives, precautions are needed so they do not cause serious or fatal injury [A04].
Source: [A04 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0078]

FACT_ID: sec15-F24 | anchor: A04
With air bags the driver and every passenger should keep their seat belts secured because most people killed by air bags were unbelted, the driver should hold the wheel from the side with the right hand at four o'clock and the left at eight o'clock so the arms are not between the bag and the body, and both front seats should be tilted slightly back as far from the air bag as possible [A04].
Source: [A04 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0078]

FACT_ID: sec15-F25 | anchor: A04
An infant in a rear-facing safety seat must never ride in the front passenger seat of a vehicle with a passenger-side air bag, and when there is no other way a child there must be in a forward-facing seat, properly secured, with the passenger seat pushed all the way back, while all children twelve and under should ride buckled in the rear seat with younger children in child safety seats [A04].
Source: [A04 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0078]

FACT_ID: sec15-F26 | anchor: A05
Keep the doors locked and the windows up while driving or parked, avoid hitchhikers however innocent or needy they appear, learn your vehicle's controls and safety equipment, and turn off your wireless communication device or enable its hands-free function before driving [A05].
Source: [A05 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0079]

FACT_ID: sec15-F27 | anchor: A05
Lock purses, wallets, luggage and valuables in the trunk or glove compartment, and if you become lost do not study a map on the roadside but drive to a well-lit, populated public place such as a service station or restaurant, locking the vehicle and taking the keys [A05].
Source: [A05 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0079]

FACT_ID: sec15-F28 | anchor: A05
Always park in well-lit areas with your keys in hand as you approach, check inside and underneath the vehicle before entering, and if you feel you are being followed drive to a well-lit populated area and call the police, which in most areas means dialing nine one one [A05].
Source: [A05 -> src:rdmv-wv-class-e:west-virginia-division-of-motor-vehicles:handbook:pdf-0079]
